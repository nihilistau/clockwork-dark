# The Clockwork Dark

## Nature of the Wound
The Clockwork Dark is not a demon with a name you can banish. It is a metastasizing logic: gears in wheat, brass filaments in nerves, militias marching in perfect time until they march into the sea. It stains the world inward from the Heartlands. It does not want worship and it has no use for the dead. It wants the world to run on time.

## It Is a Pattern, Not a Person
Nothing has been seen. Nothing has spoken. What people describe when they finally describe it is an arrangement — rows too straight, tallies too round, a week that comes out even. It converts living order into ticking mechanism, and living order is everywhere, which is why there is no front line and no siege. The wheat was already a pattern. It only needed correcting.

## Signs at the Margin
At Edgewood's edge the signs are small: stillborn lambs with brass teeth, bread that rings when it cracks, clocks that tick hours that never were, a road that measures shorter than it did. Travellers who ignore these signs may live quiet lives and die of ordinary things. Those who listen may not stay quiet long.

## The Nine-Day Count
It started on the ninth of the wheat month. It has taken exactly nine days to cross each ring since, and it does not vary. Anyone can count it. Almost nobody does, because counting it is the point at which it stops being weather and starts being an appointment.

## What It Takes First
Every household it has taken had one thing in common: something in it kept time. A clock, a loom, a mill wheel, a woman who hummed the same four bars at the same hour every morning. The advice that circulates is to break your clock. The advice is not wrong. It is also not enough, and the people giving it know that.

## Why Nobody Stops It
Stopping a pattern requires naming it, and naming is the costly art. There is no army to meet and no gate to hold. The Marches have raised levies against it and the levies march very well now. The one thing that has ever measurably slowed it was done by a painter who is under a barrow, and she did not finish.
