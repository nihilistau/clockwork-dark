# Millhaven

## The Town
Four hours up the road from Edgewood: a walled market town of two thousand, a garrison, a grain exchange, and a gate that closes an hour earlier every week without anyone having issued an order to that effect. Millhaven is the largest thing most Edgewood villagers will ever see, and the furthest inward most of them will ever go.

## The Gate
Timber, iron-shod, and manned. There is a recruitment board with fresh nails in it, which means somebody up there expects volunteers. The queue to enter is counted, and then counted again by a second man who does not compare his number with the first. Nobody has explained the second count.

## The Market
Chalk boards, weights, and the quarterly tally coming out perfect. Stalls sell what the Marches make and what the Heartlands used to. Prices have not risen, which is stranger than if they had — the roads inward are shut, the refugees are outside the wall, and the price of grain in Millhaven has not moved a copper since spring.

## The Barracks
Sergeant Sera Venn's yard. Drill at the appointed hour, muster at the appointed hour, and half the watch coming off the wall in a step nobody called. Venn noticed it first and has told exactly one person. She is holding orders dated before the corruption ever reached Millhaven, telling her exactly where to stand when it did, and she has not asked who signed them because she has read the signature.

## Streets That Are Not There
The tanner's boy from Edgewood walked to Millhaven, spent a day, came back, and named four streets that do not exist. He is not lying and he is not simple; he can draw them. Two other people have since described the same four streets, in the same order, without having met him.

## Living With It
Millhaven's people are not stupid and are not asleep. They have simply calculated that the town is safer than the road, which is true, and that noticing is more dangerous than not noticing, which is also true, and they are correct on both counts right up until they are not.
