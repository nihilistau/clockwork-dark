# Wards and Charms

## What People Carry
A hare's foot on red thread. A bent iron nail turned over in the doorframe. A wax saint with the face left smooth. A card of brass pins, nine to the card, worn through the collar point-down where the wearer cannot see it. None of these are magic in the sense that sympathy is magic. Most of them are habit with a good reason underneath it that nobody remembers.

## The Ones That Work
Iron does something. This is not folklore; the effect is small, consistent and reported identically across four hundred miles by people who have never met. Cold-worked iron in a threshold, a nail turned over, a knife under a pillow. It does not stop anything. It appears to make a house harder to include, which is a different verb and the right one.

## The Ones That Reassure
Everything else, probably. Tinkers sell reassurance without embarrassment and without pretending otherwise if asked directly. A frightened household that has done something sleeps better than a frightened household that has not, and sleep is worth actual copper in a bad season. This is a defensible trade and the tinkers defend it.

## Sympathy Charms Are Not Wards
A ward keeps something out. A sympathy charm binds two things together, which is the opposite operation, and it takes from the wearer for as long as it holds. People buy them for protection and they are not protection. Ilya says so at the point of sale, quietly, once, and does not repeat herself, and it is not clear whether that is honesty or a test.

## The Nine Count
Ward pins come nine to a card everywhere they are sold, from the Marches to the far side of Millhaven, by families who do not coordinate prices on anything else. Nine is the count that holds. Ask for a tenth and the price stops being a number. No tinker will explain it and none of them appear to enjoy having the rule.

## Breaking Your Clock
The most-repeated advice in the Marches this season, and the only piece of it that is genuinely new: every household the pattern has taken had something in it that kept time. So break your clock, stop your loom at night, vary the hour you rise. It is not enough. It is measurably better than nothing, which puts it ahead of everything else on this list.
