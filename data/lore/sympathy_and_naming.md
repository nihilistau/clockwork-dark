# Sympathy and Naming

## The Costly Art
There are no fireballs. What exists is sympathy: binding like to like, so that what is done to one is felt by the other. A thread of hair, a thread of coat, wax over both. It works. It works reliably enough that tinkers can sell it with a straight face and a warranty. It takes the binder's own strength to hold, every hour it holds, and the binder does not get to choose when to stop paying.

## What It Costs
Focus, first — the practitioner's ability to hold two things as one thing at once, which is exhausting in a way that has no ordinary equivalent. Then material: wax, hair, cloth, something of both parties. Then, for the deeper bindings, blood or memory. A binder who has done too much sits down afterwards and does not remember deciding to.

## Naming Is the Harder Half
Sympathy binds; naming defines. To name a thing correctly is to say what it actually is rather than what it is called, and correct naming has effects that shouting a spell name does not. It is also the only known way to address a pattern rather than an object, which is the entire reason anyone still studies it.

## The Price of a True Name
Every account of a true naming ends the same way: the namer paid. Not always their life. Sometimes their own name, which is worse in ways that are hard to explain to anyone who has not watched it happen — the person is still there, still working, and the village cannot hold on to who they are from one week to the next.

## Why Nobody Teaches It
There is no school. There are tinker families with a tradition, a handful of Marches scholars with fragments, and the unnamed practice, which teaches the opposite discipline: how not to name. These three groups do not talk to each other and each thinks the other two are the reason things are as bad as they are.

## Sympathy Sickness
Overwork it and the binding starts running the wrong way. The practitioner begins feeling what they bound rather than the reverse — weather on skin that is not theirs, hunger they did not earn. Refugees from the Heartlands describe whole villages of it. There is no treatment beyond cutting the binding, and cutting a binding you can no longer locate is not a procedure, it is a hope.
