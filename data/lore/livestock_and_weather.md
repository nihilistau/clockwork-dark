# Livestock and Weather

## Animals Notice First
Dogs in Well Row have stopped barking at night. Nobody agrees whether that is a mercy. Cats leave a house four or five days before anything else is visibly wrong with it — Brindle has moved twice this season and both houses have since had reasons. Horses will not take the last stretch of the Millhaven road after dusk and no carter argues with a horse about that any more.

## The Stillborn Lambs
A lamb turned up dead in the Fallow east pen with brass filings where its teeth should have been. There have been four more between here and Millhaven, all in ewes that grazed strips which took the wrong rain, all born at term, all otherwise perfect. The filings are not in the gum. They are in the tooth, in the position of enamel, correctly shaped.

## Milk and Souring
Somebody's milk soured in the pail before it ever left the goat. This is impossible in the ordinary way and the ordinary explanations — dirty pail, sick animal, hot day — were all checked by people who know how to check them. It has happened three times in Edgewood since midsummer, in three different households, on three different animals, all at the same hour of the morning.

## The Lost Goat
Goats are found. That is the whole of the folk wisdom about goats: they go, they are noisy, they are found. One did not come back and was eventually found in a place it could not have got to, with the bell still on the strap and the clapper still in the bell, and no sound had been heard by anybody in a village of ninety.

## Weather Losing Its Variance
Even rain. A wind holding one bearing for nine days. Frost at the same hour on consecutive mornings. Nothing here is destructive and every part of it is wrong in the same specific direction — a natural process losing its noise. Farmers are the best-placed people alive to notice this and the worst-placed to say so, because a farmer who talks about the weather being too regular sounds like a farmer.

## Keeping Animals Alive
Bring them in at night. Do not graze a strip that took the wrong rain, whatever the fodder situation is. Vary the milking hour — the same advice as breaking your clock and for the same reason. Iron on the byre door. None of it is proven. All of it costs almost nothing, which is the actual argument for doing it.
