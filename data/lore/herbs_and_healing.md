# Herbs and Healing

## No Physicians Here
Edgewood has no physician and Millhaven's is expensive and mostly a barber. What the frontier has is household knowledge: three or four women per village who know eleven plants properly, can set a bone, and know the difference between a wound that needs cleaning and one that needs closing. It is not primitive. It is a curriculum, taught by watching, and it works about as well as anything works.

## Yarrow
Feathered leaf, cut in flower, packed into a wound. It slows bleeding. This is not belief — it does it, it does it reliably, and the frontier discovered it several hundred years before anybody wrote it down. Dried, powdered, worked into boiled linen and rolled, it becomes a bandage that can sit in a pack for a season.

## Feverfew and Bittercress
Feverfew for headache and for whatever the headache is a sign of; bitter enough that nobody takes it recreationally, which is part of its usefulness. Bittercress in quantity is the base of the green draught that brings a fever down. The draught does not heal anything. It buys the body a night, and a night is often the whole of what is needed.

## The Glen
A south-facing bowl in the deep forest where all three come up thicker than they should, and come up all year, which they should not. There are faint rows if you stand at the top and let your eyes go slack. Somebody planted it, a long time ago, and it has not gone over in the absence of anyone tending it — which is the part that should worry a herbalist more than it does.

## What Cannot Be Treated
Sympathy sickness. Wounds that will not close because the person is bound to something that is still being hurt. A household where everyone has begun rising at the same minute. None of this is in the curriculum, none of the eleven plants touch it, and the honest herb-women say so, which is why the frightened go to the tinkers instead.

A woman who can set a collarbone in a lamplit kitchen and have the arm working by spring is doing something the world cannot presently do any other way. It is not a lesser magic. It is not magic. It is simply what people had, and it kept most of them alive.
