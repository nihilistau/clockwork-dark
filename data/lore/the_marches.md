# The Marches

## What the Marches Are
Market towns, militia garrisons, toll roads, and the administrative habit of counting things. The Marches were the buffer between the Heartland kingdoms and the frontier villages, and their whole competence was logistics: grain moved, levies raised, roads kept. That competence is now the problem. A region organised to run on time is a region already half-converted.

## The Toll Roads
Three spans of old stone and a chain, and a clerk at a table who writes down more about you than the toll requires: name, origin, trade, destination, and how many in the party. The toll is four copper. The writing is free, and the ledgers go somewhere, and nobody at the bridge can tell you where.

## Grain and Tally
The Marches tally grain every quarter. Millhaven's tally has come out perfect four quarters running — not close, perfect. No harvest is perfect. A clerk who queried it was moved to a different desk, which is a Marches answer to everything.

## Refugees
The ditch camp outside Millhaven's wall is canvas and cart-boards, and it is there because the wall is closed and the road inward is worse. The refugees are Heartlanders, mostly. They talk about crops rotting in patterns and clocks running backward and men who walk with tick-tick breath, and the Marches have decided this is hysteria, because the alternative is a decision.

## The Burned Farmstead
Sills standing, roof gone, a fire that took the house and left the byre. It was two hours off the toll road and it was not an accident, and the militia report on it exists and says nothing. Whoever burned it did so in a way that suggests they were putting something out rather than starting something.

## Why It Matters to Edgewood
Everything Edgewood cannot make comes through the Marches: salt, iron stock, cloth, news. The road to Millhaven is four hours and it is the only artery the village has. When the Marches close — and they are closing an hour earlier each week — Edgewood does not become independent. It becomes alone.
