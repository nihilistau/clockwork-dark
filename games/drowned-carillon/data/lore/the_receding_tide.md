# The Receding Tide

## What the Marks on the Post Mean
Four marks are cut into the notice-board post at Bellfounders' Quay: low water, spring tide, flood mark, full chime. Everyone will tell you they are a tide gauge. The bottom two are. The top two have never been water.

## Low Water
The ordinary state, and the only one most people on this coast will ever knowingly live through. The bar is walkable six hours a day, the boats go out, and the note under the sea-noise is there for anyone who stops working long enough to hear it, which is nobody.

## Spring Tide
Brass stops warming in the hand. The set-nets begin to come up sorted. Somebody's child hums four notes for a fortnight and cannot say where she got them. Nothing on this coast has yet happened that a bad season could not explain, and the explanations are getting more work than they used to.

## Flood Mark
There is a fisher on the Wet Steps working a line that is not there. The count of the missing and the order the choir stands in turn out to be the same list. At this mark the coast stops being able to pretend, and what it mostly does with that is stop talking.

## Full Chime
The organ does not need the tide any more. This is the mark nobody has seen and everybody has described, and every description is the same one: the whole coast in tune, and quiet, and standing.

## The Quiet Life Is Not a Failure
A net-mender who mends nets, carries oil, sells brass to Halloran and never once goes below the ninth step has lived a whole life on this coast and finished it. Nothing here punishes that. The tide does what it does regardless, and always has.
