# Bellfounders' Quay

## The Last Dry Granite
A working harbour of cut granite on a coast that has very little of it left: net lofts on piles over the water, a chowder pot that has been on since before anyone now living was born, a casting yard that gave the quay its name, and four lamps that still have glass in them.

## The Shore-Bell
A great bell in a timber frame at the quay head, cast here, hung here, and rung exactly once. The rope is still on it and the knots are checked by hand every morning. Visitors ask why nobody rings it. Quayfolk answer with the price of fish.

## The Notice Post
Tarred, black, and thick with layers of paper that will not come off. The tide table is nailed to it, along with whatever work is going and one standing bounty in a hand nobody will identify.

## The Wet Steps
Forty treads going down under the quay to the water. The green starts at the ninth. Nobody will tell you why the ninth, and everybody counts.

## How the Quay Floods
Not from the seaward side. It comes up the steps, through the drains, and out of the well, which is how a coast that has watched the sea its whole life keeps being surprised by it.
