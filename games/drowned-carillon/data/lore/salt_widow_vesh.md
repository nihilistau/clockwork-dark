# Salt-Widow Vesh

## The Net-Mender
Three strands at a time, tar to the second knuckle, back to the sea. She runs the lofts, sets the bar nets, and is the reason four boats still go out. She is blunt to the point of unkindness and has never once been wrong about weather.

## Three Tides
A husband and two brothers, on three separate tides, none of them the same year. The quay counts nineteen gone in eleven years and she can name all nineteen in order, which is a thing she does out loud when she has had a drink and a thing nobody has yet connected to the order the choir stands in.

## Why She Hears It Clearest
Grief tuned her. What the Carillon takes is the mourned, and what it leaves in exchange is an ear for itself. She is the best warning bell on this coast and the most endangered soul on it, and she knows both halves of that.

## What She Can Do That Nobody Else Can
She can look at a tuned thing standing in the water and tell you who it used to be. Sometimes she can tell you whether it can be calmed rather than broken. She does not enjoy either.

## The Ninth Step
At the turn of the tide she goes down and stands there with the water at her knees, listening. She is not grieving. People who have gone down after her say she is answering.
