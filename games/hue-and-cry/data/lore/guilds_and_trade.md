# Guilds and Trade

## The Worshipful Company of Chandlers
The guild of the Rise fixes the price of tallow, stamps the pure beeswax, sets the standard wick, and has opinions about wick-length that have ended friendships. Its halls on Chandlers' Rise are the richest rooms in the city after the palace. A chandler who is expelled from the guild cannot sell a candle in Tallowmere, and the guild publishes its expulsions slowly, when it publishes them at all.

## Tallow and Beeswax
Tallow is rendered fat: cheap, smoky, and what the Snuffs burns. Beeswax is sweet, bright and dear, and what Silk Row and the Temple burn. The difference in price is the difference between a living and a fortune, and a great deal of Tallowmere's quiet dishonesty lives in the gap: "pure beeswax" that is three parts tallow, "blessed" tapers no priest has touched, and a guild stamp that can be bought.

## Marsh & Daughters
Tobiah Marsh dips candles on the Rise in the old way, forty dips to the frame, and takes on day-dippers in the morning when the wax is hot. He complains about tallow prices to anyone within earshot and pays fairly for straight candles.

## Wickmarket
The market at the city's heart: pie-men, fishwives, a knife-grinder, stalls that sell everything a candle-port needs and several things it does not. Two shops there buy what you should not be selling. Pell Hollis keeps Hollis's Sundries and weighs a stranger as carefully as their brooch. The stallholders pay coppers for errands and talk to each other about everybody.

## The Snuffs' Trade
Marrow runs a rag-and-bone yard in the Snuffs and melts things down, and nothing that goes into Marrow's pot comes out looking like what went in. Old Nance lets pallets by the night. The Porters' Hall hands out carrying work by day and other work by night.

## The Quay
Dock Mag makes up her porters' gangs from first light until noon and shouts them on and off the barges; a day's carrying pays two crowns and a heel of bread. The customs house counts the tallow-boats in and the candle-cases out, and is said to be the most honest building in the city, which the customs men find insulting.
