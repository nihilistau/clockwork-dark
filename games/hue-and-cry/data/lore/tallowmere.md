# Tallowmere

## The Candle-Port
Tallowmere makes candles for the whole coast, and has done for three hundred years. The tallow-boats come up the estuary heavy with rendered fat, the beeswax comes down from the hill farms in wicker, and everything leaves again as light: dipped tapers by the gross, moulded church candles, the thick yellow dips the poor burn a finger at a time. The whole city smells faintly of wax, tallow or smoke, depending on the wind, and a visitor stops noticing by the second day.

## A City That Is Always Lit
Nobody in Tallowmere is ever quite in the dark. There are lamps on iron brackets at every corner, tapers in the windows, lanterns on poles carried by the Watch, and a lamplighter's round that starts in Wickmarket at dusk and does not finish until the Green. Tallowmerians are proud of it. They say a city that is always lit is a city with nothing to hide, and they say it most loudly in the streets where that is least true.

## The Districts
**Tallow Docks** is wet boards, tarred rope, the customs house and Dock Mag shouting porters on and off the barges. **Wickmarket** is stalls, pies, a knife-grinder, and two shops that buy what you should not be selling. **The Snuffs** is where the poor burn candle-ends and the Porters' Hall keeps its union charter in the window. **Silk Row** is glass lamps and money. **Chandlers' Rise** holds the guild halls and the Temple of the Everflame. **The Lantern House** is the Watch, its sergeants' desk and its cells. **Margrave's Hill** is the palace and the flame. **Gallows Green** is grass, ballad-sellers, and the gallows in the middle of it.

## Money and Measures
Tallowmere counts in crowns and pennies, weighs silver on the stallholders' brass scales, and trusts nobody's scales but its own. A day's honest carrying on the quay pays two crowns and a heel of bread. A bed at Old Nance's in the Snuffs is a crown a night, a tavern room three. Most people in the city are one bad week from sleeping in a doorway, and most of them know somebody who has.

## Tallowmere Manners
It is rude to snuff someone else's candle, ruder to blow it out, and very bad luck to light a taper from a lamp on the Green. A porter who carries for you is owed a nod. A Lantern who swings his lamp into your face is doing his job, and it is wiser to let him.
