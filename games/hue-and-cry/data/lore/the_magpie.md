# The Magpie

## The Legend
For two years a thief called the Magpie has been robbing Tallowmere, and has never once been seen. The Magpie only ever steals things that shine: silver, jewels, gilt, a bishop's mitre once, the brass knocker off the Lantern House door. Left behind, as often as not, is a single black feather on a windowsill, which the ballad-sellers on the Green have made a great deal of.

## What the City Says
Everybody in Tallowmere has an opinion about the Magpie. The stallholders say it is a gentleman, because nothing plain is ever taken. The porters say it is one of the Company gone private, because the jobs are too clean. The Watch says it is a professional who came in off a barge, and has been saying so, with a drawing, ever since the morning a Lantern shouted "the Magpie!" at a stranger on the quay. The ballads say the Magpie is a lady, a ghost and a jackdaw by turns, depending on which verse sells.

## What Is Actually Known
Very little. The Magpie works at night, prefers houses with something bright on show, goes over roofs as easily as through doors, and has never been caught with anything, because nothing the Magpie takes has ever turned up at a fence in Tallowmere. The Watch keeps a list of what was taken and where; the list is long, and Captain Ardane knows every line of it by heart.

## The Stranger Off the Barge
Since the morning on the quay, the Watch has had a face to put to the name, and the Magpie has had someone else to take the blame. Every robbery now lands on the stranger's head in the Watch's books. Whether the real Magpie finds this funny, useful or insulting is something only the Magpie knows.

## The Truth of It
<!-- scope: #gm_secrets -->
The real Magpie is someone who lives in Tallowmere, moves freely in its streets by night, and has a reason to want the city's shining things -- not a stranger at all. Which of the city's people it is has not been settled by this corpus and must never be stated from it: the narrator is told who the Magpie is only when the player has earned it, and until then every one of the city's people is as likely as any other.
