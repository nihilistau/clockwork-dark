# The Militia

## Levies, Not Soldiers
The Marches militia is a levy system: each town holds a roll, each roll is called when the reeve says, and the men who answer are farmers with two weeks' drill. It works against bandits and has never once been tested against anything else. It is now being expanded, and the expansion is being run by people who understand rolls and do not understand what they are raising a roll against.

## Sergeant Sera Venn
Millhaven's drill sergeant. Competent, unsentimental, and doing arithmetic with people because somebody has to and the alternative is a clerk doing it. She fills the levy from villages that will notice least, which is a monstrous policy she chose deliberately over three worse ones. She is not the villain of this and would not accept being called anything else.

## The Conscription List
Ninety-one names. Eleven of them are from Edgewood, a village that has never been levied because it is too small and too far out. The list was drawn up before the quota that justifies it. Anyone who copies it down and compares the dates finds out something about who is actually issuing orders in the Marches.

## The Press
A press gang is legal in the Marches during a declared emergency, and an emergency has been declared for reasons that are not in writing. In practice it is four men, a wagon and a notice read aloud twice, in case the first time was ignored. It takes who it can catch, which means it takes the poor, which means the ditch camp.

## Marching in Step
Half the Millhaven watch came off the wall in a step nobody called. This is the sign the militia cannot explain away, because a militia knows exactly how hard it is to get farmers to march in step at all, and knows that it never happens by accident, and knows that nobody called it.

## Orders From Before
Venn is holding written orders dated before the corruption reached Millhaven, telling her exactly where to stand when it did. She has not burned them. She has not obeyed them either. She keeps them because they are the only physical evidence that any of this was arranged, and because she has read the signature and has not yet decided what to do about having read it.
