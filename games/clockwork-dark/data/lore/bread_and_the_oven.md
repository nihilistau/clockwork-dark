# Bread and the Communal Oven

## One Fire for the Village
Edgewood bakes in one place. The communal stone oven in the square takes a day to come up to heat and holds it for two, so the village bakes in rotation and the rotation is the single most important schedule in anybody's life. Fuel is shared, heat is shared, and the order of the rotation encodes forty years of who did what for whom.

## Why the Baker Matters
Maris does not own the oven; she owns the knowledge of it — which stone runs hot, how long after the coals are raked the heat is right for bread and how much longer until it is right for pastry, and how to read a loaf's bottom crust to know what the fire did while you were not watching. Take away the baker and the village still has an oven and no longer has bread.

## The Rotation
Dawn is Maris's. Mid-morning goes to whoever has flour. Afternoon is pies and roots and whatever the heat is still good for. Evening the fire is banked and the sour is set to sulk overnight. The night before a festival, nobody sleeps, and the oven is held at temperature by people taking two-hour turns, and this is considered a pleasure rather than a chore.

## Bread That Rings
A loaf should sound hollow when you rap the base. A loaf made from wheat that took the wrong rain rings — a clear, sustained, tuned note, like a struck bell, and it rings from the crack rather than the knuckle. Maris burned a batch that did this, put the whole tray in the midden, and would not discuss it. She has bought no grain from the east strips since.

## Brass in the Grain
The other sign, and the worse one: bright flecks in the flour that are not chaff and do not burn off. Bakers find it first because bakers put their hands in more grain than anyone. The advice that has circulated as far as Millhaven is to avoid bread with brass flecks, which is not useful advice, because by the time it is in the flour it is already in the field.

## Bread as the Measure
When a village stops baking together, it has stopped being a village. That is not sentiment; it is the mechanism. The rotation is the thing that forces ninety people to speak to each other every single day about something practical. Everything Edgewood knows about itself, it knows because of the queue at the oven.
