# Forest Margin

## The Clearing
The forest clearing where travellers wake is generous but not tame. Birch and fern, mushroom circles, game trails that double back when watched. Smoke from Edgewood drifts west even when the wind blows south — a fact children repeat like a rhyme and adults have stopped repeating at all.

## Forage and Watchers
Herbs, resin, and clay hide in the undergrowth. Things watch from stillness without moving. Hidden paths lead to barrows older than the village, best left unopened until nerve and need align. A careful forager can feed themselves off the margin indefinitely; a careless one gets exactly one warning.

## The Deeper Wood
Past the last snare line the trees stop being a wood and start being a distance. Nobody from Edgewood has a reason to go further than the snares, so nobody has, so nobody knows what the distance is. The charcoal burners go furthest, and the burners are not from anywhere.

## The Herb Glen
A south-facing bowl where feverfew, yarrow and bittercress come up thicker than they have any right to, and come up all year, which they should not. Somebody weeded it once. The rows are still faintly visible if you stand at the top and let your eyes go slack. Nobody weeds it now and it has not gone over.

## The Charcoal Burn
Turf-capped stacks and a hut. Burners come in season, sleep rough, sell to the forge and to Millhaven, and leave. The village trades with them and does not learn their names, which is an old arrangement neither side minds. A burner watches a stack for six days without sleeping properly, which is why burners notice things at night that other people do not.

## Rules of the Margin
Do not eat from a ring you did not walk into from the outside. Do not follow a trail that is easier than it was yesterday. If the smoke lies about the wind, trust the smoke and go the other way. None of these are laws of nature. They are what is left after two hundred years of people who ignored them not coming back.
