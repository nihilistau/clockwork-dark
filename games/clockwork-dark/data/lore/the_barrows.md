# The Old Barrows

## Nine Mounds
An hour beyond the deeper wood: nine grass-covered mounds in a line that is too straight to be weather. They predate Edgewood by an unknown margin — the village has been there four hundred years and arrived to find them already old. Nothing grows on the ninth one but grass, and grass grows on it very well.

## What the Village Does About Them
Does not come here, and does not discuss not coming here. Children are told the mounds are graves, which is true, and that graves are private, which is a lie of omission doing a great deal of work. There is no prohibition written down anywhere, and there does not need to be, because everyone in three generations has absorbed it before they could ask.

## The Barrow Mouth
The ninth mound has a dressed-stone throat in its side. Open — not opened, open, with no spoil heap and no tool marks and lintels set by people who knew exactly what they were doing. It is three degrees colder three paces in than the season allows, and the cold does not increase with depth, which is the wrong way round for a hole in the ground.

## Grave Goods and the Absence of Them
Barrows of this age elsewhere in the Marches are stuffed: pots, beads, iron, a horse. This one is swept. Whatever was interred here was interred with nothing, in a chamber built to a standard nobody within a hundred miles could match, which is not how you bury a chieftain. It is how you seal a room.

## The Painter's Burial
She is in there, sixty years dead, and hers is the only interment anyone can date. The village put a painter in a chamber they will not walk past. Nobody has ever given a reason, and the two families who might know have a policy about it that is indistinguishable from the unnamed practice: do not name it, do not write it down, keep the ordinary hours.

## Going In
It can be done. People have. What is agreed by the ones who came back is that the chamber is dry, that the dressed stone continues further than the mound is wide, and that the count of the steps down is not the same on the way out. Nobody agrees on which direction the discrepancy runs, which is itself worth noticing.
