# Edgewood Village

## The Margin Settlement
Edgewood is the last comfortable village before the deep woods give way to the Marches. Timber frames lean together around a communal stone oven. Traders pass through twice a season, and the shrine to unnamed saints never lacks a candle — though nobody can name the saints. Roughly ninety people, forty of them children, and a headman who is really just the man with the largest table.

## Maris Hearth and the Bakery
Maris Hearth runs the Hearth Bakery with flour on her sleeves and a hum in her throat. Villagers say she hums to keep the gears quiet. She buys wild mushrooms from travellers and sells loaves that taste of honest hunger, not prophecy. She sleeps on a cot above the ovens, still dressed, because the fire has to be fed at three.

## Well Row
The lower street: twelve houses, a shared well, and the dogs that have lately stopped barking at night. Nobody in Well Row agrees whether that is a mercy. The well has tasted of iron since midsummer and nothing has been dropped in it. People still draw from it. There is nowhere else.

## Hallam's Forge
Hallam mends hinges, blades, cart-iron, pot-legs and nothing with a spring in it. He will tell you why if you have an hour, and the story takes an hour whether or not you have one. Since spring he has refused clock work outright, which in a village with one clock is less a policy than a superstition with a leather apron on.

## The Fallow Holding
East of the square, past the pens. The Fallows have farmed it four generations and do not invite people out any more. The wheat on their far strip stands in rows too straight for any wind to have made, and a stillborn lamb turned up in the east pen with brass filings where its teeth should have been. The Fallows have not said so out loud. Everyone knows.

## What Edgewood Does About It
Nothing, carefully. The village has a working method for things it cannot fix: it does not name them, it does not write them down, and it keeps the ordinary work going at ordinary hours. This looks like cowardice and is closer to a kind of engineering. It has held for two seasons. It will not hold for four.
