# The Nine Count

## The Number Keeps Turning Up
Nine mounds in the barrow line. Nine pins to a card. Nine panels on the shrine wall. Nine saints in the Marches fragment. Nine days to cross each ring. It started on the ninth of the wheat month. Individually each of these has a mundane explanation and there is a person ready to give it to you. Together they are the single most-noticed fact in the Marches and the least-discussed.

## Counting It Yourself
Anyone can. Take a district the pattern has reached, take the date the first sign was reported, take the date the next ring inward reported its first sign, and the interval is nine days. It does not vary. It has not varied for four rings. This is the piece of information that turns a superstition into an appointment, which is exactly why nobody wants it counted out loud.

## The Children's Game
The children have a new counting game and not one mother will admit to teaching it. It counts to nine, resets, and the reset is the interesting part — the rhyme does not end at nine, it starts again mid-word, so a group of children playing it produces a continuous count with no seam in it. Asked where they got it, they say from other children, which is what children always say and is, this time, not sufficient.

## Why Nine and Not Ten
The one substantive answer anyone gives — a tinker, drunk, and she denied it the next day — is that nine is what a count can be before it becomes a cycle. Ten closes. Nine holds open. A card of nine pins is a count that has not finished, and a thing that is not finished cannot be completed by something else.

## Drawings in the Dirt
Children in Edgewood square drew gears in the dirt, counted out exactly, with the right number of teeth for a wheel none of them has ever seen. By morning the drawings were gone and the dirt was smooth — not scuffed, smooth, the way a floor is smooth. Nessa Fallow drew the same wheel on the back of a flour wrapper and kept it, which is why there is any evidence at all.

## The Tenth
Every tradition that uses the count has a prohibition on the tenth: the tenth pin, the tenth day, the ninth panel finished. Read charitably it is a warning against completing a pattern. Read uncharitably it is a warning against completing THE pattern, in which case the shrine wall is not a devotional object at all and the prohibition on touching it is the most load-bearing rule in the village.
