# The Heartlands

## What Was There
The seat of kingdoms: cities with water brought in on arches, libraries, a coinage everyone accepted, and roads good enough that the Marches were only ever a frontier because the Heartlands decided where the frontier was. Nobody in Edgewood has been. Two people alive in the village have met somebody who had.

## What Refugees Say
That crops rot in patterns. That clocks run backward and then, worse, run correctly again. That there are men who walk with tick-tick breath and are otherwise entirely themselves — they know their families, they do their work, they are not hostile, and they cannot be persuaded to stop for anything, ever, including sleep.

## The Letters
Odran's brother drove the Heartlands run in spring and did not come back. He writes. The letters arrive on the same day of the week, every week, in a hand that never varies — not similar, identical, letter for letter, as though traced. The content is ordinary. He asks after the family. He mentions the weather. Odran has stopped opening them and has not stopped keeping them.

## The Wound
Somewhere inward there is a place the pattern is coming out of, and the refugee accounts converge on it more than they converge on anything else: a district where nothing is wrong at all. No ruin, no corpses, no fire. Everything works. The mills turn, the fields yield, the people are polite, and the whole of it runs on a schedule that nobody set and nobody can now stop keeping.

## Why the Road Inward Is Empty
It is not blocked. There are no barricades and no hostile force. The road inward is empty because the traffic on it has become entirely one-directional, and has been for a season, and the last carter to try it turned around at the second milestone past the toll bridge for a reason he could not afterwards articulate to anybody's satisfaction, including his own.

## The Milestones
Past the toll bridge the metalling changes and the milestones start agreeing with each other too exactly — every one at its precise interval, every one cut to the same depth, none weathered differently from any other despite being different ages. It is the last thing travellers report before they report nothing.
