# The SynthSec Grid Point

## The Wire
A militarized checkpoint between the city and the dark: mast, gate, barracks corridor, and a fence line that is maintained with a diligence nothing else out here receives. Order at gunpoint, billed by the hour — SynthSec is a contractor, and the contract is with somebody who pays on time and is never named on the invoice.

## The Mast
It reads faces, not badges. This is the single most useful fact a runner can hold about the Grid Point, and Dane gives it away free to people he drinks near, which tells you what fourteen months on this gate have done to him. Papers can be perfect; the face wearing them is what the mast files. Head down, no eye contact. It cannot read what it cannot model, and looking away is a smaller data point than looking at.

## The Three Doors
The gate passes nobody the city is hunting — above that line there is no price, because the mast files the refusal automatically and Dane cannot unfile it. Below the line: papers that spend, or four hundred and four seconds of the corporal looking at the ground, or the Colosseum undercroft out of Ripper Street — three hours of service dark that delivers you outside the wire with the walk still to do.

## The Trucks
They go out full and come back full. Nobody unloads them at the gate. Dane has logged them for fourteen months without once being told what the cargo is, and the not-being-told has a shape to it — a deliberate, budgeted shape — that a man who stands still for a living has had a great deal of time to study.

## What the Trucks Carry
Storage drives. The city's day, racked and cycled: hauled up full of feed-take, hauled back scrubbed for refill. The Grid Point is a valve on a circulatory system between Intake and the hall below the Shadow zone, and Dane's six years of guarding have been six years of standing on the vein. A player who brings him the manifest hands him the answer SynthSec never would — and a man with nothing left to guard is a door.
