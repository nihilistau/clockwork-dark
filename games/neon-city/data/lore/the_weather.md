# The Weather

## The Wheel
The sky over the Sprawl runs a rotation everyone knows and nobody schedules around properly: clear, overcast, rain, acid rain, storm, and back to clear. The forecast feeds call it to the minute inside the city, which is not the same as being useful — knowing the acid arrives at 15:40 does not make the awning yours.

## Acid Rain
Fat drops that hiss on the hoardings. It bites skin, eats gear seams, and empties a street with practised speed. The exposed grounds — the yard, the Plaza approach, everything past the wire — are where it collects its real invoices: an hour outdoors in the acid costs condition, equipment, or both. A rain shell and a filter mask are not comfort purchases. They are the toll.

## Storm
The one hard "wait" the world imposes. A storm closes the Shadow crossing outright: not dangerous-but-passable, closed, in the way a door is closed. Expeditions budget for it or are taught to. The timestamp does not budget for anything, which is the whole tension of the last week of a crossing.

## Planning in Bars
Indoor waypoints — Noir, the Pit, the Anchor, the Bunker — do not care about the sky, which is why expeditions are planned in them. The Anchor's window table has watched more route arguments than any map in the city, and Ines could referee them from memory by now, and does not, because the ledger stays out of other people's columns.
