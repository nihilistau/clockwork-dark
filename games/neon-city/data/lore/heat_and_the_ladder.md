# Heat, and the Ladder Down

## The Tiers, as the Street Names Them
Clean: nobody is looking for you; enjoy it. Noticed: your handle is in somebody's notes, and vendors add a polite few percent. Wanted: there is paper on you; dealers stop answering and contraband is refused at every counter that values its licence to not have one. Hunted: bounty teams work your last known corners, and the Grid Point will not pass you — the mast does not negotiate. Burned: every camera in the city knows your face. Get off the street.

## It Never Fades
Heat is not weather. It does not blow over. The city's attention, once earned, is held — in notes, in feeds, in the patience of databases — until somebody spends money making it let go. This is the single most expensive fact in a runner's life and the one most often learned late.

## The Ladder
The going rates, posted nowhere and known everywhere: an ID scrub for one hundred thirty, which dissolves an alias in one database. A cooldown spoof for three hundred — forged closure paperwork, your file marked resolved by a signing authority that does not exist. A burner face for seven hundred, subdermal and deeply unpleasant for forty minutes. And Ghost Protocol, fifteen hundred, from people you will never meet: for most purposes you briefly stop existing.

## The Favour Rates
Cheaper than the ladder and harder to buy: Mira takes a name off a list for five hundred, when she likes you. Dane's four seconds of looking at the ground is four hundred, and it is not a bribe — it is a care-plan instalment with a gate attached, and it needs to be treated like one.
