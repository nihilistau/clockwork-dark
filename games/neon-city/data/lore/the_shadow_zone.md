# The Shadow Zone

## Past the Wire
No streetlights, no cameras, no count. The city's romantics call this freedom, from inside the city. The people who have crossed it call it the dark, and use the word the way sailors use "the sea" — a thing with moods, a thing that takes, a thing you provision against.

## The Uncounted
People live out there. Every camera forgot them and they have decided to return the favour: no handles, no standing, no prices in credits. Everything past the wire is barter, and fair is the only currency that has kept its value. They know the ground, the weather's habits, and the lift shed's age, and they trade all three for news and food.

## The Weather, Unmediated
The same sky as the city, with nothing between you and it: no awnings, no doorways, no forecast that reaches this far with any conviction. Acid rain bites flesh and gear alike; a storm closes the crossing outright, and the closing is not negotiable. Expeditions are planned in bars because the bars have roofs, and the planning is mostly about the sky.

## The Lift Shed
A poured-slab structure older than every logo in the city, holding a freight lift head whose doors want codes and whose winch interlocks want three pairs of hands. It goes down. It is not documented to come up. The uncounted do not camp near it, which is the most informative fact about it anyone has collected.

## The Hall Below
Cold, and the hum: nineteen years of accumulated description in a hall poured before the city had this name. There are no prices below the lift because there is nothing to buy and no one to sell it — the machine does not transact; it counts. A visitor's arrival changes nothing in its behaviour, which is the most frightening thing about it and the reason every ending in this story is a decision made ABOUT it, never a negotiation WITH it.
