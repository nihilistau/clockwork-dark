# The DeepState Bunker

## Not on Any Map
You do not find the Bunker. It finds you, and it only finds the cool — arrive quiet, under the threshold where the city's attention would follow you in, or the door that is not on any map simply continues not being on it. Nobody knows how it decides. The alley's theory is that it does not decide; it verifies.

## The Shelving
Drives, numbered, going back further than the towers do — and the numbering does not start at one. The shelving is the oldest continuous record in the city and possibly the point of the city, depending on which end of it you believe was built first.

## The Archivist
Keeps the record of what the city agreed to forget. Refers to living people in the past tense and is usually right. No face you can hold in memory longer than it takes to look away, and blots the page like it is 1908. The cold greeting, delivered without cruelty: "You were a promising entry. Entries close."

## Wren Solis
The cutout — "the part of the wire you can cut without losing the signal." Four names this year, answers to none of them twice; the only face DeepState will let you see. The counter sells dossiers, access codes, and the only honest map of the Shadow zone, at a quarter over market. The price is on it. The price is not a suggestion.

## What DeepState Is
The shadow under the shadow: the organisation — if that word applies to something with no members anyone can name — that was keeping the count's older ledgers before the towers existed, and intends to be keeping them after. The forecast on the player's shard is theirs in the sense that the FORM is theirs: the count produces; DeepState curates what the production is allowed to mean. Their interest in the player is exactly what the Archivist says it is — an interesting variable, to be resolved by observation for preference, by intervention if the record requires it.
