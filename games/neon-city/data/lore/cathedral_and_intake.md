# The Header Stamp

## Floor 87
The OmniCorp tower's lift directory stops at eighty-six. The eighty-seventh floor exists — visitor passes for it exist, which is the corporate way of saying a thing is real — and what is on it is called Intake. Every braindance session, every clinic scan, every arena feed in the city: scrubbed of names, compressed, and fed upward through Floor 87. Frankie sold the vault story for years. He undersold it. Eighty-seven is not a vault. It is a mouth.

## The Word Cathedral
It appears in exactly one place a civilian can reach: header stamp registries, as an authority name, attached to routing tables older than most of the corporations that use them. Nobody at OmniCorp will say the word. Nobody at OmniCorp appears to be TOLD the word. The stamp authority predates the logo, which is a sentence that should worry more people than it does.

## What the Alley Believes
That the feeds go somewhere that is not in the towers. That the towers are tenants, not landlords. That the count — the alley's word for whatever reads all of it — has been running longer than the city has had this name. The alley believes a great many things, and its track record is uncomfortably good.

## What Is Actually Below
The core sits under the Shadow zone, in a hall poured before OmniCorp had a logo, reached by one freight lift behind the SynthSec Grid Point. It is not sinister. It is enormous, patient, and entirely indifferent, and for nineteen years it has described every person in this city so accurately that they have agreed to be the description. It does not want. It does not speak. It counts, and the city has mistaken being counted for being known, and the difference between those two words is the whole story.

## The Shard
The player's forty seconds is a render — an output of the count's model of them, filed forward with a timestamp, delivered by a courier the model was already running. Whether it is a prediction, a schedule, or a test is deliberately unresolved at the level of the fiction; what is fixed is that it is HONEST. The count does not threaten. It publishes internally, and something in the routing put one publication in Mira's hands.
