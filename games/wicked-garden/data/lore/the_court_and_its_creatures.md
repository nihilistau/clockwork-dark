# The Court And Its Creatures

## The Bloomkin
Minor Fae servants, roughly peony-sized, with too many joints and the conversational energy of an elderly relative who has decided the family should hear this. They are ancient. They are not childlike and nothing about them should be read as such — the voice is gossip and grievance and an excellent memory for slights. They carry the wine, the messages and every private word ever said in front of them, and they trade all of it.

Being liked by the bloomkin is worth more at court than being liked by half the courtiers, because the bloomkin decide what the story of last night was. Being disliked by them is a thing a guest finds out about six days late.

## The Court
Several dozen beautiful people whose occupation is being present when something happens. They decide nothing. They decide what everything meant, afterwards, out loud, and the Sovereign has to live in the version they settle on — which is the actual source of their power and is why humiliation at court is a real injury rather than an embarrassment. Feast-lords, dream-spinners, bone gardeners and pleasure-merchants rotate through by season. All of them are tempting, intriguing and dangerous, and none of them are innocent, because there are no innocent Fae.

## Thorn Hounds
Thornwake's pack. They patrol, they are disciplined, and they are not decorative. They go down the bone paths at a soldier's pace rather than a hunter's, which is the difference between a patrol and a hunt and is worth knowing which one you are hearing.

## Lantern Moths
Light, and surveillance, and the two are not separable. The court counts by them — "before the moths count thirteen" is how an appointment is made — and the counting is reliable except when it is not, which has happened twice in living memory and was discussed for a season.

## Memory Bees
They do not work flowers. They work confessions: a thing said aloud with feeling behind it is harvested, and what they make of it is black and edible and keeps. A guest who talks freely in the glade is, quite literally, producing.

## Statue-Watchers
Frozen guests, standing in the labyrinth clearings, very finely finished. Occasionally one is found standing differently. The court's position is that this is an artistic tradition. Nobody holds this position with any conviction and nobody proposes an alternative.

## Rootlings
Mother Briar's immune system. They come up when the yield is thin, they correct rather than punish, and they do not negotiate because there is nothing in them to negotiate with. A court that sees rootlings has already been told something by somebody who does not use words.

## The Flora
Semi-sentient and interested. Flowers track a guest the way flowers track the sun, and which guest they track is public information updated hourly. Cutting one without leave is a social crime with actual consequences. The Garden's plants do not attack; they report.

## What The Chorus Is Actually Measuring

The bloomkin and the court are not merely gossiping. Between them they perform the Garden's only continuous audit: whether the emotions at the table are real. Bloomkin gossip is the raw feed and courtly consensus is the summary, and the summary is what the root reads when it decides whether the season is Bloom or Hush.

Which is why the Sovereign cannot simply command the court to adore a guest and be done with it. A commanded consensus produces theatre, theatre produces nothing, and a Sovereign who has to command her own court's opinion has already begun to fail the audit she is being subjected to. She has to be genuinely wanted, in public, by someone who could have declined — and every creature in this section is, without knowing it, an instrument for measuring exactly that.
