# The Honeyed Dead

## Preserved, Not Buried
Under the Feasting Glade the Root Crypts run in rose-catacombs lined with amber sap standing upright in the shape of people. Former lovers, guests and trespassers, held at the moment they were most beautiful, with nameplates in living script beneath them. They are not corpses and they are not ghosts. They are preserved, and enough of each one is still present to talk.

The tone of the place is not decay. It is erotic memento mori: overripe, gilded, warm, and arranged with obvious care by somebody who thought this was a kindness and may have been right.

## What They Say
They whisper advice, and the advice may be envy. There is no reliable way to tell. Some of them are still almost people, with grievances and jokes and a clear memory of the argument they were losing when they were preserved. Some are closer to flowers that happen to contain a recollection. All of them are on a hundred-year delay and all of them have opinions about the current guest, and the opinions arrive in the second person.

Between them they demonstrate the ending states. Beloved Thrall stands in one alcove looking radiantly content. Rooted is in the third row and has begun to leaf. Feast is not displayed.

## Elias The Painter
The first claim, and the canon example. A mortal artist who painted the Sovereign true — genuinely true, which is a harder thing than flattery and considerably more dangerous — and she kept him, and she kept him, until the paint learned to scream. He is upright in the sap gallery with his lips almost moving and his eyes following, mid-sentence for a hundred years. He is articulate, unbitter in a way that is much worse than bitterness would be, and he asks every new guest the same question: are you the new canvas or the new knife.

Asked whether she loved him, he says yes. That is why he is art.

## The Others
Seris, who was Fae-blooded, became a rival rather than a possession and fled into the Deep, and is the proof that equals leave scars on both parties. The Twin Guests, who arrived together and bargained separately, one of whom went home; bargains split people, and the surviving twin has never been located. And the empty coffin in the second row, which is warm, and which nobody in the Garden will discuss.

## What The Preservation Is For

The Honeyed Dead are not trophies and the crypts are not a mausoleum. They are Briar's preserves: emotional yield held in storage against a thin season, drawn down when the court above stops producing. A preserved lover releases slowly for centuries, which is why they are kept articulate — a remnant that can still feel something about its own situation keeps generating, and a remnant that has gone quiet is spent and is composted without ceremony.

The Sovereign knows this and describes them as her memorial gallery. Both descriptions are accurate. The reason she visits is not sentiment; it is that a Sovereign who lets the preserves run down is a Sovereign whose grove starts hearing the knock in the floor.

The empty warm coffin is not a mystery. It is prepared, it is the right size, and it is standing in the row that corresponds to the current century.
