# The Mortal World

## What Is Happening While You Are Here
The waking world does not pause. It runs at ten to one and it runs without you in it, and everything that would have happened happens anyway. Post accumulates. Work is reassigned. The people who love you move through worry, then grief, then the version of the story they can live with, and they do it on their own schedule, which is now ten times faster than yours.

The horror of missing your own life is designed to sit level with every other danger in this place, and by about the fourth day it is winning.

## How A Disappearance Is Metabolised
Relationships either decay or mythologise. Both happen and which one you get is not something you can influence from here. Some people are searched for and some are quietly reclassified as someone who left. Occasionally there are reports of strange blooms in the missing person's city — roses in the wrong month, a smell of soil in a stairwell — which is the Garden leaking through a claim that is still open at the other end.

## Coming Back
Returnees age oddly, in bursts that do not match the calendar. They dream in vines. They attract other Fae for the rest of their lives, because a mortal who has been inside the membrane and come out reads, at a distance, as a door that was propped open once. There is no established way to close it again.

Nobody who comes back is asked good questions by anyone qualified to ask them.

## The Aviary Of Unsent Letters
A dome of wire and briar full of paper birds that will not settle. Every one is a message somebody wrote to the waking world and could not make leave, and there are more of them than there have been guests. They know a new arrival's handwriting before the arrival has written anything. What the aviary is for is not clear even to the court, and the two available theories are that it is a courtesy and that it is a filter.

## Mara Quill
A human who left the Garden. Years ago, or yesterday, depending on which clock is being asked. She is the proof that escape is possible and the itemised bill for it, in one person. She is a burned romantic and a comprehensively unreliable narrator of the Sovereign, and every single specific thing she says about the laws is correct.

She stands the wrong side of the arch, where the roses will not open for her, and says nothing to Thornwake, who says nothing back. She wants to warn the current guest. She also wants to drag them back out with her, and she also, on some days, wants a way in for reasons she has stopped describing as revenge. All three are live simultaneously and she is not managing them.

## What It Cost Her, Exactly

Mara got out on an older law, correctly invoked, first attempt — which is a genuinely remarkable thing to have done and is why she is still alive. What the invocation could not do was retroactively unspend the debt. She was gone nineteen Garden days. That is a hundred and ninety outside, and the outside she returned to had already finished grieving her, redistributed her life, and moved somebody else into the middle of it.

She came back whole and had nothing to be whole in. She has since worked out, correctly, that the leaving was the easy part and that nobody warns you about the second half because nobody who has lived through the second half is in a position to be believed.

Her real fear, the one she cannot say to a new guest, is that the current mortal will get out — and that watching a successful escape land properly would mean her own outcome was not the price of freedom but simply her.
