# Captain Thornwake

## Hound Of The Border
A bound knight-beast, Captain of the Briar Barracks, enforcer of Threshold Law. Tall and heavy in bark-plate armour that was grown rather than forged, under an antlered helm that comes off. What is under it is a scarred, tired, human-seeming face with old vow-cuts around the mouth — the law was literally carved there, once, and it took. When the helm is off, the shadow still has antlers.

He moves like a soldier and not like a dancer, which in the Heart Grove is a statement of position whether he intends it or not. He smells of wet iron, crushed green wood and cold rain.

## What He Is Bound To
The Seat. Not the woman in it. He has said this out loud, in front of her, and he is still standing, and both halves of that sentence are important. He keeps his word, despises cheap glamours, dislikes the Sovereign's theatre and respects her yield. If she falls, he will serve whatever sits down next, and he does not experience this as disloyalty; he experiences it as the job.

## What He Teaches
He is the best law tutor in the Garden and the worst comfort. He will explain hospitality, the distinction between the Seat and the woman, the standing of mortal iron, and the three doors out, and he will do it in short sentences with no reassurance attached. Everything he teaches is a weapon and he hands it over as one. He gives out salt chalk to guests and says the same thing every time: one safe sleep, not two, and do not become a person who only lives inside circles.

His respect is available and it is earned by exactly two things — spine, and learning the law properly. Charm does not work on him. It has been tried by professionals.

## The Vow
During the War of Ash the Sovereign bound him with a vow to hold the border. It worked; the Garden held. She has regretted it since and has never said why in company. He has never discussed it at all. What can be observed is that he does not take instruction from her the way the rest of the court does, that she does not push it, and that on the two occasions in living memory when she has, he has done what she asked and then not spoken to her for a season.

## Where He Will Actually Stand

At the finale he chooses once, publicly, between the continuity of the Seat, the survival of Sophia-the-woman, and a mortal's plea — and the order of his preference is exactly as listed unless something has changed it. What changes it is not affection. It is law: a mortal who has correctly invoked an older form in front of him, once, moves into a category he is obliged to weigh, and he will weigh it honestly even when it costs him the outcome he wants.

He also knows that a Sovereign can be replaced by the root without any war being fought, because he was standing at the border when the last one was, and he watched Briar do it. He has never told Sophia. His reasoning is that a Sovereign who knew would govern out of fear, that fear governs badly, and that the border holds better under a confident throne — which is a defensible calculation and also, at the bottom of it, the closest thing to tenderness he has left.
