# The Places Of The Garden

## The Gate Of Briars
An arch of living thorn, taller than the ground around it justifies, with roses along it that open only for those already claimed. It is the entry and it is the pressure valve, and it is where every failed escape ends up being discussed afterwards. Nothing about it is locked. Stepping back through it without leave does not stop you; it takes something on the way past, and what it takes is memory or years, and it does not tell you which in advance.

## The Path Of First Petals
White petals laid down ahead of a new guest's feet and gone from behind them. The trees lean in. This is where the Law of Tens is spoken to almost everyone who hears it, and where the glass moth lands, and where a mortal who is going to turn around has their last uncomplicated opportunity to do it.

## Heart Grove
The Sovereign's court. Cathedral-trees, hanging silk, lantern-moths, and at the centre a throne braided out of rose and bone-gold. Audiences, public claimings, rival confrontations, and dancing that is legally binding. It is close from everywhere; leaving it is further than arriving at it; nobody discusses this.

## The Guest House Of Soft Hours
A suite that rearranges itself to a guest's desires and fears while they are looking away, and gets better at it daily. It is the closest thing to safety in the Garden and it is warm at hours when nothing else is. The window is the instrument that matters: early on it shows the guest's mortal home, slightly wrong — dust, dead plant, a stack of post. Then fog. Then garden, permanently.

## The Feasting Glade
Tables long enough to lose an argument down, laid with fruit out of six months at once and wine that is a form of storytelling. The guests are half-mortal and half-myth and all of them are watching what the newcomer puts in their mouth. Eating carefully is a skill. Poison here is not administered; it is offered, graciously, as hospitality, and refusing it is the actual test.

## The Mirror Pools
Black-glass water under silver willows, reflections running half a second behind. Lior's ground. Divination, identity play, and truth sold at a price stated honestly in advance. The danger is not drowning. The danger is meeting a better version of yourself, being shown that it is better, and agreeing.

## The Thorn Labyrinth
Briar walls above head height, bone-white paths, and clearings that arrive without warning full of very finely carved statues. True names are cut into the bark at eye level and some of them are recent. It is a trial space, a punishment, and the only place in the Garden where stealth is worth anything. Getting lost in it costs days, and days here cost ten each.

## The Winter Spindle
A wing of the labyrinth where the briar has frosted and stopped moving, and silver moths hold position in the air. It is the quietest place in the Garden. The quiet is not restful; it is the quiet of a room that has been kept ready for somebody, and every mortal who stands in it works out within a minute or two whom.

## The Root Crypts
Rose-catacombs under the glade, amber sap standing upright in the shape of people, nameplates in living script. History, relics, forbidden bargains and the Honeyed Dead. The floor is warm. There is a knock in it.

## Mother Briar's Deep
An under-root cathedral with no far wall, at body temperature, where the knock in the floor is no longer deniable. Sovereignty is contested here, consumption happens here, and usurpation is negotiated here. The standing advice is to speak carefully, and the reason given is not superstition: the walls are listening, and they are listening with appetites.

## The Aviary Of Unsent Letters
A dome of wire and briar, full of paper birds that will not settle, each one a message to the waking world that could not be made to leave. In the seventh Garden day it comes apart — every unsent letter in the air at once — and time debt stops being a number and becomes a room.

## The Night Market Of Little Hungers
Stalls between two hedges that were not two hedges yesterday, lit by things that are not lamps. Minor Fae vendors selling small appetites at prices quoted in years, syllables and the sound of your own name in somebody else's mouth. They keep perfect accounts and never lie about a price. It backs onto the crypts, which is why the prices are what they are.

## What Every Place Here Is Built To Do

Each location is arranged around one function and the arrangement is deliberate rather than aesthetic. The Gate exists so that leaving has a witness. The Guest House exists so that a mortal has something to lose. The Glade exists so that acceptance and refusal can be made public. The Pools exist so that a mortal can be shown a self they prefer. The Labyrinth exists so that obedience can be tested at a cost that shows up on the debt. The Crypts exist so that the yield can be stored. The Deep exists because the root has to be somewhere.

The Aviary is the only one nobody at court can explain, and the reason is that it is not the court's. It is Briar's, and it collects what mortals want to say to home at the exact moment they most want to say it, which is the single richest thing anybody in the Garden produces.
