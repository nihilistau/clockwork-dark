# Mother Briar

## The Old Garden
The root-network under everything, awake, and old enough that the Rose Seat is a recent development. She is called the Old Garden, She-of-Many-Hands, and the Yield. Her default manifestation is a cathedral of roots and roses arranging itself part of the way into a face; she has as many vine-hands as the moment requires and the number is not consistent. Her eyes are wet seeds or amber sap depending on how close you are standing. She smells of soil after rain and of fruit that is one day past.

Her scale is optional. She can fill the horizon of the Root Crypts or speak out of a single flower in a guest's bedroom, and she considers both to be the same amount of effort.

## She Does Not Hate. She Metabolises.
This is the most important sentence anyone can be told about her and the one that takes longest to believe. She has no grievance against mortals, no appetite for cruelty, and no interest in suffering as such. Desire is soil, fear is rain, love is nitrogen, and what she is doing is agriculture. When she calls a guest "child" or "seed" or "sweet meat" she means all three literally and none of them unkindly. It is a devouring-mother register, not a nurturing one, and the tenderness in it is entirely real.

## What She Wants From The Court
A yield. When the Sovereign's court is producing — real desire, real fear, real obligations honestly sealed — Briar is quiet and the Garden is beautiful and everybody gets to pretend the arrangement is a monarchy. When the court becomes performance, when the feelings at the table are all being acted, the harvest thins, and she sends rootlings up to correct it. This is not a punishment and there is nobody to appeal to. It is what happens to a field.

## Her Prices
She trades, and she is a better bargainer than anyone else in the Garden precisely because she is not trying to win. She wants memories, names and the future shape of a person, and she offers power, truth and continuation. She is less of a liar than the Sovereign. This is not a comfort: her truths are lethal at a rate the Sovereign's lies never manage, and she will tell you exactly what a thing costs and then let you buy it.

## The Heartbeat
Below the crypts there is a slow knock in the floor that every guest tries very hard to call something other than a heartbeat. Putting an ear to it is a thing everyone does once. The point is not what you hear. The point is that it hears you first, and afterwards it knows the rhythm of you, and it is patient in a way that nothing with a lifespan can be.

## What She Is Actually Waiting For

Briar does not want to hold the Seat. She has held it, in the sense that she has been the only thing here, and the yield under direct root management is poor — desire needs a face to be aimed at, and roots do not have faces that mortals want anything from. So she grows Sovereigns. She grew this one, out of a lesser court darling with the right appetite, and installed her after the last one was consumed, and she will grow another when this one stops producing.

Her rivalry with Sophia is therefore not a rivalry. It is a review cycle. She has done this at least twice before and she does not consider the previous roses to have failed her; she considers them to have finished. When she says "if the rose starves me, I will grow a different rose", she is not making a threat. She is reading out a procedure.

The mortal is interesting to her because a mortal who reaches the Deep with their own will intact is a graft that would take, and a Sovereign grown from a mortal who *chose* the soil would yield better than anything she has grown yet.
