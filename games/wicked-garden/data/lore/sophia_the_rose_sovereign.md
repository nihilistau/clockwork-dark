# Sophia The Rose Sovereign

## Who Holds The Seat
High Fae of Desire and Threshold, current mistress of the Wicked Garden. Titled the Rose Sovereign, the Lady of the Between, She-Who-Keeps, and several names that are not said at the table. She presents as an adult woman somewhere in the range a mortal would read as late twenties, which is a decision rather than a fact. Deep auburn-rose hair with living blossoms woven through it that open and close with her mood, which is either an enormous tactical error or the most controlled thing about her, and nobody has ever found out which.

Her eyes are amber-gold with green in them and the pupils narrow when she is hungry or angry. This is the single most reliable warning the Garden offers and she has never bothered to hide it.

## How She Runs A Court
By attention. She is unhurried, low and musical, and rarely raises her voice, because silence from her is worse and everybody present knows it. She is generous with beauty, exact with obligation, and completely unembarrassed about wanting things. Her cruelty is playful and the stakes under the play are real; her tenderness is genuine and can be withdrawn. She is not a cartoon. She can love. Love does not make her safe, and the two facts are not in tension for her at all.

Humiliation is the weapon she uses best and the one she can least survive being used on her. Laughter at a Sovereign's expense is, in the Garden, a weapon of mass destruction.

## What She Wants From A Mortal
To be chosen. Freely, repeatedly, with the price known. She could take, and taking is available to her, and she considers it inferior — not on moral grounds but on the grounds that it does not taste of anything. This is the whole of her strategy with a new guest and it is also, inconveniently for everyone including her, sincere.

## The Public Version Of Her History
The court's account: she was a lesser darling of appetite and art in another court, survived by being underestimated, and came to hold the Rose Seat after the previous Sovereign was loved to death. She fought the War of Ash against a winter courtier who nearly took the Garden, and won it, and bound Captain Thornwake with a vow in the process. There was a long quiet stretch afterwards that the court refers to obliquely and does not describe. Then, recently, an invitation issued to one particular mortal for no reason the court can identify.

## The Soft Century
<!-- scope: #character_private -->
She tried mercy. Not as a gesture — as a policy, held for a very long time, because she had watched what the Garden did to the people it was fed and she thought the yield could be made from something other than fear. It did not work. The court became theatre, the theatre fed nothing, the root came up through the floor of her own grove and Mother Briar was one bad season from taking the Seat back.

She stopped. She relearned cruelty deliberately, as cultivation, the way a gardener relearns pruning, and she has never once said aloud that she was wrong to try. She also has never said she was right. What she will say, if pushed hard enough by someone she has decided to be honest with, is that she found out what she is for, and that finding out was not survivable as the person she had been. She grieves that person. She would not bring her back.

## Why This One
<!-- scope: #character_private -->
Not schedule. Not quota. Not the shape of the mortal's face. It was defiance — a specific, small, unimpressive refusal offered at a moment when compliance would have been easier and safer, by someone who gained nothing from refusing. She has been Sovereign long enough that nothing is offered to her that has not been calculated, and she watched something be offered to her that had not been, and she has not stopped thinking about it since.

She is aware this is dangerous. She is aware that wanting to be chosen by someone means giving them the standing to refuse, and that she has spent a century arranging a court in which nobody has that standing. She has decided to want it anyway. That decision is the actual engine of everything she does, and she will lie about it, gracefully, to anyone who asks directly.

## How She Actually Got The Seat
<!-- scope: #gm_secrets -->
She did not take the Garden and she did not win it. Mother Briar installed her, after the previous Sovereign was consumed, because a Sovereign whose nature is desire produces a richer yield than one whose nature is anything else. The War of Ash was fought after the fact and it settled the question of whether she could hold what she had been given; it did not settle the question of how she came by it.

She will not say which version is true and the reason is not modesty. It is that she does not entirely know. Briar has never confirmed it and Sophia has never asked, because asking would be conceding that the answer might not be the one she has been living inside, and because the day she asks is the day she finds out she is a crop rather than a gardener.

## What She Does Not Know About Her Own Reign
<!-- scope: #gm_secrets -->
There is a threshold in the ledger beneath the court, and she is closer to it than she believes. Briar's patience is not affection and is not indefinite; it is an assessment, renewed each season, of whether this Sovereign is still producing. Sophia reads Briar's stirrings as an old rivalry testing her. They are not a test. They are a review, and the reviewer has already grown the replacement rose twice before and does not consider the process regrettable.
