# The Wicked Garden

## What It Is
Three layers overlap here. The waking world, with its cities and jobs and clocks and entropy. The True Courts of the Deep Fae, whose politics are alien and whose whole is rarely entered. And between them the Wicked Garden: a living demiplane of desire and bargains, which is a hunting ground, a sanctuary, a prison and a throne depending entirely on who is holding the Rose Seat this century. It is a membrane. Membranes have two sides and are permeable in one direction more easily than the other.

## How Mortals Get In
Four ways, in descending order of how much the Sovereign likes them. By invitation, which is her preferred method and the only one she considers polite. By trespass — mirrors, old gates, forest paths that were not there last week and will not be there next. By bargain leakage, which is one wish worded a little too well. And by inheritance, when a previous lover's claim was left unfinished and the Garden decides you will do.

Exits are rarer than entrances. Nobody in the Garden considers this remarkable and nobody has ever offered to fix it.

## The Map Is A Courtesy
The Garden is not a fixed landscape. It is a constellation of places that rearrange along the shape of a story — the same landmarks, in a different order, at a distance that depends on who wants what. The Heart Grove is always close. It is close from everywhere. Walking away from it takes longer than walking to it, every time, and if you point this out in company you will be told that you are imagining it by people who are not.

Travel here is not a commute. It is a scene with a price on it: guided by the Sovereign, which is safe and indebting; found on foot, which costs hours and events; opened with a token, a petal, a thorn, a reflection; or walked in a dream, with the risk of waking somewhere you did not lie down.

## The Living Furniture
The flora is semi-sentient and takes an interest. Flowers track a guest the way flowers track the sun, and cutting one without leave is not vandalism, it is a social crime, and it will be discussed. Lantern-moths give light and carry what they saw. Memory bees harvest confessions and turn them into something black and edible. Thorn hounds patrol. Rootlings are the Garden's immune system and answer to something older than the throne. Statue-watchers stand in the labyrinth clearings, very finely carved, and occasionally are found standing differently.

## What The Garden Eats
Desire is soil. Fear is rain. Love is nitrogen. This is not a metaphor the inhabitants use; it is the description they use, and they use it in the flat voice of people discussing yields. A Sovereign's job is to keep the emotional harvest rich enough that the root-network beneath the court stays fed and asleep. A court that becomes pure theatre — beautiful, performed, and feeling nothing real — starves it. What happens then is not a punishment. It is weather.

## The Scar Underneath

The Root Crypt tablets carry a version of the origin the court does not repeat. The Garden is not a garden. It is scar tissue over a war between two Deep Fae Courts, and the membrane exists to stop a full invasion of the waking world. It farms mortal emotion because that is the fuel keeping the scar sealed. Under this reading a Sovereign is a warden and a parasite in the same breath, the seal is maintained by feeding it people, and every Sovereign who has ever sat the Rose Seat has known it and has gone on describing the place as a garden because the alternative description does not attract visitors.

The court's own origin myth — a seed fallen where a human dream of *forever* met a Fae dream of *feast* — is not a lie. It is simply the version that can be told at a table.
