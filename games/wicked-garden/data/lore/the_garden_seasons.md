# The Garden Seasons

## Four Weathers, None Of Them Astronomical
The Garden has seasons and they have nothing to do with the sun, which is not reliably present. They are emotional and political states of the whole demiplane, they turn when the court's mood turns, and everyone inside can feel which one is running the way a mortal can feel a change in pressure before a storm.

## Bloom
Courtship weather. Feasts, gifts, dances that are contracts, soft danger everywhere and no visible teeth. The Sovereign is at her most generous and the Garden is at its most beautiful, and both facts are doing work. Bloom is when a guest is most likely to accept something without asking the price, which is not a coincidence and is not, strictly speaking, a trap either. It is simply what the weather is for.

## Thorn
Trial weather. Jealousy, tests, the labyrinth opening its assignments. The court becomes noticeably more interested in who is standing where, rivals introduce themselves properly, and favours become impossible on purpose. Nothing in Thorn is offered that is not also a measurement.

## Harvest
Reckoning weather. Threads come due, endings become visible in the pools, and the court stops being able to pretend the season is early. The Sovereign is at her sharpest and least playful. Debts that have been carried comfortably for days become suddenly, publicly, itemised.

## Hush
The dangerous one. Mother Briar is loudest in Hush, and the Sovereign is either at her most vulnerable or at her most cruel, and there is no way to tell in advance which of the two is arriving. The court goes quiet. The flowers stop tracking. The knock in the floor of the crypts is audible from the glade, and everyone above ground behaves as though it is not.

## The Seasons Are Not The Engine's Phases
The four seasons are this story's own clock and they are not the engine's evil-phase enum. This story runs no doom ticker at all — the manifest sets the base rate to zero — so the engine's phase stays dormant from the prologue to the finale, on purpose. Nothing in this Garden should be written to trigger on the phase; the pressure here is four named clocks, a debt that only grows, and the season the court is currently living in.

## Who Turns The Weather

The Sovereign's word is weather in the small sense — a room's temperature, the length of a path, whether the roses open. It is not weather in this sense. The four seasons turn on the yield: Bloom while the harvest is rich, Thorn while it is being tested, Harvest while it is being taken in, and Hush when it has come in thin.

Which means Hush is not a mood. Hush is Briar being hungry, and every Sovereign who has ever held this Seat has learned to read it as an appointment rather than a season. Sophia manages Hush by producing something real for the root to eat before it comes up looking. In a century of practice she has never once failed to. She has, twice, come close enough that the court floor cracked.
