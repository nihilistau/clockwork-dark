# Ashen Vale

## The Courtier At The Cold End
Androgynous High Fae of winter ash and silver moths, exiled out of a winter court and never once described as a refugee by anyone who has met him. Pale, in a cloak that is moth-wing at some angles and fabric at others, hung with black glass. His breath frosts in rooms that are warm. He is exquisitely mannered, and the manners are not a disguise over contempt; they are the delivery mechanism for it.

He sits at the far end of the Feasting Glade table, where the fruit does not ripen, and he has never been asked to leave. That fact is itself a whole conversation about the state of the court.

## The War Of Ash
He very nearly took the Garden. Winter moths came through the membrane in a season the court still does not describe in detail, and the Sovereign held the Seat by binding Captain Thornwake with a vow she has regretted since. Vale lost. Vale did not leave. The Garden's laws do not provide for expelling a guest who has committed no offence since, and he has committed no offence since, and he brings this up in conversation approximately once a season in a tone of mild regret.

## What He Says He Believes
That the Sovereign runs the Garden on heat, that heat burns membrane, and that when it goes the Fae weather will come through into the waking world and drown a great many mortals who never agreed to any of this. He states this openly. It has the considerable rhetorical advantage of being at least arguably correct, and it is the reason a thoughtful mortal finds him harder to dismiss than they expected to.

## The Offer
He will offer a mortal freedom, and what he offers is real. Passage, intelligence, a colder gate, protection from the Sovereign's lesser spies. The price is always one of four things: a warm memory of her, an unnamed future service, a sealed kiss, or a single syllable of a true name. Every one of them is priced fairly. Every one of them is another collar in a different metal, and he would say so himself if asked, which is precisely what makes him dangerous — a liar can be caught out, and he has arranged never to need to lie.

## What He Is Actually After

He does not want the mortal free. He wants the Sovereign humiliated, and those are not the same objective even when they are served by the same act. A Sovereign with an obsession is strong; an obsession is a reason to hold the Seat and a demonstration that she can still want. A Sovereign whose mortal walked out in front of the court is a Sovereign with a visible hole in her, and a Sovereign who is visibly holed is replaceable — which is the state Vale needs the Garden in before he can move on the Seat at all.

The mortal is therefore not the point of the offer. The mortal is the instrument of it. He would rather they left whole than not, because a wreck is easy to explain away and a survivor cannot be. This is the closest thing to kindness in his position and it is entirely load-bearing.

He also knows that the previous Sovereign was consumed and that Briar installed the current one, which is intelligence the rose court does not have. He has not used it. He is saving it for a moment when saying it out loud in the Heart Grove would do the most work.
