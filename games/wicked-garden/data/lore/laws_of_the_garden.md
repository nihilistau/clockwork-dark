# Laws Of The Garden

## Law Is Not Magic
There are no spells here. There is law, and law in the Garden is physical: correctly invoked, it constrains a High Fae the way a wall constrains a body. This is why the Sovereign's word is dangerous and why Thornwake, who is not more powerful than her by any measure, can occasionally stop her. She can break a law. Breaking one costs her something she cannot easily replace, in front of witnesses who count.

Guests learn the laws through consequence, through tutors, and through scars, in that order of reliability.

## Hospitality Binds Host And Guest
A guest under a roof is owed safety by the holder of that roof, and owes conduct in return. Both halves bind. A host who harms an accepted guest has broken something the whole court can see, and a guest who abuses hospitality dissolves their own protection without anybody having to lift a hand. It is the most useful law a new mortal can learn and the one most often invoked wrongly, because guests believe it protects them from unpleasantness. It protects them from harm. Those are different, and the difference is most of the first three days.

## Gifts Create Threads
A gift accepted without negotiation creates an obligation, and the obligation is named later, by the giver, at their convenience. This is not a trick played on the ignorant; it is simply what a gift is here, and every Fae in the Garden knows it the way a mortal knows that a signature means something. The move that is available and almost never taken is to ask, at the moment of offering, what the gift costs — which converts an open obligation into stated terms. Refusing outright is also available. It is an insult, and an insult is a price, and a price you have chosen is not the same thing as a debt you have not read.

## True Names Open And Close Doors
A name is not a label. It is the accurate statement of what a thing is, and to hold one is to hold a lever. Names open gates, close bargains, stop a Sovereign mid-sentence and pull a person back out of a transformation that had otherwise finished. They are also the most dangerous thing anyone can carry, because a name works in both directions and does not care who is holding which end.

## Blood And Seed Are Ink
The ritual language for sealing. A bargain sealed by word is a bargain; sealed with the body it is heavier, harder to sever, and readable by every witness afterwards. The Fae are not squeamish about this and are baffled by mortals who are, while being entirely capable of using that squeamishness as leverage. Nudity carries no shame here. Standing unarmed of glamours does, and the distinction is the whole of Fae modesty.

## The Sovereign's Word Is Weather
Whoever holds the Rose Seat can alter conditions inside the membrane by saying so. Not everything, not without cost, and never retroactively — but the temperature of a room, the length of a path, whether the roses open, whether a door was ever there. It is why the Seat is worth having and why a Sovereign who is being defied in public is in more danger than a Sovereign losing a fight.

## Older Laws Outrank New Thrones
The most important law and the last one anybody teaches. The Rose Seat is recent. The root beneath it is not. When Mother Briar stirs, the older law is available for invocation and it beats the Sovereign's word outright — which means a mortal who has learned the correct older form can, on one specific matter, on one occasion, be right at the Sovereign in a way she cannot simply out-weather. Getting the form wrong is worse than saying nothing at all.

## What The Laws Are For

The laws are not ethics and they were never a compromise between parties. They are the operating constraints of the membrane, written down after the fact by things that needed the seal to hold, and every one of them protects the Garden rather than anybody inside it. Hospitality keeps guests alive long enough to be worth harvesting. Gift-threads convert affection into an accounted obligation the root can actually eat. Names are the emergency stop. The Sovereign's word is the throttle, and "older laws outrank new thrones" is the clause that lets the soil overrule the crop when the crop starts failing.

A tutor who explains the laws as a code of honour is not lying. They have simply never been in a position where they had to know the difference.
