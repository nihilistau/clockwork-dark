# Gifts Threads And Bargains

## Gifts Are Politics
Every object handed to a mortal in the Garden is a position taken in public. What was given, by whom, in front of whom, and whether it was accepted, refused or renegotiated, is the substance of court conversation for the rest of the week. There is no such thing here as a small present. There is a large present and a present that is being deliberately small in order to say something.

## The Four Answers
A gift may be accepted, which is warm and creates an unnamed obligation. It may be refused, which is an insult, costs standing, and buys back something of yourself. It may be renegotiated — accepted with the cost stated aloud first — which is the move that marks a mortal as worth talking to and which almost nobody makes, because it requires being cool at exactly the moment they are being flattered. Or its cost may be raised: taking the gift and extracting a concession for the taking, which is rude, dangerous, and the single fastest route to being treated as a peer instead of a pet.

## The Anatomy Of A Bargain
Offer, terms, seal, thread. The offer is theatre. The terms are the only part that matters and the part everybody is encouraged to skim. The seal makes it real — a word, a kiss, a gift, blood — and the weight of the seal determines how hard the resulting thread is to sever. What remains afterwards is the thread: an obligation that persists until it is discharged, broken at cost, or transformed into something else.

Contracts here appear as illuminated manuscripts with living ink. The ink moves while you read it. It is not changing the terms; it is drawing attention to the clause you skipped.

## What Bargains Are Made Of
The currencies are Protection, Pleasure, Knowledge, Time, Freedom, Name, Body, Service, Silence and Sovereignty. Time and Name are the two that mortals consistently undervalue, because a mortal thinks of time as a quantity and a name as a word. Silence is the cheapest to promise and the hardest to keep. Sovereignty is almost never on the table, and a bargain that puts it there is a bargain somebody is planning to lose on purpose.

## Cutting A Thread
Four things sever a bargain. Mirror-glass, which cuts anything sealed and does not distinguish between a contract and an affection — Lior sells the knife and warns about this in the same sentence and nobody has ever listened. Law, correctly invoked, which requires actually knowing it. Death, which works and is regarded as a slightly gauche solution. And the Sovereign's word, available only to someone with a Seat to speak from.

A thread that is broken rather than discharged leaves a mark on both parties, and everyone can see it, and everyone will have an opinion.

## What The Threads Are Actually For

An obligation is not merely social. A sealed thread is a channel, and what runs down it toward the root beneath the court is the emotional yield of the relationship it describes — which is why the Garden's whole culture is arranged to produce as many live threads as possible, and why a Sovereign who cannot generate them is a Sovereign who is starving her own ground. Affection alone is not edible. Accounted, named, mutually-acknowledged obligation is.

This is also why stolen consent is described as lesser meat. A thread sealed by someone who was not free to refuse carries almost nothing. The Garden does not have a conscience about it; it has a preference, and the preference happens to look like one.
