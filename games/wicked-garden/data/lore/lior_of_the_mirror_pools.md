# Lior Of The Mirror Pools

## The Kind Unkind
High Fae of reflection, possibility and self-theft, keeper of the Mirror Pools, addressed as they and answering to Of the Mirror Pools, the Kind Unkind, and Borrower of Faces. Androgynous, uncannily symmetrical, with patches of liquid silver on the skin that move slowly and eyes of still black water that blink out of time with each other. Hair like willow-shadow. Bare feet that rest on the meniscus without breaking it.

Their reflection occasionally mouths different words. They have never explained this and appear to find the question rude.

## What They Sell
Not prophecy. They are explicit about it: they do not sell truth, they rent possible selves. A guest may look into the black water and see an ending — theirs, in motion, from the inside — and the looking is real and the ending is real and neither is a promise. What is charged is memory. A childhood, a lover, an achievement, a specific afternoon; paid over, gone, and the hole left behind is exactly the shape it was.

They are honest about being dishonest, which is a genuine and unusual courtesy. They advertise the knife. What they will not do — ever, on principle, and it is a real principle — is tell a customer that the price is too high.

## Cruel Via Empathy
They do not manipulate by lying. They show a person the thing they already secretly wanted and then step back and let the wanting do the work. Their favourite sentence about it is that they only show you what you already fear wanting, and it is true, and it is also the most efficient cruelty available in the Garden.

Looking too long at a flattering ending is the specific trap. A guest who has watched themselves become the Sovereign's equal will find survival feels like failure afterwards, even when survival was the win. They warn about this. The warning does not help and they know the warning does not help and they give it anyway, which is either decency or a refinement of the trick.

## Their Position At Court
Useful to everyone and owned by nobody. The Sovereign cannot ban them without blinding herself, since half of what she knows about her own court comes off that water. Vale trades futures of the winter routes with them. Briar pays in deep reflections of the roots and considers reflections low-calorie and the whole arrangement mildly annoying. Thornwake distrusts them flatly, on the grounds that mirrors make deserters.

## The Collection

Lior is not renting possibilities out. They are taking copies in. Every guest who looks long enough leaves a version of themselves in the water — coherent, continuous, and increasingly convinced it is the original — and the collection is now large enough to constitute a population. This is what the mirrored legion is. It is not a metaphor and it is not a threat; it is inventory, patiently assembled over centuries by something with no interest at all in the Rose Seat.

They are not working for anyone. They will help a mortal escape, help the Sovereign hold, help Vale open a cold gate and help Briar count the yield, in the same week, sincerely each time, because none of those outcomes affects the collection and every one of them produces another look into the water. The reason they are the most dangerous individual in the Garden is that they are the only one with no stake in how the story ends.
