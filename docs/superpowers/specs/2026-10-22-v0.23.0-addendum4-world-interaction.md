## §0 — Rule 12, first

Nothing here adds a register, a rating, a tier, a ceiling or any intensity
language, in the engine, in data or in a prompt. The "Rule 12 in this
design" section of the main spec stands unchanged. In particular:
- typed text is read only against closed lists: activity and room phrases,

  `talk` move, and never a refusal;
- the storyteller prompt's changes (W10) are length, a CHOICES section, the

## §1 — Goal, in the owner's terms

The world outside the phone is an interactive story, as dev-story is.

1. **Type anywhere.** In every room of the flat, the building, the street,
   every venue and every home, a typed line is a turn, as a choice is.
   "Make breakfast" or "go to the kitchen" does what it says when that is
   on offer (§3).
2. **People are there:** the staff and neighbours who belong there (W8),
   the person the player arranged to meet, who actually arrives (W4), and
   sometimes someone from the phone, met by chance (W9).
3. **Richer than a text.** The narrator knows who is present (dossier or
   companion block), writes 80-200 words and offers choices with intents.
   In a scene, what the player TYPES is scored like a picked move (W7).
4. **With or without a scene.** Typing always works; only what a typed line
   can do changes (§3).
5. **Nothing is lost on the way.** Choices never vanish after a phone
   action, a plan made where you stand can be kept, and nobody texts "I'm
   here" while they are not (W1-W5).

## §2 — Where the cast lives, and where it must NOT go

**Background people go through `npc_sim`** (`engine/world/npc_sim.py`),
exactly as dev-story's do: `paths.npc_schedules`, PEOPLE HERE, dossiers,
`present_npc_ids`, `/api/people` and the cast gate (F§2). That is the
engine's pattern (rule 7) and needs no engine change.

**The cast (the ~1,200 generated people) must NOT go through `npc_sim`**
(F§2), for four reasons:
1. `known_npc_ids` and `resolve_npc` walk every NPC on every prompt,
   `/api/people` call and cast-gate pass: 1,200 a call at day 30.
2. The cast gate's roster is `known_npc_ids`, and `relations.met` is never
   written for a contact. So prose naming an absent contact would fail, the
   buzz line included.
3. The strip's portraits are `shipped_art_url`; the cast's are
   `engine/social/media` URLs.
4. `npc_sim`'s pins and overrides are flags. They know nothing of windows,
   home templates, plans, `away` or `ghosted`.

**The cast's presence is `engine/social/presence.py`** (new). It is
read-only and derived on read, like `npc_sim` (`npc_sim.py:1-29`). It draws
nothing that moves a counter and writes nothing. It is the ONE answer to
"where is pid now", and it reads in two tiers:
- **Plan presence.** From a plan row's `arrives_at` (W4): O(open plans).
- **Chance presence.** Only when the player is at a venue (W9): O(live
  contacts), never the population.

**No second agent.** Dev-story's Sophia is a second agent with her own
prompt. The cast is voiced by the storyteller from the companion block
(main R10, §10.6). One agent per generated person is not viable, and
`agents.yaml` stays absent (main §8.3).

## §3 — Typed text and rule 1

Typed text carries no intent at the ROUTE (rule 1;
`default_state.resolve_player_intent` returns `{}` for it,
`default_state.py:799-835`). That function stays byte-identical. This story
reads the line later, on the server, under the lock.

- **The route is not widened.** The client still sends only
  `choice_id: "custom"` and `custom_text` (`App.jsx:275-300`). Any `intent`
  key a client might add is ignored, as today. Both route handlers
  (`default_scene.py:720`, `:813`) also hand the text to `run_guarded` as
  `typed_text=`. The default is None, and it is read only on the turnstile
  branch.
- **Read under the lock (C2).** `_run_world_turn`
  (`default_scene.py:470-512`) exists only for a `paths.social` session. It
  reads the line there:
  1. the turnstile is held;
  2. the turn lock is held;
  3. the `phone_up` re-check;
  4. `voicer.drain_inbox`;
  5. then `engine/social/typed.py::intent_for(state, text)`, the result
     replacing the route's `{}`, then `run_turn`.

  So the reading sees the state the turn will run on, and no voicer commit
  can land between the reading and the execution.
- **Only what the engine offers now.** `intent_for` returns either an
  intent whose action and target are in `intents.legal_intents(state)` at
  that moment, checked with the same `find_verb` and targets test that
  `tool_dispatcher.execute_intent` re-applies (`tool_dispatcher.py:150-170`),
  or `{}`. The check and the execution sit under one lock, so a typed line
  can never come back as a refusal. At worst it is narrated with no
  mechanic. A test pins that no typed turn's receipts hold a refusal.
- **The resolution order** (first match wins):
  1. **An activity or a room**, by the matcher below: a legal `do` target
     (a scene's shared activities included) or a legal core `travel`
     target;
  2. **in a scene, a topic** (W7): a legal `ask_about:<t>` or
     `tell_about:<t>`;
  3. **in a scene, `talk`** (W7);
  4. otherwise `{}`. The line is narrated only, with no mechanic and no
     time, as today.
- **It never leaves, commits or phones.** A typed line never infers:
  - a `go` (a venue, a home, "Head home");
  - a closer;
  - a plan, a promise, an `approach` or a phone action;
  - `go_to_work`, `carry_on_working` or `leave_early`.

  Commitments in person still come only from closers (main §10.4, I10).
  The narrator offers those as choices (W10).
- **The receipt says so.** It carries `typed: true`, and the narrator is
  told "The player said this; the engine read it as: <label> (<band>)".
- **Hosted.** Typed text is play text. It is never logged and never in a
  metric; `tests/test_admin_no_play_text.py` gains a typed turn. Only the
  inferred action and target are logged.

### 3.1 The matcher (`typed.match_offered`)

The matcher is deterministic, conservative and closed (re-review R1). It
matches a phrase the data lists, ANCHORED at the start of a clause, in a
line that carries no modal, desire, hypothetical, negation or time-shift
cue. It never matches on a phrase found merely somewhere in the line.
Ambiguity is no match.

**Inputs.**
- The legal `do` targets and the legal `travel` targets, and only those.
- Their phrases:
  - `activities.yaml`: each activity's `label` and a new `say:` list of
    base-form verb-object phrases, each two words or more ("make
    breakfast", "cook breakfast"; `tidy_up`'s "tidy up", "clean up", "do
    the dishes").
  - `activities.yaml typed.rooms`: per flat and building place, its
    `locations.yaml` `name` and a list of nouns (`apt_kitchen: [kitchen]`,
    `apt_hall: [hall, front door]`, `bldg_lift: [lift]`). They are kept in
    the story's own file, so core `locations.yaml`'s schema is untouched.
- The cue lists, all data, first values, in `activities.yaml typed:`:
  - `modals: [would, could, should, might, maybe, perhaps, sometime,
    someday, "let's", "want to", wanna, "love to", "like to", if, wish,
    "i'd", "for you", "for us"]`;
  - `negations: ["don't", "do not", not, never, "won't", "can't",
    cannot, "didn't", no]`;
  - `shifts: [later, tomorrow, tonight, after, afterwards, before,
    earlier, yesterday, soon, "instead of"]`;
  - `leads: [i, "i'll", "i'll just", ok, okay, now, then, "let me",
    so]`;
  - `progressive_leads: ["i'm", "i am"]`;
  - `movement: [go, head, walk, step, wander, pop]`.

**Steps.**
1. **Normalise.** Casefold the line and collapse whitespace. A line
   wrapped in asterisks (`*makes breakfast*`) is an ACTION: strip the
   asterisks and mark it `acted`.
2. **Reject the whole line** if it ends in `?`, or holds any `modals`,
   `negations` or `shifts` entry as whole words (`convo.names_label`,
   `convo.py:394-402`).
3. **Split into clauses** on `,` `;` `.` `!` and the words `and` and
   `then`.
4. **Strip leads.** In each clause, strip one leading `leads` entry. A
   leading `progressive_leads` entry is stripped too, and marks the clause
   `progressive`.
5. **Match the phrase's verb in an allowed form.** A clause matches a
   phrase only if the clause now STARTS with that phrase, its first word in
   one of these forms:
   - the base form ("make breakfast"): bare or after a lead;
   - `+s`/`+es` ("makes breakfast"): only in an `acted` line;
   - `-ing` (`e` dropped: "making breakfast"): only in a `progressive`
     clause or an `acted` line.
6. **Rooms.** A clause matches a room only if it starts with a `movement`
   verb, in the same forms, and one of the room's phrases follows within
   three words ("go to the kitchen", "head into the bedroom").
7. **Exactly one target** across all clauses. Two or more is no match.

**Why "I'll" is a lead.** A typed world line is the player's own move,
made now, in a story told in the present. With no time-shift or modal cue,
"I'll make breakfast" says what the player does next, and the narrator
renders it at once. It cannot be a promise the engine fails to keep:
- a promise is a phone construct (`engine/social/promises.py`, made only
  from a chip, the model's `promise` proposal or a typed phone phrase);
- a commitment in person comes only from a closer (main §10.4, I10);
- §3 forbids a typed line to infer either.

So the reading as an action leaves nothing owed. Every deferred reading
("I'll make breakfast later", "...tomorrow", "...sometime") carries a
`shifts` or `modals` cue and is rejected at step 2.

**Test cases** (`tests/test_social_typed.py`). Each runs with the target
legal where it matters, `make_breakfast` and the kitchen legal unless the
row says otherwise.

| Line | Result |
|---|---|

| "*makes breakfast*" / "*making breakfast*" | `do make_breakfast` (acted) |
| "I make breakfast" / "I'll make breakfast" / "ok, make breakfast" / "I'm making breakfast" | `do make_breakfast` |
| "do the dishes" / "tidy up" | `do tidy_up` |

| "I could make breakfast" / "maybe make breakfast" / "let's make breakfast" / "I want to make breakfast" / "if I make breakfast" | none (modal) |

**Three gaps remain** (read from the code at `6fe497c`, not run):

3. **The narrator's choices are thrown away** after any phone glance,

**Size.** S, as before, now smaller: no row-building of its own.

### W2 — Arriving where you already are

**Behaviour.** A plan can be kept without a `go` (F§4(a)).

**`plans.meet_if_here(state)`.** It opens the scene (`scene.open_scene`)
when all of these hold:
- an open `now`/`tonight` plan's person has arrived (W4: `arrived` is set
  on the row);
- the player is at the plan's place, with the phone down;
- no scene is open.

It is called ONLY after the clock has stopped moving (I4), from:
- `world.go`, after `social_go` writes the place (today's `_arrive`);
- `put_down`, after any away jump;
- the "Wait for" target below;
- the end of every `do`, interrupted or not;
- `end_scene` with `then: stay`;
- `_run_world_turn`, after its drain.

It never runs inside an event handler.

**"Wait for {name}".** This is a world `scene` target, `plan:<id>`. It is
offered while an open `now`/`tonight` plan's place is here and its person
has not arrived. It advances the clock (rule 2) to the plan's `arrives_at`,
capped at `plans.yaml arrival.wait_cap_hours` (first value 2), then calls
`meet_if_here`.

**`your_place` (minor).** The plan's place counts as "here" in any of the
flat's seven places. The scene always opens in `apt_lounge`: a
`social_go` there, 0 minutes, "you let them in". It never opens in the
lift or reception.

**Reuses.** `plans.arrive`, `scene.open_scene`, and the `scene` verb's
target style (`at_home:<pid>`, `scene.py:849-865`).

**Tests.**
- `test_plan_made_where_you_stand_opens_on_arrival`;
- `test_wait_for_them_opens_the_scene`;
- `test_your_place_opens_in_the_lounge`, from each of the seven places;
- `test_put_down_meets_if_here`.

### W3 — Arrival times, from the right origin (and C1)

**Behaviour.** A plan row gains `due_at` and `arrives_at`.

|---|---|---|
| `now` | `made_at` | `due_at` + travel + lateness (W4) |

cannot open before `due_at`.

**Every site that changes origin (C1):**
1. `plans._make` (`plans.py:459-480`) writes `due_at`, `arrives_at` and,

2. `plans._schedule` (`:483-494`) pushes `plan_lapse` at `arrives_at +

3. `plans._upgrade` (`:497-519`): a `soon` plan becoming `now`/`tonight`

   `plan_lapse` and `plan_arrive`, replacing the row's earlier events.
4. `plans.arrive` (`:622-638`) judges stood-up as `now - arrives_at >
   patience_hours`.
5. `plans.running_late` (`:583-590`) works inside `now <= arrives_at +

6. The `late` op (`:430`) extends `patience_hours` as today. Its window is

ordering and recency. They are unchanged.

### W4 — The person is somewhere

**Behaviour.** A `now`/`tonight` plan not made in person models the
person heading there.

**Travel and lateness, by kind (I3):**
- **A venue:** `places.travel_minutes(venue)`.
- **`your_place`:** `places.travel_minutes(places.home_of(state, pid))`,

- **`their_place`:** they are home already. `arrives_at` is `due_at`, with

- **Lateness:** drawn from `plans.yaml arrival.late_minutes` (first value

- it sets `arrived: true` on the row, through `social_plan` op `arrived`

- if the player is NOT at the place, it queues their `arrived` message: a

It never opens a scene. The scene opens at the next `meet_if_here` call
site (W2).

### W5 — A quiet phone while together

**"Together".** `presence.with_player(state, pid, here_view)` is true for:
- the scene's person;
- an arrived plan person at the player's place;
- a W9 chance person at the player's venue.

The caller passes `here_view`, computed once per pass (I7).

**In two parts (I6):**
1. **At the delivery seam** (`messaging.py:700-760`, `_land_due`), a due
   event from such a person is not landed. It is re-pushed by
   `conversation.yaml together.hold_retry_minutes` (first value 15),
   replacing itself. This covers replies and initiations alike.

### W9 — Chance meetings with the cast

**Who can be where.** `presence.chance_here(state, location)` answers for
venues only: never the flat, the street, a home or the registry office.

  - anyone in a scene.

  At most `scenes.yaml chance.max_candidates` (first value 200), the most
  recently in contact first. The population is never read.
- **When.** `behaviour.status_at(person, now)` is `out` or `free`.
- **Where.** The venues whose hobbies overlap theirs (the overlap
  `places.pick_venue` uses, `places.py:67-85`).
- **The draw.** One counterless `stable_rng(seed,
  "chance:<pid>:<day>:<block>")` per person and block of
  `chance.block_hours` (first value 3).

    `{out: 0.15, free: 0.04}`.
  - From the same draw, it picks which ONE fitting venue.

  So a person is in one place per block, the answer replays, and reading it
  draws and writes nothing.
- **At most `chance.max_here`** (first value 2) are present at once.

**Cost (I7).** The callers are:
- the world turn's narration block;
- every phone payload's `world.here`;
