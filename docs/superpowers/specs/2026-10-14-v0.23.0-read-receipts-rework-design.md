## Rule 12 in this design

This section is the controller's ruling, written down. It overrides every
other line in this file.

1. **The engine has NO concept of physical intimacy, in any form.** No data
   file lists a physical act. No move, gate, refusal, willingness row, depth
   rung, prompt line or test names one. Nothing anywhere says "too soon".
2. **What happens physically, on a date or anywhere else, is the register
   of the storyteller prompt and the owner's model.** It is never engine
   data, never a gate and never a refusal. No scene prompt and no texter
   wording rates it, limits it or tiers it.
3. **The growth ladder is self-disclosure and topics only.** It measures
   what a person will talk about and share, and how open they are: small
   talk, then everyday life, then personal stories, then hopes and fears,
   then family and the future (§6.1). No rung's meaning implies an
   intensity tier.
4. **Life logistics are relationship-stage outcomes.** Staying over, moving
   in and marriage follow from the stage, the evening's circumstances and
   the life-event engine (§6.5, §10.5, §12). None is tied to a depth rung,
   and none is framed as intimacy.
5. **The guard is structural, not a lexicon** (§19.6). Every rung is built
   only from topic kinds in a closed disclosure vocabulary, and the
   validator refuses anything else at load. There is no banned-word list.

## Goal

When v0.23.0 ships, a player who installs READ RECEIPTS can do the following:

1. **Have a real conversation.** They match with someone, the person answers
   straight away, and the two of them talk. Replies come back instantly, and
   they get longer and fuller once a chat is going. The conversation ends
   where it naturally would ("my break's over, text you after work?"), not
   at random.
2. **Keep the conversation going across the whole history.** That holds
   across apps (from Matchstick to Natter), across days and across the time
   they met in person. The person remembers what was said, follows up on
   it ("how did the interview go?"), and keeps what they promised: if they
   said they would message in the morning, they do.
3. **Move time on themselves, and only on purpose.** Time moves when the
   player puts the phone down and does something, or when someone says
   "message me later", "message me at work" or "message me in the
   morning". Nothing else moves it: not waiting for a reply, not closing
   the browser.
4. **Live somewhere.** The flat has a bedroom, lounge, kitchen, dining room,
   a hall with the front door, the lift and the building's reception. The
   player can cook, sleep, get ready, go to work and head out into the city.
5. **Meet people in places, with no set appointment time.** Plans are made
   open-ended: "drinks sometime", "free now?", "come over tonight". The
   meet-up itself is played as a scene at the venue or at someone's home:
   narrated, with choices, and with the person present and talking. The
   world is also typed into, in a scene or out of one: a line the player
   types runs ONE offered choice or scene move, or is narrated only
   (addendum 4 §3, §3.1).
6. **Watch people grow.** Everyone starts on small talk. As they get closer
   they open up, each at their own speed: their day, then their stories,
   then their hopes and fears, then family and the future. What they will
   commit to grows with the relationship: swap numbers, meet, come over,
   move in, marry. Some people never get far. Life happens to them too:
   they change jobs, move house, have a bad month, get a dog.
7. **Make it their own slice of life.** The player can marry someone, settle
   into something long-term, or stay on the apps for good. Randomness, life
   events and the cast's own traits push back on whatever they choose.
   There is no ending.
8. **Swap numbers in a way that feels natural.** Someone who likes the
   player offers their number. An ask that is turned down says why, and
   meeting in person swaps numbers as a matter of course.
9. **See it.** Every room of the flat, the building, the street, every venue
   and the four kinds of home have painted plates in one house style.

## Non-goals

- **Rule 12, first.** See "Rule 12 in this design". Nothing here rates,
  filters, tiers or bounds content.
- **No meeting strangers in person.** Every in-person scene is with someone
  the player already knows from the phone. Meeting someone new at a bar is a
  NOT WIRED row (`engine/social/scene.py`).
- **No children and no moving city.** Kids and long distance are NOT WIRED
  rows. A partner who gets a job elsewhere is a life event (§11) that ends or
  strains the relationship. It never moves the player.
- **No group chats and no friends cast.** The friend contact remains
  NOT WIRED (v0.22 §22).
- **No wall-clock time.** R-03 stands. Within a session nothing moves on the
  wall clock, and the default for away time is now "none" (§3.6).
- **No static portrait pack.** The people are generated from the seed (1,000
  or more a run). A companion's face is their per-person portrait from the
  existing media pipeline (v0.22 §13.4), or the procedural tile when no
  provider is on.

## §1 — What the v0.22.0 code does, and why it plays flat

The owner reported five problems. Each one traces to code, cited below at
v0.22.0 (`60215f4`). The review checked these citations; a few line offsets
are about ten lines out, which changes no conclusion.

### 1.1 "It just jumps hours between messages"

**Every reply is scheduled at a drawn latency, and the only way to receive it
is to jump the clock to it.** Five things combine:

1. A player's send schedules a `read` at
   `now + lognormal(read_minutes)` (`engine/social/messaging.py:546-562`).
   The medians run from 1.5 minutes (banterer) to 45 minutes (slow burner).
   An `asleep` read moves to `next_awake`.
2. The read decides a reply at
   `lognormal(reply_minutes) × status_mult / (interest/50)`
   (`messaging.py:565-570`, `:597-604`). The medians run from 4 to 120
   minutes (`games/read-receipts/data/social/behaviours.yaml`). `status_mult`
   is 2.5 at work and 1.8 when out, and low interest multiplies the delay up
   to 5×.
3. The client sees a reply only after the clock passes its `at`. `wait
   {until: next}` jumps straight there, with a cap of 8 h
   (`engine/social/turn.py:158-174`, `social.wait_next_cap_hours`). "Let
   time pass while I watch" steps 15 minutes after every 30 idle seconds
   (`ui/src/stories/read-receipts/settings/letTimePass.js`).
4. Every send costs 2 in-game minutes (`apps.yaml` `cost_minutes.text`), so
   even a fast exchange moves the clock.
5. Initiations fire at fixed `at_hours` with `prob_per_day`
   (`behaviours.yaml`, `good_morning` at 7, 8 and 9). Together with the
   above, this gives the "three a day" rhythm the owner described.
   Separately, the away advance on resume adds real elapsed time × 1.0, up
   to 72 h (`engine/social/away.py`, `config/default.yaml` `social.away`).
   Coming back to the browser therefore moves the world on too.

### 1.2 "They only replied to the last message, in text-speak"

**The prompt, the decision and the budget all ask for a short line about the
last message.** Ten causes:

| # | Cause | Where |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | The persona tells the model "Most texts are short: one to three lines, and more than one bubble only when this person would send several in a row" | `games/read-receipts/prompts/texter.md`, paragraph 3 |
| 2 | The `reply` situation is "Answer what they last said, the way you text": a literal last-message focus | `texter.md` `## Instructions` |
| 3 | A reply's `max_bubbles` is `min(3, 1 + burst)`, so one player message gets at most 2 bubbles, and the prompt repeats it ("Send at most 2 messages") | `messaging.py:602`, `:633`; `engine/social/voicing.py:646-647` |
| 4 | `llm.profiles.texter.max_tokens: 360` must hold the whole JSON envelope (bubbles, `regard`, `learned`, `shared`, `proposals`), which leaves roughly 150-200 tokens of words | `config/default.yaml:370` |
| 5 | 11 of the 18 weight units of `texting_styles` are `length: short` | `games/read-receipts/data/social/population.yaml:405-415` |
| 6 | Every fallback line, which is the whole experience with no model and the hosted fallback, is 1-6 words ("ha", "yeah fair") | `texter.md` `## Fallbacks` |
| 7 | The voice prompt has a 4096-token window (`social.voice.window_tokens`), minus the profile's `max_tokens`. The budget trims the verbatim tail from 24 bubbles down to 12 (`memory.verbatim_min`) before it touches anything else | `engine/social/memory.py:568-591`; `voicing.py:687-688`; `config/default.yaml` `social.memory` |
| 8 | Older talk survives only as a 180-word thread summary. In hosted mode, and whenever the utility lane is busy, that summary is the deterministic fallback: the first sentence of each message, oldest dropped first | `memory.py:261-284` |
| 9 | Memory is per THREAD. Moving from Matchstick to Natter starts a thread with an empty summary and tail: `snapshot_of` reads `thread.summary` and the thread's own messages | `voicing.py:330-365` |
| 10 | Each reply is voiced as its own due message, framed by "They last wrote 3 hours ago" | `voicing.py::_right_now` |

### 1.3 "No change in character from the original seeding: an eternal first meeting"

**The voice is handed the dating profile on every message, forever.** Five
causes:

1. `_who` (`voicing.py:506-529`) gives every prompt the person's dating
   bio ("Your profile says: …"), their prompt answers and their fixed
   texting style. It does so at day 1 and at day 200 alike.
2. The only lines that change are the stage words, the closeness band and
   the mood (`voicing.py:536-547`).
3. Nothing writes the person themselves.
   - Canon grows only from facts the model chose to share.
   - An arc fires for only a few archetypes (`behaviours.yaml` `arcs:`).
   - Traits, texting style and `intent` are drawn once
     (`engine/social/population.py:382-423`) and never move.
4. The situations re-greet after any gap: `check_in`, `good_morning`,
   `reconnect`.
5. What a person is willing to do is a stage plus a regard gate
   (`relationships.yaml` `stages:`). What they will talk about is not
   modelled at all.

### 1.4 "The phone number doesn't seem to work"

The v0.22.0 ledger found the root cause during its final fix wave, at
ledger line 197:

- The `numbers` gate needed trust 40 and 6 in-game days.
- A real session moves hours, not days, so every ask in a session was
  "not yet".
- `ask_decision`'s `why` was dropped, so the declines read as random.

Wave 2 lowered the gate to warmth 30, trust 23 and 20 exchanges with no
days, and carried the `why`. The gate still needs about 14 player messages,
each one a drawn latency apart (median 96 in-game hours,
`relationships.yaml` header). Under §1.1 that is many jumps of hours.

The per-day regard caps are a second, hidden day gate
(`relationships.yaml` `regard.per_day_gain` {warmth 6, trust 4}). Trust 23
from a start of 15 needs a second in-game day whatever the play (the
header says so). The number is reachable now, but it does not feel
reachable.

### 1.5 "Everything lives on the phone"

The world is one node, `home`, with no edges
(`games/read-receipts/data/world/locations.yaml`, v0.22 §2.5). The engine
refuses the narrated turn for the story outright: `/api/game/choice` and the
socket's `player_choice` answer "Read Receipts is played on the phone"
(`engine/scenes/default_scene.py:582-585`, `:662-666`).

A meet-up is a calendar slot (`relationships.yaml` `meets.slots`: morning
10-12, afternoon 14-16, evening 19-21, 1-7 days ahead). It is then one
SOCIAL_MEET draw and a recap card (`engine/social/meets.py`). The player
never sees the date.

## §2 — The design in one page

The owner's three demands meet in one model. The mechanism is listed
against each:

- **Time is the player's.** The in-game clock moves only by actions
  (`clock.advance_time`, rule 2), and the actions are priced so that real
  play and in-game time keep a stated ratio (§3). In a live conversation,
  replies have **zero in-game latency**. The only wait a player feels is the
  model writing, shown as typing dots. A gap of hours happens only when the
  player puts the phone down and lives, or sleeps, or asks for it.
- **A conversation is a thing the engine knows about.** An **episode**
  (§4) is one burst of talk with one person. It has a length the engine
  drew from the person's character and depth. Their schedule nudges it to a
  close: work, lunch ending, bed, going out. Night owls and people who text
  at work keep going. It ends with a sign-off, often with a **promise**
  (§5) that the engine keeps.
- **People are a growth curve, not a profile.** Each person has a
  **disclosure depth** (0 small talk to 4 family and the future) and an
  **openness** meter that fills at their own **pace** (§6). Depth decides
  what they talk about and share, and who the voice is told they are now,
  in place of the dating bio. What they will commit to (number, meet, come
  over, move in, marry) is the relationship stage's, read from a
  willingness table (§6.5).
- **Memory is per person, across everything.** Episodes (chats AND
  in-person scenes) get recaps. Recaps fold into a story-so-far. Open
  threads hold promises, plans and follow-ups that the engine re-surfaces
  when their time comes (§7).
- **The world is a place you play in.** The flat, the building, the street,
  15 venues, 4 home types and a registry office are locations (§8). With the
  phone down, the player gets the engine's ordinary narrated turn: a plate,
  narration, and choices with intents. A meet-up is a **scene** (§10). The
  storyteller narrates it and voices the companion in person, from the same
  person block the texter reads. The engine resolves every moment.
- **Slice of life.** Long-term stages run past "Together" to living
  together, engagement and marriage (§12). Life events on both sides
  (§11) are deterministic draws that change state, which the model voices.
  Staying single is a stance. The engine never pushes against it.

Everything the model writes is still proposed and clamped (rule 1). Every
write goes through `apply_effect` (rule 3). Every draw comes from a named
stream (rule 4). Every number lives in story data and is measured by the
simulator before it ships (rule 10).

## §3 — Pacing around real play time

### 3.1 The clock

The clock still moves only through `clock.advance_time` from an action
(rule 2). `settings.world.tick_hours: 0.0` stays. A player action is one
of these:

- a phone action (`run_mechanics`, v0.22 §16.1);
- a narrated world turn (§8.3), whose choice may be picked or typed: a
  typed line moves time only through the offered choice it matched or the
  scene move it became, and a line that matched nothing moves none
  (addendum 4 §3.1);
- the phone's `put_down`, a free phone verb that only sets `phone_up`
  false (§8.2).

There is no other mover. Two clocks that moved time in v0.22 are retired
(§3.6):

- the "Let time pass while I watch" auto-wait;
- the default real-time away advance.

### 3.2 The exchange rate: what a real minute is worth

The design targets below are written in REAL minutes of play. Each is
reached by pricing the actions; nothing reads a wall clock.

| Mode | Real time | In-game time | Ratio (in-game per real minute) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Chatting (phone up, an episode live) | One exchange: the player reads and types (~25-40 s), then the model writes (6-30 s, §20.2) | 1 minute per player text, 0 per reply (§3.3) | **about 0.9-2.5 minutes** (derived from the latency, §3.4) |
| Swiping | ~4 s a card (v0.22 T30: p95 32 actions a minute) | 0.2 minutes a swipe (unchanged) | about 3 minutes |
| World turn (phone down) | One choice: read the narration and pick (~30-45 s) | The activity's minutes (§8.4): 10-60 | **about 20-90 minutes** |
| A scene (a date) | One moment a choice; 5-8 moments | 15-35 minutes a moment | A 2-3 h date in about 5-8 real minutes |
| Jumps | Instant | "Until they message", "this evening", sleep until morning, "go to work" | Unbounded, chosen |

**A session**, as a design target, is 30-45 real minutes. It covers about
one in-game evening and the next morning. For example: 15 minutes of
conversations and swiping, one date, home and bed, then a morning message.

A relationship from first match to a first in-person meeting takes 1-3
such sessions for a well-matched, open person, and more for a slow one.

### 3.3 Costs, as data

The new `games/read-receipts/data/social/conversation.yaml` `costs:` block
replaces `apps.yaml` `cost_minutes` for messaging:

- `text: 1`, `voice: 2`, `photo: 1`, `video: 2`;
- `reply: 0`. A person's reply in a live episode lands at the in-game
  minute the player's message was sent.

`swipe: 0.2`, `like: 1`, `edit: 2` and `move: 3` stay in `apps.yaml` (games
and settings). World activity costs live in `data/world/activities.yaml`
(§8.4). A scene moment's cost lives in `scenes.yaml` (§10.3). The clock
targets of `wait` and sleep live in `conversation.yaml` `clock:`:

- `evening: 18`;
- `morning: 7`.

A player's waking band from their creation job shape overrides `morning`
(MINOR 4).

### 3.4 How the simulator measures pacing (rule 10's harness)

`scripts/simulate_social.py` gains a **real-time cost model**, so every row
is reported per REAL hour or per session.

**`REAL_SECONDS`** gives each policy action a cost. The values below are
first estimates from v0.22 T30's measured cadence. They are printed in the
harness header. They are calibrated three times: phone actions in T9's latency probe, world and scene actions in T29's measured pane session, and all of them re-checked live in T31:

| Action | Real seconds |
|---|---|
| Send a text | 30 |
| Read a reply | 3 per bubble |
| Swipe | 4 |
| A world choice | 35 |
| A scene moment | 40 |
| Model latency | 6, 15 or 30 per reply (`--model-seconds`). **Every banded run is made at all three, and the bands hold at 30**, the worst case (I8) |

Rows that follow arithmetically from these assumptions are labelled
**derived** in the report, never banded as measurements. The chat ratio is
one of them.

A **session** in the harness is 40 real minutes and ends with the policy's
"bed". The harness reports in-game minutes per real minute by mode (derived)
and the rows of §17.

### 3.5 Where gaps still come from

These are the only gaps, and each one is chosen or earned:

1. The player puts the phone down and plays in the world, sleeps or goes to
   work (§8).
2. A sign-off with a promise: "text you after work" (§5). The player may
   jump to it with "until <name> messages".
3. The person is unavailable when the player starts a conversation, for
   example asleep, or at work without texting there (§4.2). The message is
   "Delivered", and their read comes at their next available moment. The
   player feels that gap only if they choose to wait for it.
4. Ghosting, left-on-read and non-replies, decided per episode start (§4.6).

### 3.6 Retired movers of time

**"Let time pass while I watch"** goes, with `letTimePass.js`, its Settings
row and `social.settings.auto_wait`. A reply in a live episode needs no time
to pass.

**The away advance** defaults to `social.away.mode: none`
(`config/default.yaml`). A returning player picks up at the same in-game
minute. The lock screen shows what was waiting. The `real` mode and
`engine/social/away.py` stay as a config option.

**`wait {until}`** keeps its verb (`turn.py`) and its `1h` target name
(MINOR 4). Its label becomes "Wait". Its targets are:

| Target | Waits until |
|---|---|
| `next` | Someone writes or a promise falls due; cap 8 h |
| `1h` | One hour has passed |
| `evening` | `clock.evening` |
| `morning` | `clock.morning` |
| `promise:<id>` | That promise's window opens |

The `15m` target goes, because it existed for the auto-wait.

## §4 — Conversations: episodes

New module **`engine/social/convo.py`**. It replaces the reply-latency half
of `messaging.py`.

### 4.1 What an episode is

An episode lives at `state.social["people"][pid]["episode"]` and is one of
two kinds:
- a chat, in any of the person's threads, across apps;
- an in-person scene (§10).

```
{id: "e<n>", kind: chat|scene, thread_id | location, started_at, last_at,
 exchanges, target, energy, mode, end: none|winding|signed_off|lapsed,
 end_reason, promise_id?, topics: [topic ids touched]}
```

A person has one episode at a time. Closed episodes leave state as recaps
(§7).

An episode **opens** when any of these happens:
- the person replies to a cold message (§4.2);
- the player answers a message of theirs;
- an initiation lands (§4.7);
- a scene starts.

An episode **closes** when any of these happens:
- either side signs off (`signed_off`);
- no message either way for `conversation.yaml` `episode_gap_minutes`,
  45 in-game minutes by default. The status is `lapsed`, checked at the
  next clock advance. This applies to chat episodes only;
- the scene ends. A scene episode closes ONLY at its scene's end and never
  lapses, since its moments carry no messages (m9).

### 4.2 Availability: will they answer now?

`convo.available(state, pid, at)` is derived. It draws nothing and writes
nothing (I14). It combines:
- the person's schedule status (`behaviour.status_at`, v0.22 §5.2);
- their **phone habits**, from `conversation.yaml` `habits:` with archetype
  weights:
  - `at_work`: `never | glances | chatty`;
  - `late_night`: `no | sometimes | night_owl`;
  - `in_company`: `rude_not_to | glances`.

**Where the fixed attributes come from (I14).** Habits, like pace, home,
topics and layers (§6, §10.5), are drawn once per person from a counterless
key, `stable_rng(seed, f"{stream}:{pid}:{attr}")`. They never use
`person_rng`, so the draw order cannot matter. They are materialised into
`people[pid]` by a `social_person` effect at first match (rule 3). A read
path that meets an unmaterialised person (the payload, a hint) computes
them from the same key and writes nothing.

| Status | Answers a cold message when |
|---|---|
| free, out | Always available. `out` replies cap at `quick` mode |
| work | `chatty`: available. `glances`: one short exchange, then a sign-off with a promise ("at work, talk at lunch?"). `never`: not available |
| asleep | `night_owl`: their sleep band is shifted late by `habits.night_owl_shift_hours`. `sometimes`: available in their first sleep hour, with mode capped at `quick`. `no`: not available |

**Not available:** the message is delivered and a `read` event is queued
at `convo.next_available(pid, at)`. That is a schedule boundary, not a
lognormal draw. The read opens an episode, so the person's reply, or their
left-on-read, lands then.

**What the player sees.** The thread header shows what the person has told
the player about their days. At depth 1 or more their schedule is known:
"Usually at the hospital till 7". Below depth 1 it shows nothing.

### 4.3 Instant replies, and sends while they type

In an open episode, a player's send runs this chain inside its own
`advance_time`, so it needs no `wait`:

1. `social_message`.
2. A `read` at the send's `at`.
3. The person's reply decision.
4. A due message at the same `at`.

The voicing pass picks it up at once (v0.22 §16.2). The client shows typing
dots for as long as the model takes.

**Sends while they type (I6).** A due message that a voicing pass has
MARKED is never withdrawn or re-decided.
- **A send that arrives while the reply is still unmarked** joins its burst.
  The decision is re-made in place (v0.22 `pending_read`), with no second
  reply.
- **A send that arrives after the mark** is held as "spoken since the
  snapshot". When the pass commits its reply, the commit sees that the
  player spoke after the snapshot. It then queues the NEXT decision at the
  commit's `at`, which answers those sends.

This is the conversational rhythm the owner described: they finish their
message, then answer what you added. No reply is ever discarded, and a fast
typist cannot starve their own reply.

### 4.4 Length and the natural end (the nudge)

At each reply decision, `convo.decide` computes the episode's **end
pressure**. Every term is data in `conversation.yaml` `talk:`.

- **Target length.** `target` exchanges are drawn when the episode opens
  (SOCIAL_CONVO, through `person_rng`, a per-episode draw recorded in the
  episode row) from the archetype's `talk.exchanges` `[low, high]`. The
  draw is scaled three ways:
  - by depth (`talk.per_depth`);
  - by hour (`talk.late_mult`, above 1 for night owls);
  - by status (`talk.work_mult`).
- **Energy.** Energy starts at 100 and loses `talk.energy_per_exchange`
  each exchange.
  - A player message shorter than `talk.short_player_chars` costs extra.
  - A message that names one of their topics or hobbies gives a little
    back. This uses whole-word label matching, as `shared_hobby_mention`
    does.
- **The schedule boundary.** This is the in-game minutes until their status
  next changes, such as work starting, lunch ending or their bed band.
  - Under `talk.boundary_minutes` (20), pressure rises steeply.
  - A night owl's late band is not a boundary. Neither is a `chatty`
    worker's shift start.
- **The result** moves through three steps:
  1. `continue`.
  2. `winding`: the voice is told to start bringing it to a close, and
     why.
  3. `sign_off`: they go, with a reason and maybe a promise (§5.2).

  After `sign_off` the person is **away** until their next available
  moment. `talk.after_sign_off` (0-2) gives the one-more-thing replies they
  will still answer.

The reason is the engine's, taken from their schedule and state. It is never
random text, and the model voices it. Examples:
- "your shift starts at 7";
- "lunch is over";
- "you're up early";
- "you're about to head out with friends";
- "you're winding down for the night".

### 4.5 Modes: fuller replies when a chat is going

`Decision` (`engine/social/kinds.py`) gains `mode: quick | chatting | deep`:

| Mode | When | `max_bubbles` | Bubble cap | Reply cap (`max_tokens`) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `quick` | Episode exchanges under `modes.chatting_after` (3); status `out`; a `glances` worker; a sign-off | 1-2 | 280 | 300 |
| `chatting` | The episode is going: exchanges at or past `chatting_after`, energy above `modes.deep_energy` | 1-4 | 500 | 560 |
| `deep` | `chatting`, plus three more conditions: the player's last messages average over `modes.deep_player_chars`; depth 2 or more; the episode touched a topic at their depth | 1-3 | 700 | 760 |

- `max_bubbles` no longer depends on the burst (§1.2, cause 3). A burst only
  raises the minimum: three player messages get at least 2 bubbles.
- The reply cap goes per call through `backend.chat`'s existing
  `max_tokens` argument (`engine/llm/backend.py:585`). The profile's
  `max_tokens` becomes a ceiling (800).
- The prompt window reserves the MODE's cap, not the profile's (MINOR 8).

On a 4096-context server, modes degrade (§7.4).

### 4.6 Ghosting, left on read, non-replies

v0.22's draw per read (`messaging.py:589-600`) becomes a draw **per episode
start**:

- **Reply:** `reply_prob × interest_factor × depth_factor`.
- **Ghost:** `ghost_per_episode × (1 - interest/100) × stage_factor`.
  - The key is renamed from `ghost_per_exchange`.
  - Its values are re-measured in T29.

Inside an open episode nobody ghosts mid-sentence. A ghoster ghosts
BETWEEN episodes: they sign off normally and never open another.
`left_on_read` stays, shown as "Read".

**Promises are protected (MINOR 11).** No ghost is drawn for a person who
holds an open promise to the player. Ghosting waits until it is kept.

### 4.7 Initiations: nudges, not timetables

`behaviours.yaml` `initiations:` rows lose `at_hours`. Each gains `window:`,
one of `waking | commute | lunch | after_work | evening | late`. Each
person's schedule resolves the window to hours (`conversation.yaml`
`windows:`). The minute is drawn on SOCIAL_CONVO.

- `prob` is per window by depth (`[d0, d1, d2, d3, d4]`).
- An initiation never queues while an episode with that person is open.
- It is skipped if the player started one with them within
  `skip_if_talked_minutes`.
- Promises (§5) and follow-ups (§7.3) are initiations with a reason, and
  they outrank a window's check-in.
- Depth exists from T2, so T3 reads it directly. No depth stub is needed
  (§24).

**v0.22 arcs are kept (MINOR 10).** `behaviours.yaml` `arcs:` and the
`arc_beat` event still fire, and turners still turn. Their `reveal` rows
are voiced through the new `opens_up` situation, beside depth layers (§6.4).

## §5 — Promises and sign-offs

New module **`engine/social/promises.py`**.

### 5.1 A promise

```
state.social["promises"][id] =
{id: "p<n>", pid, by: person|player, window, opens_at, closes_at,
 about (≤100 chars, optional), made_at, status: open|kept|broken|void}
```

The window is one of:
- `lunch`, `after_work`, `tonight`, `morning`, `weekend`;
- `at_work`: whoever the window belongs to is at work (I11);
- `later`;
- `at:<hour>`.

**Who the window belongs to (m4).** The rule is set by the speaker:
- **The SENDER's schedule** resolves every window except `at_work`. The
  sender is whoever will write in the window:
  - "Message me in the morning" uses the person's morning.
  - "I'll message you in the morning" uses the player's morning.
- **`at_work` is the one exception.** It resolves against the RECEIVER's
  work band, meaning whoever is at work:
  - "Message me at work", said by the player, uses the player's work band.
    The person messages into it, whatever their own habits.
  - "I'll message you at work", said by the player, uses the person's work
    band.
  - If the person has no work band, the window resolves to their next free
    band, and the answer says so.

**How each window resolves:**

| Window | Resolves to |
|---|---|
| `morning` | That schedule's waking band |
| `lunch` | Its midday free band |
| `after_work` | The end of its work band |
| `at_work` | The receiver's next work band (see above) |
| `later` | The resolver's next free band starting at least `promises.later_min_hours` (2) ahead (MINOR 4) |

**A window that does not exist** for that person resolves to their next free
band. For example, a nurse on nights has no "after work" tonight. The answer
is worded truthfully: "after my shift, so tomorrow morning".

### 5.2 How promises are made

Rule 1 holds throughout: a promise comes from a structured intent, or from a
bounded proposal the engine validates. There are three routes.

1. **The player's sign-off chips.** This is a new phone verb, `sign_off {pid,
   who: me|you, window}`, with the label "Talk later". It offers:
   - "I'll message you at lunch / after work / tonight / in the morning /
     from work";
   - "Message me at lunch / after work / tonight / in the morning / at
     work".

   The chip sends the player's typed text, or a default line, and records
   the promise.
2. **The person's own sign-off.** A `sign_off` decision (§4.4) carries
   `promise_window` when the archetype's `promise_prob` draw says so.
   - The engine records the promise BEFORE voicing.
   - The voice is told "Say you'll message them after work."
   - `promise_claimed` fails a reply that promises a different window.
   - A breadcrumber makes no promises: "maybe later" is not one.
3. **The model's proposal.** A reply may propose `promise {window, about?}`
   (`relationships.yaml` `proposals:`).
   - It is legal only while `winding` or `sign_off`.
   - It is accepted if the window resolves within
     `promises.max_ahead_hours` (48).
   - This is how a freely typed "message me in the morning" becomes a kept
     promise when a model reads it.

**With no model**, `promises.yaml` `phrases:` matches the player's typed
message, such as "message me in the morning" or "text me at work". It uses
`agreement.py`'s matcher: a whole phrase, case-folded, not in a question.
A match becomes a pending `promise` request, which the person's next
decision accepts as in route 2.

### 5.3 Keeping them

**The person's promises are kept.**
- A `promise_due` initiation is queued in the window, with situation
  `promised_message`.
- It is withdrawn (`void`) only if the person leaves (unmatched, blocked or
  ended) or the relationship fades.
- No ghost is drawn while it is open (§4.6). A holiday life event (§11.3)
  starts only after the person's open promises close.
- No archetype breaks a promise. An archetype that would is one that does
  not make them.

**The player's promises are tracked.**
- **Kept.** A message from the player in any of that person's threads
  inside the window keeps it: status `kept`, trust `promises.kept_trust`
  (+1), and openness `gain.promise_kept`.
- **Broken.** Silence until `closes_at + promises.grace_minutes` breaks it.
  The status becomes `broken`, with trust and openness costs
  (`promises.broken`) and a follow-up row "you said you'd message at lunch".
  The person's next decision carries `broken_promise` to the voice once.

**What the player sees.**
- Promises are listed on the contact card and in Calendar's "Coming up".
- `wait {until: promise:<id>}` jumps to the window, and so does the world's
  "Until <name> messages".
- A promise into the player's work band lands MID-SHIFT, because long
  activities stop at notifications (§8.4).

## §6 — Growth: who people become with you

New module **`engine/social/growth.py`**. Its data lives in `growth.yaml`,
`topics.yaml` and `layers.yaml`.

### 6.1 Disclosure depth and openness

Each person keeps a growth record in `people[pid]["growth"]`:

```
{depth: 0..4, openness: 0..100, pace: float, guarded: 0..n, revealed: [layer ids], peak_depth}
```

**The five rungs are fixed in the engine** (`growth.RUNGS`):

| Depth | id | Disclosure kinds (the closed vocabulary, §6.7) |
|---|---|---|
| 0 | `small_talk` | `places`, `hobbies`, `work_outline`, `the_apps` |
| 1 | `everyday` | `daily_life`, `work`, `friends`, `plans`, `opinions` |
| 2 | `personal_stories` | `upbringing`, `family_outline`, `past`, `travel`, `proud_of` |
| 3 | `hopes_and_fears` | `hopes`, `worries`, `regrets`, `values` |
| 4 | `family_and_future` | `family`, `future_plans`, `home_life`, `money` |

A rung **means** only its kinds. It names what a person will talk about and
share with the player. There is nothing else on a rung: no willingness
column, no commitment and no act (see "Rule 12 in this design").

`growth.yaml` `depths:` rows carry only these keys:
- `id`;
- `kinds` (a subset of the vocabulary);
- `floors` (warmth and trust, §6.4);
- `counts` (play counts, §6.4).

**Any other key is a load error.**

### 6.2 Pace: different speeds for different characters

`pace` is fixed per person by a counterless `stable_rng` key and
materialised at first match (I14). It multiplies three terms:
- the archetype's `growth.pace` (`behaviours.yaml`), for example romantic
  1.4, steady 1.0, slow_burner 0.6, dry_texter 0.5, needy 1.3 and
  breadcrumber 0.8;
- each trait's `growth.trait_mult` (`growth.yaml`), for example "guarded"
  0.7 and "open" 1.3;
- a lognormal jitter, `growth.jitter` (0.2).

Some archetypes **cap** depth (`growth.cap`):
- a breadcrumber never passes 1;
- a dry texter never passes 2 on the phone, but in person
  (`cap_in_person`) may reach 3;
- a ghoster's openness decays twice as fast.

### 6.3 What fills openness

Every gain is measured by the engine. Every number is `growth.yaml`'s.

| Event | Openness |
|---|---|
| An exchange in an open episode | +`gain.exchange` × pace × `gain.mode_mult[mode]` |
| An episode that ends with a sign-off on good terms (warmth moved up across it) | +`gain.good_end` × pace |
| A scene moment that lands (§10) | +`gain.moment[band]` × pace |
| A kept promise (either side) | +`gain.promise_kept` |
| Supporting them through a life event (a reply in the event's window, or a scene) | +`gain.support` |
| A broken promise, left on read past their tolerance, a fight day, a request to share above their depth (a scene `ask_about` deeper than depth) | −`loss.*` |

The model's `regard` feeds openness only through the "good end" term. The
model never writes openness.

### 6.4 Depth transitions, floors and counts

A depth is reached when three conditions hold:
- `openness >= 100`;
- the rung's warmth and trust floors hold;
- the rung's play counts hold.

**No floor or count reads in-game days** (§1.4's lesson). A static test
also covers the regard caps (§19.3, I9).

| Rung | Warmth floor | Trust floor | Counts |
|---|---|---|---|
| `everyday` (1) | 25 | 18 | 1 episode |
| `personal_stories` (2) | 40 | 32 | 3 episodes, 1 scene |
| `hopes_and_fears` (3) | 55 | 48 | 3 scenes |
| `family_and_future` (4) | 68 | 62 | 8 scenes |

These are first values. T5 measures them, the simulator keeps them in their
bands, and the bands themselves are fixed in T29 (rule 10).

`family_and_future` no longer needs a stage. Disclosure is independent of
the relationship's label (Rule 12, §4).

**Regard is capped per EPISODE, not per day (I9).** `relationships.yaml`
`regard.per_day_gain` becomes `regard.per_episode_gain` {warmth 6,
trust 4}. A scene counts as an episode, and its moment bands add their own
exact effects. Three more per-day caps convert the same way:
- the fallback's `per_day` becomes `per_episode` {warmth 3, trust 2};
- `media.yaml` `budget.*.per_day` becomes `per_episode`;
- `relationships.yaml` `asks.per_day` becomes `asks.per_episode`.

Rough reachability at the attentive pace:
- trust from 15 to 62 is about 12 episodes or scenes;
- trust 80 is about 16;
- that is 4-8 sessions for a fast person.

T5's first pass (depth 0→1) and T29's full pass confirm or move these numbers.

**On a transition:**
- depth rises by 1 and openness returns to 0;
- a `social_growth` effect writes a history row ("Opened up about their
  family");
- **a reveal** is queued for the next episode.

The reveal is the person's next **layer**: an authored canon fact for that
rung. It is drawn (counterless key) from `layers.yaml` rows whose `kind` is
one of the rung's kinds and whose `when:` holds, which reads job, flags and
life. For example, "Their dad was a fisherman out of the Quays". The voice
gets it as situation `opens_up`.

**Depth never goes down.** Trust and warmth carry damage. A betrayal
(§12.4) adds `guarded` +1. Each `guarded` halves openness gains for
`growth.guarded_episodes` episodes. That count is in episodes, not days.

### 6.5 Willingness: the relationship's commitments

`growth.yaml` `willing:` maps each **commitment** to its gate. The
commitment ids are a closed engine enum, `growth.COMMITMENTS`:
- `number`, `meet`, `come_over`, `go_to_theirs`, `stay_over`;
- `exclusive`, `meet_family`, `move_in`, `propose`, `stay_or_go`.

Each row's gate may read only these:
- **stage**: a minimum stage by `STAGE_RANKS`;
- **regard floors**: warmth and trust;
- **play counts**: episodes and scenes;
- `needs` (both sides' intent, v0.22 `needs:`);
- **for `number` and `meet` only**, the `everyday` rung (depth ≥ 1). Swapping
  numbers or meeting for coffee follows from talking about one's day. That
  is disclosure, not logistics.

**No life-logistics commitment may read depth.** This covers `stay_over`,
`move_in`, `propose`, `meet_family` and `exclusive` (Rule 12, §4). The
validator refuses a `depth` key on any row but `number` and `meet`, and a
test pins it (§19.6).

**Media asks** keep v0.22's stage unlocks (`media.yaml` `unlock_stage`),
unchanged. They are not on this table.

| Commitment | First values (T5/T29 measure) |
|---|---|
| `number` | depth ≥ 1, warmth 25 |
| `meet` | depth ≥ 1, warmth 30, trust 22 |
| `come_over`, `go_to_theirs` | stage `meeting`, 1 scene |
| `stay_over` | stage `dating`/`fling`, plus the evening (§10.5) |
| `exclusive` | stage `dating`, warmth 70, trust 65, 4 scenes, `needs: {both: long_term}` |
| `meet_family` | stage `exclusive` |
| `move_in` | stage rank ≥ `exclusive` (so also from `engaged` and `married`), 14 days since `exclusive` began, 12 scenes, warmth 78, trust 72, no household yet |
| `propose` | stage `exclusive` or `living_together`, warmth 85, trust 80 |
| `stay_or_go` | §11.3's job-offer answer: stays when warmth ≥ 70 and trust ≥ 65 (MINOR 3) |

**The stage rows** (`relationships.yaml` `stages:`) keep only what each stage
means:
- `talking`: 4 exchanges, automatic;
- `numbers`: trigger `ask`, gate = `willing.number` (MINOR 3);
- `meeting`: the first scene done;
- `dating`, `friends`, `fling`: the branch after `meets_good: 2`;
- `exclusive`: asked and accepted. Its id stays `exclusive` and its words
  become "Together" (MINOR 2).

**Asks.** `relationships.ask_decision` reads the table. Its `why` gains
`not_yet` ("not there yet"), which the voice and the client both receive.

### 6.6 Who they are now: the end of the eternal first meeting

`_who` (`voicing.py`) is replaced by `growth.who_now(state, pid)`, which
gives the voice:
- **Name, age, job, neighbourhood and home**, current after life events
  (§11.3).
- **Traits and texting style.** At depth 2 or more, a `short` texting style
  reads as `medium` in a chatting or deep episode.
- **The dating bio and prompt answers**, at depth 0 only.
- **"Since you met"**: up to `growth.life_lines` lines from their life
  events.
- **"What you talk about with them now"**: their topics at their current
  rung and below.
- **"What you still keep to yourself"**: the next rung's topic labels. With
  this, a person deflects in their own voice.

### 6.7 Topics and the closed vocabulary

`growth.KINDS` is the closed disclosure vocabulary in code: the union of the
§6.1 kinds. `growth.yaml` `kinds:` mirrors it as data. At load, validation
(`growth.check_data`, run from `validate_content.py` and the cache warmer)
refuses each of these:
- a data kind that is not in `growth.KINDS`;
- a rung that lists a kind outside its own §6.1 set;
- a `topics.yaml` or `layers.yaml` row whose `kind` is not in the
  vocabulary;
- any unknown key in a `depths:`, `topics:` or `layers:` row.

`topics.yaml` rows are `{id, kind, label, when?, follows_up?}`. For example,
"their shifts" (`work`) or "their sister" (`family_outline`). Each person
draws `growth.topics_per_kind` topics, using a counterless key. Topics feed:
- the voice (§6.6);
- the scene's `ask_about` and `tell_about` moves (§10.3);
- the energy term (§4.4).

## §7 — Memory: continuity across the whole history

`engine/social/memory.py` is reworked from per-thread to **per person**.

### 7.1 What a person remembers

Kept in `people[pid]["memory"]`:

| Part | What | Bound |
|---|---|---|
| `current` | The open episode's messages across apps, verbatim. For a scene, its moment receipts and the companion's spoken lines | `memory.verbatim` (40 bubbles or lines), budget floor `verbatim_min` (16) |
| `bridge` | The last `memory.bridge_bubbles` (6) of the previous episode, verbatim: "how you left it" | 6 |
| `episodes` | Closed episodes: `{id, kind, where, from_at, to_at, recap (≤ 70 words), topics, end_reason}` | `memory.episodes_kept` (12), the oldest folded into `story` |
| `story` | The relationship so far | `memory.story_words` (300) |
| `open` | Promises (§5), plans (§9) and follow-ups (§7.3), each `{id, kind, about, due_at?, by}` | `memory.open_max` (8) |
| `canon`, `knows`, `told` | Unchanged (v0.22 §12) | 40, 30, contacts |
| `history` | Engine lines | `history_max` 60 |

Thread `summary` and `summary_through` are retired. A thread keeps only its
messages and its state spill (v0.22 §17.2).

**Scene narration is not kept in memory (I16).** A scene's full narration
goes to the archive spill (`phone_archive.jsonl`, a `scene` row per turn).
`memory.current` keeps only the companion's spoken lines, under the same
40-line bound. The recap reads the spill off the lock, as v0.22's summary
does (`memory.with_archive`). Once the recap commits, `current` is cleared.

### 7.2 Recaps

When an episode closes, the voicing pass queues a **recap job**. It is a
utility call on `small` with reasoning off. It is degradable and uses the
new `prompts/episode_recap.md`.
- **A chat recap** works from its messages. It gives 1-3 sentences from the
  person's side: "You talked about…; they told you…; you left it at…".
- **A scene recap** works from its moment receipts and its narration.
- **The deterministic fallback** is built from engine facts: the topics, the
  end reason, the promises, and the band words for a scene. For example:
  "You talked about their shifts and the climbing gym; they had to go to
  sleep; they said they'd message you in the morning."

When `episodes` overflows, the oldest folds into `story` through
`engine/memory/summarizer.py::summarize_with`. Its fallback keeps the newest
sentences.

Recaps and folds commit with their `base`. A stale one is refused, as
v0.22's summaries were.

### 7.3 Follow-ups

A reply may propose `followups: [{about ≤ 100 chars, when: tomorrow|days:<n>|after:<window>}]`.
- There are at most 2 per reply, and the engine bounds them.
- They are recorded as `open` rows with a `due_at`.
- When one is due, the person's next initiation or reply carries it: "They
  had their interview today; ask how it went".
- Life events write follow-ups too (§11).
- With no model, only life-event follow-ups exist.

### 7.4 The voice prompt's window, on any server (I7)

`llm.profiles.texter.min_context` stays **4096**, so a 4096-context model
still binds. The voice window is derived per call:

```
window = min(social.voice.window_tokens, binding.usable_context) − the mode's reply cap
```

`social.voice.window_tokens` is 8192, a ceiling.

**On a 4096-context binding the texter stays usable** and degrades in three
steps:
1. `deep` uses `chatting`'s caps.
2. `episodes` keep 3, and `story` is cut to 120 words before any other trim.
3. `current` keeps its floor of 16.

A test with a 4096 stub binding asserts three things: the prompt fits, a
reply is made, and no part listed below as never-trimmed is cut.

**Trim order**, oldest first within each part:
1. older `episodes` (down to 3);
2. `story` words;
3. `canon`;
4. `history` (down to `history_min`);
5. `current` (down to 16).

`now`, `who_now`, `open` and `bridge` are never trimmed.

### 8.2 Phone up, phone down

There is one state flag, `state.social["phone_up"]`. It is saved and written
by `social_settings`.

- **Any phone action picks the phone up** (MINOR 6). `run_mechanics` sets
  `phone_up` true first, so there is no refused phone verb. Picking up is
  free, except during a scene (§10.3, `in_company`).
- **`put_down`** is a phone verb labelled "Put the phone down". It is free,
  sets `phone_up` false, and the world comes forward. The `wait` verb's old
  "Put the phone down" label goes (§3.6).
- **While `phone_up` is true, world choices are refused** with "Put the
  phone down first." The client greys them out, including in the
  side-by-side layout (§14.1).
- **Episodes lapse on their own.** If nobody writes for
  `episode_gap_minutes` of in-game time, the episode lapses, which in
  practice means the next world activity closes it.

Notifications that land while the phone is down show as a **buzz banner**
in the world view: name and app, never words. The narrator is told only the
count and who ("Your phone buzzes twice: Kai, Mo"), never the content.

### 8.3 The narrated turn, opened for a phone story

`/api/game/choice` and the socket's `player_choice` (`default_scene.py:582`,

`phone_up` is true. The turn is the engine's ordinary `run_turn`, with one addition: a phone
story reads the player's typed line in `_run_world_turn`, after the drain
and under the turn lock, against the engine's own offer
(`engine/social/typed.py`; addendum 4 §3). `resolve_player_intent` is
unchanged and the route carries no intent.
- the storyteller (§14.4);
- `legal_intents`;
- decks for the player's life-event moments (§11.2);
- the outcome-aware evaluator.

There is no `agents.yaml`, as in THE LONG CON (it stays absent).

**Directives (I1).** `games/read-receipts/game.yaml` declares
`settings.governance.directives: [StorytellerMind, SocialWorld]`.
`config/default.yaml`'s list is untouched, so `EvilPhaseTone` and
`DoomSignsInterceptor` never apply to this story.

`SocialWorld` is a new directive in `engine/agents/governance.py`. It

It is a no-op without `paths.social`. Seam tests pin two things: no

### 8.4 Activities

`data/world/activities.yaml` defines the world verb **`do {activity}`**.
Each activity has:

```
id, label, at: [location ids], minutes | until: <clock key or window>,
when?: predicate, effects?: [social effects], interruptible: bool, narrate: <one line>
```

| Activity | Where | Cost |
|---|---|---|
| Make dinner, make breakfast, eat | Kitchen, dining | 20-45 min |
| Shower and get ready | Bedroom | 25 min |
| Tidy up | Lounge, kitchen | 30 min |
| Watch something, read | Lounge, bedroom | 60-120 min (interruptible) |
| Sleep | Bedroom | Until `clock.morning` or the waking band (interruptible) |
| Go to work | Hall | Until the end of the player's work band (interruptible) |
| Collect a parcel | Reception | 10 min |
| Step outside | Street | 5 min |

**Long activities stop at notifications (I11).** An `interruptible: true`
activity advances to its end OR to the first notifying event before it
(`turn._next_notifying_at`), whichever comes first. When it stops early:
- the receipt says "Your phone buzzes: Kai";
- the turn offers "Pick up the phone" and "Carry on" (`do` with the same
  activity, resumed to its original end).

Sleep stops only at a person's promise or a `late_night` person's
message, and only if the player set "wake me for messages" in Settings,
off by default.

**At work, the player stays at work.** Work continues in segments: the
player is "at work" (`state.social["at_work"]` true until the band ends). A
promise into the player's work band therefore lands mid-shift, and the
player may text and then carry on. The player's own `at_work` habit is
always `chatty`.

**What is legal at work (m11).** While `at_work` is true, the world
verbs are limited to two:
- `do carry_on_working`, which resumes to the band's end and stays
  interruptible;
- `do leave_early`, which ends `at_work`. It has no mechanical cost
  beyond the time already spent, and returns the player to `apt_hall`
  through `social_go`.

The phone stays usable. `go` and the other activities come back once
`at_work` is false.

**Rule 6.** Sleep is offered in the bedroom with no gate. The story has no
stamina.

### 8.5 Going out: `go`

`social_go {to}` is a world verb. Its targets depend on where the player
is (N5).

From the flat, the building or the street:
- any venue;
- a home with an open plan or invitation;
- `registry_office` on its life event.

From **every place outside the flat and building**, which means the street,
any venue, any home and the registry office, the target is
`apt_lounge` ("Head home"). It is always offered, while no scene is open,
with no gate (it is the way back, like rule 6's rest). The other `go`
targets are also offered from a venue or the street when no scene is open.

A scene's own way home is its closers (§10.4). Tests:
- `test_every_location_reaches_home`: from each of the 28 locations, with
  no scene open, a legal sequence of `travel`, `go` and `do leave_early`
  reaches `apt_lounge`;
- `test_a_quiet_visit_goes_home`;
- `test_call_it_a_night_goes_home`.

Travel minutes are data (`venues.yaml` and `homes.yaml` `travel_minutes`).
A `social_go` effect writes `location_id` after `clock.advance_time` for the
travel, with `arrest` (`effects.py:1510`) as the precedent.

**At most 8 options (MINOR 5).** The world verb lists eight, in this order:
open plans' destinations, then Home, then venues ranked by the player's
shared hobbies and last visits. The phone's new **Places** app (a phone
verb `go`, from v0.22's separate phone catalogue, with no 8-option cap)
lists every venue. Choosing one there puts the phone down and goes.

**A venue with no plan is a quiet visit, but not an empty one.** There is
no planned scene and no stranger. The venue's staff are there on shift
(`npc_schedules.yaml`, addendum 4 W8), and a live contact may be there by
chance (W9), shown as "Also here" and approachable with "Go over to
{name}". A stranger who is not yet a contact is never there (§21).

## §9 — Plans with no set time

New module **`engine/social/plans.py`**. It replaces the appointment half
of `engine/social/meets.py`: slots, `days_ahead`, `propose_meet {day,
slot}`, the `meet_start` events, the no-show and the morning lapse. The
v0.22 `meet` phone verb (`meets.py:1054`) is removed. Its job is done by
`ask {what: meet}`, the plan sheet, and `go` (MINOR 6).

### 9.1 A plan

```
state.social["plans"][id] =
{id: "m<n>", pid, kind: coffee|drinks|dinner|walk|visit|film|climbing|games|gig|their_place|your_place,
 venue?: location id, when: now|tonight|soon, made_at, patience_hours, by: player|person,
 due_at, arrives_at, arrived,
 status: open|met|lapsed|cancelled|stood_up}
```

`due_at` is when the plan is meant to start (`made_at` for `now`, the start
of the evening span for `tonight`). `arrives_at` is `due_at` plus the
person's travel and a lateness drawn from a counterless
`stable_rng(seed, "plan_arrival:<plan id>")`. `arrived` is set when they
get there. An `in_person` plan (a closer that leaves together) has
`arrives_at = made_at` and `arrived` true: the person travels with the
player.

A plan has no slot and no date.

### 9.2 Making one

A plan can be made three ways.

- **On the phone.** The player sends `ask {what: meet, kind?, venue?,
  when}`. It is answered inside the episode by the `meet` commitment (§6.5)
  and by availability (§4.2):
  - "free now?" needs them available, with `plans.now_min_hours` free in
    their schedule;
  - "tonight" needs their evening free;
  - "soon" needs willingness alone.

  The venue is the player's choice, or the engine picks by shared hobbies
  (v0.22 `meets.pick_venue`, kept).
- **Their ask.** The `ask_meet` proposal, or the engine's `plans.yaml`
  `invite` initiation: "still up for that drink? free tonight?". The player
  answers in a request card.
- **In person.** A scene's closers (§10.4) can make a plan: "same time next
  week?" makes a `soon` plan; `back_to_yours` makes a `now` plan of kind
  `your_place`.

### 9.3 Keeping one

**`now` / `tonight`.** The person heads there and gets there at
`arrives_at`. `plan_arrive` only RECORDS the arrival (`arrived`), and texts
the player "I'm here" only if they are elsewhere. The scene opens when the
player and the arrived person are at the place with the phone down and no
other scene open (`plans.meet_if_here`), called wherever the clock has
stopped: after `go`, `put_down`, "Wait for {name}", the end of a `do`, a
scene's `then: stay` and a world turn. Patience runs from `arrives_at`,
never `made_at`: if the player arrives within `patience_hours` after it,
the scene starts, and after that the person is gone, status `stood_up`, at
the cost of `plans.stood_up`. A "running late!" message within patience
extends it once, by `plans.late_grace_hours`. A reply claiming "I'm here"
passes the agreement check (`arrival_claimed`) only once they have arrived.
"Wait for {name}" (a world `scene` target, `plan:<id>`) moves the clock to
`arrives_at`, capped at `plans.yaml arrival.wait_cap_hours`, and opens the
scene if they are there.

**`soon`.** The plan stays open for `plans.soon_patience_episodes` of the
person's episodes, counted in episodes, not days. After that it lapses
quietly. Either side's ask turns it into a `now` or `tonight` plan.

**Calendar** lists open plans and promises under "Coming up". There is no
slot grid.

**A flake** is drawn when a `now` or `tonight` plan is made (SOCIAL_SCENE).
It shows as their message before the player sets off.

## §10 — Scenes: meet-ups played in person

New module **`engine/social/scene.py`**. Its data lives in `scenes.yaml`.
It replaces `meets.py`'s single quality draw and recap card.

### 10.1 A scene

```
state.social["scene"] = {pid, plan_id, location, started_at, moments: [{move, topic?, band, minutes}],
                         energy, prior, closing}
```

Only one scene is open at a time.

**How a scene opens.** It opens when the player and the person are both at
the plan's location: the player arrives with `go` or waits ("Wait for
{name}"), the person arrives while the player is there (`put_down`, the
end of a `do`, a scene's end, a world turn), the two arrive at the flat
together, or the player approaches someone present by chance (`approach`,
`kind: chance`, addendum 4 W9). While the two are together, a text from the
person does not land (addendum 4 W5): it is held and lands when they part.

**The scene owns the turn.** `intents.scene_owns_turn` gains a social-scene
check, `social.scene_open(state)`. The check is imported only under
`paths.social`. While a scene is open:
- `legal_intents` offers the `scene` verb;
- `go` is offered only through a closer;
- `do` is offered only for `scenes.yaml` shared activities;
- the director deals no deck (N4). `director.ensure_scene`
  (`engine/content/director.py:588-631`) checks `active`, `jobs.active` and
  `encounter.active` today, and gains a fourth check:
  `social.scene_open(state)`.
  - The check runs only when `social.declared()`.
  - The package is imported lazily, so the other stories never import
    `engine.social`'s submodules.
  - The deal waits unspent, as it does for an encounter, and lands on the
    first turn after the scene ends, if its `when:` still holds.
  - Test (T14): `test_no_deck_is_dealt_over_a_social_scene`. An at-home
    scene in the kitchen with the boiler flag set deals nothing, and the
    boiler deck deals on the first turn after the scene ends.
  - A §19.5 pin shows the director is unchanged for every other story.

### 10.2 The prior

The prior is v0.22's quality formula, kept (`relationships.yaml`
`meets.quality`, moved to `scenes.yaml` `prior:`). It sets the starting
`energy` and a bias on every moment. Each moment draws once on
SOCIAL_SCENE.

### 10.3 Moments: the scene verb

`scene {move, topic?}` takes one of the conversational moves in
`scenes.yaml` `moves:`. Each move carries `label`, `minutes`, `fit`
(traits and archetype) and per-band effects. **There is no physical move,
and no move carries a depth floor, a stage floor or a refusal** (see "Rule
12 in this design").

| Move | What it is |
|---|---|
| `ask_about {topic}` | Ask about one of their topics. A topic above their rung is answered by them steering elsewhere: the band is `flat`, and the openness cost is `loss.too_deep`. This is a scored outcome, not a refusal |
| `tell_about {topic}` | Share about yourself: the player's hobbies, job and life events |
| `playful` | Tease, banter, a game |
| `quiet` | Let a silence sit |
| `talk` | Say something that names no topic (a typed line with no match). It replaces `phone` in the eight moves: picking the phone up already scores the `in_company` habit |

**How a moment is scored.** The score adds the prior bias, the move fit,
the topic fit, their mood and the SOCIAL_SCENE draw. It bands as `lands`,
`fine`, `flat` or `misstep`. The band moves warmth, trust and openness
exactly, and spends `minutes` through `clock.advance_time`.

**What the narrator is handed.** The narrator receives the move, the band
and the topic in engine words. Anything else that happens between two
adults on a date is the narration's, in the storyteller prompt's and the
model's register (Rule 12, §2). The evaluator fails prose that narrates a
landing over a misstep. It learns the four bands as receipt outcomes
(`engine/agents` evaluator, §22).

### 10.4 Closers, commitments and the end

**The closers** are:
- `another_round`;
- `go_somewhere_else`;
- `walk_them_home`;
- `back_to_yours` (`come_over`);
- `back_to_theirs` (`go_to_theirs`);
- `stay_the_night` (§10.5);
- `call_it_a_night {promise?: window, plan?: soon, then: home|stay}`. It
  ends the scene. With `then: home` (the default), it also runs `social_go`
  to `apt_lounge` and charges the travel. With `then: stay`, the player
  stays where they are, and "Head home" stays legal there (N5).

**Commitments made in person are structured (I10).** The only ways a scene
makes a promise or a plan are:
- `call_it_a_night`'s optional `promise` and `plan` arguments ("I'll text
  you in the morning", "same time next week");
- the `back_to_*` closers.

The narrator voices the engine row. Any other commitment in the prose fails
the fact checks (§10.6).

In a `chance` scene (a live contact met at a venue) the closers that make a
plan need `willing(meet)`, and the scene's end moves the number swap and
`meeting` only as far as `willing` holds then (addendum 4 C3).

**The person may close too**, for any of these reasons:
- their energy reaches 0;
- their schedule boundary;
- a run of missteps.

**At the end:**
- The moment bands average into the scene's band: `great | good | fine |
  awkward | bad`. `meets_good` and the branch carry over from v0.22.
- The episode closes, and the recap is queued.
- Numbers are swapped (§15).
- The `post_meet` text is queued at their next available window.

### 10.5 Homes, visits and staying over

**Each person has a home.** It is one of four templates, drawn by a
counterless key at first match from `homes.yaml` `weights:` over job, age
and neighbourhood. A "moved house" life event may change it.

**Going to theirs** needs a `their_place` plan or the `back_to_theirs`
closer.

**Your place.** A `your_place` plan, or `back_to_yours`, brings the person
to the flat.

**Staying over is life logistics, decided by the evening (Rule 12, §4).**
`stay_the_night` is offered at a home when two things hold:
- the `stay_over` commitment holds (stage `dating` or `fling` or later,
  §6.5);
- the evening allows it: the scene's band so far is `fine` or better,
  energy is above `scenes.stay.energy_min`, and their schedule's next
  boundary is not an early start within `scenes.stay.early_start_hours`.

The person's answer is decided from their archetype and `intent`, with one
SOCIAL_SCENE draw against `scenes.stay.accept`. A "not tonight" is a plain
answer. It is never a refusal of anything.

Its reason must be a fact that can hold (m5). It comes from two sources
only:
- the scene's energy: "they're shattered";
- the home template's `stay_reasons`: "their housemates have people over".

When neither applies, the answer gives no reason. A schedule reason cannot
be offered, because the offer already requires that there is no early
start.

If accepted:
- the clock moves to the morning;
- a short **morning scene** opens in the kitchen (breakfast, the walk to
  the door);
- a history row records "Stayed over".

Nothing about the night is engine data.

### 10.6 The companion in the narration

The storyteller voices the companion in person. It is handed the
**companion block**, built from the texter's own builders: `who_now`,
`where`, the person's memory parts (budgeted to
`social.world.companion_tokens`, 1200), and the moment receipts.

**Fact checks on the scene's narration (I10).** After the turn, every
texter fact check runs on the narration:
- `profile_contradicted`, `another_person`, `stage_claimed`;
- `promise_claimed`, `plan_claimed`, `commitment_claimed` (§13.4).

On a failure:
- **First failure**: the turn is retried once with the check's correction
  line, through the evaluator's retry path.
- **Second failure**: the engine's receipt lines ship in place of the
  disagreeing prose. These are the moment's engine words, the same path as
  §21's no-model scene. The failure is logged and counted in a metric.

## §12 — Long-term outcomes: the slice of life

### 12.1 Stages, extended

`kinds.STAGES` and `relationships.yaml` `stages:` gain three live stages
after `exclusive` ("Together"):

| Stage | From | Trigger | Gate |
|---|---|---|---|
| `living_together` | `exclusive` | Ask (`move_in`), either side | `willing.move_in` |
| `engaged` | `exclusive`, `living_together` | The player's `propose` (a scene closer and a phone ask) or the person's proposal | `willing.propose` |
| `married` | `engaged` | The `wedding` life event, `wedding_after_days` (14, first value; T29 measures) after engagement | None |

`STAGE_RANKS` gains `living_together` 6, `engaged` 7 and `married` 8 (m6).

**Moving in is not a stage step for a couple already engaged or married
(m6).** It is recorded as `state.social["household"]` (§12.3) at any stage
from `exclusive` up. It is reached through the `move_in` commitment, whose
gate is "stage rank ≥ `exclusive`". So a couple engaged before moving in can
still move in.

The stage `living_together` applies only when the couple moves in from
`exclusive`. At `engaged` or `married` the stage does not change, and the
household does.
A `chance` scene's end is gated by `willing` (addendum 4 W9, C3): the stage
moves only as far as the person would have agreed to at that moment.

**No stage is an ending.** `winding_down`, `ended` and the cold recovery
apply to every live stage.

### 12.2 The wedding

The wedding is a scene at `registry_office` (`scenes.yaml` `wedding:`). It
has three moments and allows only the moves `quiet`, `tell_about` and
`playful`. It cannot go badly.

### 12.3 Living together: the household

`state.social["household"]` holds the partner's pid. At home, the partner is
present according to their schedule. The world turn's companion block
includes them in the player's room. The `scene` verb offers an at-home scene
with no plan and no travel. Couples still text during the day.

Moving in means the partner moves into the player's flat. Moving into
theirs is a NOT WIRED row.

### 12.4 Being single, and being seen

**Staying single is a stance.** The apps never close, and nothing nags.

**Seeing others while together.** Each scene with someone else while the
player is `exclusive` or later draws a discovery on SOCIAL_LIFE
(`life_events.yaml` `seen:`). On discovery:
- trust drops by `seen.trust`;
- `guarded` rises by 1;
- the partner's mood is `hurt` for `seen.mood_days`;
- a history row "They found out".

Trust and warmth then follow the existing `cold:` rule. Winding down comes
from staying cold, and recovery from warming back above `recover_above`
for `recover_days` (v0.22, kept). **That existing rule IS the repair
(MINOR 3).** No separate repair mechanism exists or is claimed.

## §16 — Migration of v0.22.0 saves

**Decision: migrate.** The owner has played v0.22.0 and has saves.

`state.social["schema"]` is a new key: 2 for v0.23.0, absent (meaning 1)
for v0.22. `CURRENT_SAVE_VERSION` is untouched, so no other story's save
moves.

### 16.1 The hook (N8)

**One load path.** Every route that builds a run from a save calls
`engine/persistence/saves.py::SaveStore.load` (`saves.py:808`):
- the local `SessionStore.resume` (`engine/session/store.py:765`);
- the hosted store, which is the same class per owner;
- `_flush_before_load`'s reloads.

`SaveStore.load` runs `migrate()` (the engine chain), then
`GameState.from_dict`. After `from_dict` and before it returns, it calls:

```python
if social.declared():            # lazily imported, as clock.py does
    from engine.social import migrate as social_migrate
    social_migrate.upgrade(state)   # in place; a no-op at schema 2
```

**`engine/social/migrate.py::upgrade(state)`** does the following:
- It applies the table below through `apply_effect`. One `social_migrate`
  effect kind is added to `SOCIAL_EFFECT_KINDS`, so rule 3 holds, and its
  handler is the only writer.
- It sets `schema: 2`.
- It is idempotent.
- A slice it cannot read raises `MigrationError` with the save id. The save
  is refused, logged, and never half-migrated.

**Order.** The upgrade runs before everything that reads the slice:

```
SaveStore.load → migrate() → from_dict → social upgrade
  → SessionStore._build → _away_advance → _resubmit_media
  → _resume_opening (the first payload build) → the voicer's first request
```

**A guard.** `SessionStore._build` asserts `state.social.get("schema") == 2`
for a `paths.social` story. A route that bypassed `SaveStore.load` would
fail loudly at build, not voice a v1 slice.

Runs that do not load a save need no upgrade, because a new run starts at
schema 2:
- `scripts/simulate_social.py` builds fresh runs through
  `SessionStore.create`;
- `scripts/screenshot_runs.py` creates its runs.

## §20 — Hosted mode and the model server

### 20.1 One narration ticket per account: the turnstile (I5, N1-N3)

The supervisor queue answers a second narration acquire from the same
account with `other_window` (`engine/hosting/supervisor/queue.py:30-36`).
Under instant replies, a world turn and a voice pass from ONE player would
collide on that rule.

**The ruling** is a per-session narration turnstile with these properties:
- the world turn goes first;
- a voice pass never blocks on it;
- a waiting reply is re-requested, never dropped;
- no turn waits while holding the turn lock.

**What it is.** `GameSession.narration_turnstile` is a small
`engine/social/turnstile.py::Turnstile`. Internally it is a condition that
guards two fields: `holder` (`world` | `voice` | None) and `world_waiting`
(a count). The condition is held only to read or change those two fields,
never across work.

Only the turnstile's holder may acquire the supervisor's narration ticket,
so one session never makes two acquires. `other_window` keeps its meaning:
a genuinely different connection.

**It exists only for a `paths.social` session (m10).** `GameSession` builds
it lazily, and only when `social.declared()`. For every other story
`run_guarded` takes the exact path it takes today, which §19.5 pins.

**Its place in the lock order (N1).** The turnstile is a NON-leaf in
`engine/locks.py`'s one lock order, placed immediately BEFORE the session
turn lock. It has the same rank in `tests/lock_order.py`'s copy, and it is
registered with `renew_after_fork`. The order is always turnstile, then turn
lock. Nothing takes the turnstile while holding a turn lock.

**The world turn's side.** T11 owns this side, and it is the only change to
`run_guarded`, in `engine/scenes/default_scene.py:325-341`. For a
`paths.social` session the steps are:
1. **Turnstile first.** `turnstile.acquire_world(timeout)` is the only
   blocking wait. It is made BEFORE `session.lock` is tried, so a world turn
   never waits holding the turn lock.
   - It increments `world_waiting`, and then waits until `holder` is None.
   - Because a voice pass holds the turnstile for one model call at most,
     the wait is at most one call: one reply cap of generation, bounded by
     the call's own timeout.
   - The timeout is `hosting.queue_wait_seconds` when hosted, or the
     texter call's timeout locally. Past it, the turn is answered
     `STORYTELLER_BUSY` and the client re-enables. The engine never holds
     silently.
   - While the world turn waits, phone actions run normally. They take the
     turn lock and never the turnstile.
2. **Then the turn lock**, non-blocking, exactly as `run_guarded` does
   today.
3. **Re-check `phone_up` under the lock.** A phone action made during the
   wait raises it, and if it is raised the turn is refused with "Put the
   phone down first." and the turnstile is released.
4. **Drain the inbox (N2).** `voicer.drain_inbox(session)` runs under the
   lock BEFORE the buzz count and the narration block are built, so replies
   parked while the turnstile was held are delivered: committed, pushed as
   `phone_message`, and counted in this turn's buzz.
5. **Admit and run the turn**, as any narrated turn does.
6. **After the turn (N2).** While still under the lock, drain the inbox
   again. Then release the turn lock, then the turnstile. Then call
   `voicer.request(session)`, the same request `run_mechanics`' route makes
   (`engine/social/routes.py:50`). This voices whatever fell due during the
   turn, and re-requests any pass the turn deferred.

**The voice pass's side (N1, N3).** T8 owns this side, in
`engine/social/voicer.py`.
- **It never blocks.** For EACH model call, meaning each person's reply or
  recap, the pass calls `turnstile.try_acquire_voice()`. That succeeds only
  when `holder` is None and `world_waiting` is 0.
  - If it fails, the pass returns `deferred`. That is not an attempt, and
    nothing is marked or snapshotted yet. The scheduler re-queues the
    session with the v0.22 busy-lock backoff (`commit_retry_ms`, then the
    pass's normal re-request), and the world turn's step 6 re-requests it
    sooner.
  - No `VoicingScheduler` thread ever waits on a turnstile, so two players
    in world turns stall nobody else's replies.
- **It holds the turnstile per call.** Each call runs: acquire, the tried
  snapshot (turnstile, then turn lock, the declared order), the supervisor
  ticket, the model call, the tried commit (turnstile, then turn lock),
  release. Between two people's replies in a `max_per_pass` pass, the
  turnstile is free. A waiting world turn takes it there, and the pass's
  next `try_acquire_voice` defers the rest.
- **It re-requests rather than dropping.** A deferral never counts toward
  `max_attempts`. If a pass for an OPEN episode still reaches
  `max_attempts` or its deadline for other reasons (the model failing),
  it commits the templated fallback reply (§13.1). It is never silent.

**Tests**, in `tests/test_social_turnstile.py` (unit) and
`tests/test_social_hosted.py` (hosted):
- `test_world_turn_waits_at_most_one_call`: a world choice during a
  3-person pass waits for one reply only, and the pass's remaining two
  defer and then complete after the turn.
- `test_phone_send_during_world_wait_is_not_refused`: a send made while
  the world turn waits on the turnstile is answered normally. The world
  turn then refuses on the `phone_up` re-check.
- `test_voice_pass_never_blocks_a_scheduler_thread`: two sessions each in
  a world turn, while a third session's pass runs to completion on the same
  two scheduler threads.
- `test_reply_parked_during_world_turn_is_delivered`: the reply is pushed
  and counted in the next world turn's buzz, or after the current one.
- `test_no_other_window_for_one_session`, in both directions (hosted).
- **Lock order:** `tests/lock_order.py` asserts turnstile before turn lock
  on every path, and a test asserts no path takes the turnstile under a
  turn lock.

**Metrics (T28).** The per-account narration share: seconds the turnstile
was held by voice and by world, per minute.

**The rest of hosted mode.** The world turn is otherwise an ordinary
narrated turn. The phone's mechanics stay unadmitted, and
`phone_actions_per_minute: 90` stays. The admin no-play-text test gains
scene narration, recaps and stories.

### 20.2 The texter's latency (I8, MINOR 13)

- **The profile.** `llm.profiles.texter`: `min_context: 4096`,
  `max_tokens: 800` (a ceiling), reasoning off.
- **An early probe (T9).** At the end of Phase A, a latency estimate:
  - the stubs' prompt and reply token counts, per mode;
  - a tokens-per-second figure (m3). MODEL_SERVERS.md records none today,
    so T9 gets one from one of two places:
    - a short live `timings` probe (`@pytest.mark.live`, one request to
      the configured server, run only if `CLOCKWORK_LIVE_LLM` names it);
    - otherwise an ASSUMED 20 tokens/s prompt-eval-weighted figure for a
      4B model on the owner's RTX 2060, stated as assumed in the report;
  - the result recorded with its p50 and p95 per mode.

  T31 re-measures live.
- **Pre-committed levers.** Each is applied in config by the task that
  measures it, with no new design.

  | Measured p95 | Lever |
  |---|---|
  | Over 15 s in `chatting` | `modes.chatting_after` 5, and `quick`'s caps are the default until then |
  | Over 20 s | `window_tokens` 6144 |
  | Over 30 s | `deep` uses `chatting`'s caps, and docs/MODEL_SERVERS.md recommends a smaller `texter` binding |
- **Starvation.** On a server whose reasoning-off patch is untrusted (a
  CLAUDE.md deferred row), `quick`'s 300 cap starves to the fallback more
  often. T31 records the fallback rate per mode.

## Acceptance

v0.23.0 is done when all eleven of these hold.

1. **Live episodes.** A live episode runs 10 or more exchanges with no
   `wait` and ends for a reason taken from the person's schedule.
2. **Promises.**
   - "Message me in the morning", from a chip or typed with a model,
     brings their message in their morning window.
   - "Message me at work" lands mid-shift.
   - A kept player promise gains, and a broken one costs and is mentioned
     once.
3. **Cross-app continuity.** The Natter thread's first prompt carries the
   Matchstick recaps, the bridge and the open threads.
4. **People change.**
   - At depth 1 or more the bio is gone.
   - A depth step reveals a layer.
   - A life event changes `who_now` and writes a follow-up.
5. **Numbers.** Numbers are swapped within one session of talking for
   40-70% of well-matched people, at 30 s latency.
6. **The world plays.**
   - The flat, the building and the street play through narrated turns.
   - A `now` plan becomes a scene with the companion present, and its
     recap reaches their memory.
   - A visit to their home plays through.
   - The owner's playtest replays (addendum 4 §7): a plan made where the
     player stands is kept when they arrive and the scene opens, the row
     never needs a refresh, typed lines run as offered, and held texts
     land after the scene (`tests/test_rr_playtest_replay.py`,
     `ui/tests/rr-playtest.test.jsx`, and the browser-pane walk, §7.5).
   - From every location, "Head home" reaches the flat (§8.5).
   - A world turn and a voice reply from one player never refuse or drop
     each other (§20.1).
7. **Slice of life.** `devoted` reaches `married` within 40 real hours in
   10-30% of runs, and `bachelor` keeps 3-8 relationships active.
8. **Art.** 48 subjects exist on the engine's dayparts. The pack is
   promoted, or deferred by ruling.
9. **Saves.** A v0.22 save loads, migrates, gets its unanswered message
   answered, and plays.
10. **Tests.**
    - The other six stories are byte-identical.
    - The structural rule-12 guard passes.
    - `run_tests.py full` is green, with FLAKY tests listed.
    - `npm test` and the layout check are green.
11. **Docs** move with the change.
