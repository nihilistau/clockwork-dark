## Goal

When v0.26.0 ships:

1. **A negation cancels a claim only inside its own clause** (item 1). In `engine/social/agreement.py`
   the fixed three-word window is gone from all five sites. Each site gets the scope that is right
   for it: the clause prefix for a negation, and the nearest person-word for "who is the subject".
   Long negations ("I really don't think I can see you tonight") stop failing agreement. A negation
   in an earlier clause ("can't do lunch but I'll text you tonight") still fails. Both are pinned by
   committed fixtures, and the change is measured. One known miss (C4) is recorded, not fixed (§9.4).
2. **The cap of eight keeps the best eight** (item 2). Every capped intent verb ranks its rows
   before slicing, but only when they are over the cap. The survivors keep their original order, so
   a list that fits today is byte-identical. `sell` puts hot goods a fence here will take first, then
   any sale the vendor would make, then the dearest. Every other verb has a stated, deterministic
   ranking (§4.3).
3. **`run_tests.py full` takes about half as long on Windows, with the same tests at the same
   lengths** (item 3). A custom xdist scheduler deals the heavy tests one per worker, longest first,
   and never queues a second heavy test behind a first. READ RECEIPTS' per-action recomputation is
   cached where the cache is a pure function of its inputs.
4. **The flagship's ending can tell you one or two things that would have been different**
   (item 4). The engine captures the turn's pre-intent state, forks it at a pivotal turn, plays the
   road not taken and the road taken forward N in-game days with no model, and keeps the difference
   as a receipt. The flagship's epilogue words that receipt through authored templates, with the
   engine's own labels. The fork never touches the live state or its RNG, its cost is bounded by a
   step count, and how pivotal turns are chosen is spelled out (§5.4).
5. **Studio can inspect a run turn by turn and branch a what-if from a past turn** (item 5). A
   timeline lists the turns. Each one shows its Intent, its Receipt, the plan/negotiation diff and
   the evaluator's result. From a turn with a checkpoint you can branch a new save, or run a what-if
   preview on item 4's fork machinery. The original save is never written.
6. **Model compliance is recorded per profile and model, and summarised** (item 6). The existing
   evaluator, voicer and LLM client results are joined to the model and profile that produced them.
   They go to a bounded JSONL with no play text, and `scripts/compliance_report.py` summarises
   compliance per role, profile and model.
7. **Every untouched story is byte-identical, and saves from v0.25.0 load and play** (§9).

Item 7 of the owner's list is already done: the NEON CITY notes are parked (§8).

## Non-goals

These are the owner's exclusions from the review, kept out:
- No LLM "decision model".
- No parallel `agents.yaml` pipeline.
- No naming zones.

This release's own limits:
- **No balance constant moves** (rule 10). Items 1 and 2 change correctness and selection, not a
  number the simulators measure. **No pinned balance file changes value**: READ RECEIPTS'
  `relationships.yaml` gets a comment edit only (§9.4).
- **The cap stays eight.** `_MAX_OPTIONS` is unchanged, and so is the narrator's grammar shape.
- **No replay-forward of narrated turns.** A turn's effects include what the negotiation pipeline
  committed from model-written plans, and those cannot be re-derived without the model. So the
  inspector branches only from **checkpoints** of real pre-intent states (§6.3). It never
  re-simulates a past turn and calls the result that turn.
- **Counterfactuals do not narrate.** No model writes, completes, embellishes or sees a
  counterfactual, and no model-written text reaches one (§5.4, I2). The model is not told about
  them (no prompt changes).
- **Counterfactuals for the flagship only** (owner ruling 2). Phone stories cannot opt in (a fork's
  people would go quiet without the voicer, §5.2 trap 4), stories with legacy event slots cannot
  (trap 2), and HUE & CRY ends through modules, not epilogue cards. NOT WIRED rows (§11).
- **No counterfactual in the endings gallery** (owner ruling 3).
- **No new hosted-metrics kinds and no admin tab.** The compliance JSONL is written in hosted mode
  too (one file per worker process), but `engine/hosting/metrics_schema.py`'s closed schema and the
  admin panel do not change.
- **Studio stays local-only.** Hosted mode refuses it (§6.7 of v0.20.0), and the inspector's routes
  live under Studio. The journal and checkpoints are forced off in hosted mode (§6.2, §6.3).
- **Survival's cut-invariance is not fixed engine-wide.** The fork sidesteps it (§5.2) and does not
  repair it.
- **The overlay lists (v0.25.0 §7.2) are not ranked.** They are uncapped and keep their walk order.
- **No sound, art or story content** beyond the flagship's counterfactual templates (§5.6) and the
  `relationships.yaml` comment (§9.4).

### 1.1 Item 1: the negation and third-party window

`NEGATION_WINDOW = 3` (`engine/social/agreement.py:111`) is the only scope at five sites:

| Site | Function | What it decides | Who reaches it |
|---|---|---|---|
| A | `_negated` (`:470`) | whether a claim phrase is cancelled by a negation before it | every claim check (`_claim`, `_said`, `_plan_claim`, `_step_claim`, `_arrival_claim`, `_promise_hits`'s quotes), `stated`/`stated_promise`, and so **the player's own typed words** too: `promises.phrase_match` (`promises.py:235`) and `plans.on_player_text` (`plans.py:720`) |
| B | `_someone_else` (`:714`) | scene prose: whether a hit's subject is someone other than the companion | `_said`, `_promise_hits`, `_third_party` over prose, and the arrival check over prose |
| C | `_promise_hits` (`:775`) | scene prose: whether a narrated promise is the player's or the companion's | `_scene_promises` |
| D | `_third_party` (`:1013`) | texter replies: whether a third-person word makes a claim phrase someone else's | `_plan_claim` (`:920`, so `plan_claimed`), `_step_claim` (`:1030`, so `commitment_claimed`), and `_someone_elses_arrival` (`:1076`, so `arrival_claimed`) |
| E | `_someone_elses_arrival` (`:1073`) | arrival claims: "my mum just got here" | `_arrival_claim` |

The research corpus (Appendix A) measured the window against the prototype:

| Variant (negation / subject) | Long negations N (14) | Earlier-clause negations F (18) | Third parties S (10) | Scene prose (18) | Existing fixtures (85 bad / 109 good) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| window / window (**v0.24.1**) | **0/14** | **10/18** | **5/10** | **16/18** | 85/85, 109/109 |
| clause_restart / nearest (**prototype**) | **14/14** | **18/18** | **9/10** | **17/18** | 85/85, 109/109 |

So the window was wrong both ways:
- **It gave 14 false positives.** Each one costs a correction retry: a second model call, then a
  dropped bubble or the fallback after two failures (`voicing.py:1477-1488`).
- **It gave 8 false negatives:** F2, F3, F4, F5, F6, F7, F10 and F16. Examples are "can't wait,
  see you tonight" and "Not tonight, see you tomorrow". Each of these is an earlier negation that
  cancelled a later claim.

Two cases stay wrong after the prototype:
- **S2**, "my sister keeps saying see you tomorrow to everyone". Site D does not read "my <noun>" as
  a third party; only site E does. Closed by §3.2's relation list.
- **C4**, "She says she really doesn't think she can see you tomorrow". This one is a **data gap**:
  READ RECEIPTS' `games/read-receipts/data/social/relationships.yaml` `negations` (`:335`) has no
  "doesn't", "cannot", "aren't", "couldn't", "wouldn't" or "shouldn't". **Deferred** (owner ruling
  1, §9.4): a known miss this release.

The idiom exclusions are needed. Without them the plain clause scope misses `stage_claimed.yaml`
bad[0], "can't wait to tell people you're my girlfriend".

`scripts/simulate_social.py` cannot measure this change. Its `StubVoice` writes "sure, sounds
good", and its player texts hold no claim phrase. Before and after gave byte-identical reports:
6,712 checks and 0 failures.

### 1.3 Item 3: the suite's runtime

The v0.24.0 release run on Windows took 93.4 min: parallel 83.0 and serial 10.4. CI's v0.24.1 run
on 2 workers took 99.0 min.

**The cause is scheduling, not length.** The three `tests/test_social_save_size.py` params took
2,072 s, 1,855 s and 675 s. All three ran back to back on ONE xdist worker (gw5, until +83 min).
The other five workers finished everything else by about +20 min.

Why they share a worker, read from the installed pytest-xdist 3.8.0
(`.venv/Lib/site-packages/xdist/scheduler/loadscope.py`, `loadgroup.py`):
- Under `--dist loadgroup`, a test with no `xdist_group` is a work unit of one
  (`LoadGroupScheduling._split_scope` returns the whole node id). The save-size params are in no
  group: their fixture `receipts_active` is function-scoped.
- `schedule()` builds the queue **sorted by the number of tests in each unit, descending**
  (`loadscope.py:368-382`), because `loadscopereorder` defaults to True (`plugin.py:131-142`) and
  `run_tests.py` does not pass `--no-loadscope-reorder`. So every multi-test group is dealt before
  any single heavy test, whatever order collection leaves. Moving heavy tests to the front of the
  collection (the first draft's design) never reaches the queue.
- After the first unit per node, `_reschedule` gives a node another unit whenever it holds two or
  fewer pending tests (`:350-354`). A node running one heavy test holds one pending test, so it is
  always given the next unit, and a unit once given cannot be stolen. A worker that takes a heavy
  test therefore often already holds the next heavy test too.

**The second cause is READ RECEIPTS' per-action recomputation.** cProfile of the save-size harness
at 20 days (indicative shares, which overlap):

| Hotspot | Share |
|---|---|
| `state.to_client_dict` → `payload.phone_block`, built 2.3 times per action | 52% |
| `messaging._offered_asks` → `media.ask_refusal`, about 1 M calls | 20% |
| `population.profile` → `person` → `_player_key` | 10.5% |
| `apps._label_of` (a linear table scan per label, 2.16 M calls) | 10% |
| `agreement._phrases` (tuples rebuilt per call) | 9% |
| `apps.order` (reshuffles the whole day's pool per top-card read) | 8.4% |
| `agreement._another_person` → `name_forms` → `_norm` | 7-8% |

Things that are **not** hot:
- `copy.deepcopy` (2.4% in all; `ScopedTransaction` is not in the top 70);
- YAML loading (0.2 s, once);
- subprocesses (the simulator starts none).

Three of the hotspots are already CLAUDE.md deferred rows (the phone block, the agreement name
forms, the deck reshuffle).

### 1.4 Item 4: forking is already possible

`type(state).from_dict(state.to_save_dict())` is the path every turn already takes
(`StateTransaction`, `engine/game/transaction.py:52-70`). An RNG stream is
`(rng_seed, name, rng_counters[name])` (`engine/game/rng.py:120-131`), so a fork's draws advance
only the fork's counters. `active_engine` is a `ContextVar` (`engine/game/engine.py:362`), so a fork
driven on another thread cannot take over the game thread's engine.

The probe checked four things on every row: the live save dict was unchanged, the live counters were
unchanged, a second fork came out equal, and the fork took 1-7 ms.

| Story | 7 days, 24 h cut | 7 days, 1 h cut | 30 days, 24 h cut |
|---|---|---|---|
| clockwork-dark | 109 ms, ends day **10** | 39 ms, ends day **8** | 11 ms |
| hue-and-cry | 141 ms (agenda +10 draws) | 184 ms | 630 ms |
| wicked-garden / neon-city / read-receipts | 11 / 28 / 14 ms | | |

The probe found two traps:
1. **The cut is not invariant.** The same 7 days ends on day 10 when stepped 24 hours at a time and
   on day 8 when stepped an hour at a time. A 24-hour lump of hunger damage knocks the idle player
   out, and `encounter.check_death` then spends more hours (CLAUDE.md, "Survival's hunger/death is
   not cut-invariant").
2. **The legacy wall-clock tick sits outside `advance_time`.** `WorldSim.on_tick`
   (`world_sim.py:216-277`) draws the caravan, tinker and militia streams on wall-clock tick hours
   that are recorded nowhere.

Nothing in `advance_time`'s passes writes process state. Saves, media and socket emits are reached
only through a session. So a fork that is never handed to a `SessionStore` touches no file, socket
or model.

**What `advance_time` does not run** (`clock.py:166-360` runs doom, beats, clocks, survival,
`npc_sim`, agendas, law, jobs, the social pass, the death check, declared events and thread expiry):
quest evaluation (`default_state._evaluate_quests`, in `run_turn` only), deck dealing
(`director.ensure_scene`), the patrol (`law.patrol`), `authored_choice.resolve` and the negotiation
pipeline. §5.1 rules what a fork runs of these.

**There is no pre-turn snapshot today.** `default_state.run_turn` runs, in order:
`_background_tick` (`:1232`) and gossip into the ledger; `execute_intent` (`:1298`);
`authored_choice.resolve`, `law.patrol` and `director.ensure_scene` (`:1356`); the negotiation
pipeline (its own `StateTransaction`, `pipeline.py:268`); and only then `storyteller.run_turn`.
The storyteller's `StateTransaction` (`storyteller.py:989`) is taken after all of that: its own
comment says the resolved intent is "already applied to the world before this method was entered"
(`:949-953`). So the only snapshot a turn takes holds the road already taken. §5.4 adds the capture
point this design needs.

## §2 — The design in one page

- **Item 1** (§3): one clause scanner shared by all five sites, with two scopes. **Negation** looks
  back to the clause boundary, which is a comma-like mark or a coordinator (`and/but/so/or/because/
  ...`). A subject restart after the negation also cuts it, unless a complement word comes between
  them, and idioms are excluded. **The subject** is the nearest person-word, which carries across
  `and`/`or`. The switches are gone, and the prototype becomes the shipping code. "my <relation>"
  counts as a third party at sites D and E, from a closed relation list. The six missing negations
  are deferred (owner ruling 1).
- **Item 2** (§4): one helper, `_capped(rows)`, over rows that carry their own rank, sits in each
  capped builder over v0.25.0's uncapped row functions **[after v0.25.0, §7.2]**. It ranks only
  over the cap, keeps the survivors' order, and breaks ties by position. A per-verb ranking table,
  and a validator advisory for authored lists that can exceed eight.
- **Item 3** (§10): a heavy-test table in `tests/tiers.py` and a custom xdist scheduler
  (`pytest_xdist_make_scheduler`, a `LoadGroupScheduling` subclass) that deals heavy units longest
  first, one per worker, and never prefetches a second heavy unit behind a first. Then four pure
  caches in READ RECEIPTS, each keyed by its full input. The shared phone block per action comes
  last, and only if its measurement proves it safe.
- **Item 4** (§5): a pre-intent capture point in `run_turn`; `engine/game/fork.py`, which forks
  that capture, applies an intent headless, and steps with a fixed cut, survival held, quests
  evaluated per day and a fork guard. Pivotal turns are chosen by rule and ranked within a day.
  Counterfactual receipts are kept in `state.counterfactuals`, written only through a new
  engine-only effect and only when non-empty. The flagship's epilogue gains a `counterfactuals`
  block worded from authored templates and engine labels, drawn by core's `Ending.jsx`.
- **Item 5** (§6): a per-turn journal (`turns.jsonl`) and flat per-turn checkpoint files
  (`checkpoint-<turn>.json.gz`, state and ledger) with a bounded thinning, both beside the save and
  both local-only. Studio's routes read them. "Branch here" writes a NEW save, and "what-if" runs
  item 4's fork.
- **Item 6** (§7): one `ContextVar` names the role and turn, carried into the pipeline's worker
  threads. The backend writes one `call` record per model call; the storyteller, the voicer and the
  scene write one `turn`/`reply`/`scene` record each. The records go to a rotated JSONL holding only
  names from closed sets, codes and numbers, and `scripts/compliance_report.py` reads them.
- **One minor release** (§13), with a checkpoint after items 1-4 and 6. Item 5 is built last and
  moves to its own minor if C1 finds the branch long (owner ruling 4).

## §3 — Item 1: negation and the subject, scoped to the clause

### 3.1 The scanner

The prototype's switches (`NEGATION_SCOPE`, `SUBJECT_SCOPE`) are removed. Its `clause_restart` and
`nearest` behaviours become the only behaviours. The shipping shape, in `engine/social/agreement.py`:

- `_Clause(str)`: a normalised clause, equal to `_norm(raw)` as a string, that remembers the word
  indices of its comma-like breaks (`[,;:]`, a spaced dash, an en or em dash). `_norm` (`:449`)
  turns every comma and dash into a space, which is why the breaks must be remembered before it
  runs. `_clauses` yields `_Clause`s. `_step_claim` already splits on `,`/`;` before normalising
  (`agreement.py:1027`), so its parts are plain `str` with no breaks, and the same scan applies
  inside each part.
- `_scope(clause, start, stops) -> list[str]`: the words before `start`, back to the nearest break
  or stop word.
- `CLAUSE_BREAK_WORDS = {and, but, so, or, because, cos, cause, coz, although, though, anyway,
  plus}`.
- `SUBJECT_BREAK_WORDS = CLAUSE_BREAK_WORDS - {and, or}`. A subject carries across a coordinator:
  in "her brother laughs and says see you tomorrow" the brother is still the subject.
- `COMPLEMENT_WORDS`: think, sure, know, believe, reckon, expect, guess, suppose, imagine, promise,
  able, going, gonna, want, wanna, mean, plan, planning, say, said, saying, certain, feel, remember,
  if, whether, that, thought, knew, expected, told and tell.
- `FIRST_SECOND`: the I, we and you forms, including `let's` and `u`.
- `NEGATION_IDIOMS = {wait, stop, worry, mind, help, only, me}`, plus the pair "gonna lie". A
  negation directly followed by one of these is not a negation of anything after it ("can't wait",
  "don't worry", "never mind", "not me ...", "not gonna lie", "not only").
- `RELATION_WORDS` (new, I7): the closed list of words that make "my <word>" someone else: mum,
  mom, mam, mother, dad, father, parents, sister, brother, sis, bro, son, daughter, kid, kids,
  friend, friends, mate, mates, flatmate, roommate, housemate, boss, ex, cousin, aunt, uncle, gran,
  grandma, grandad, nan, partner, husband, wife, boyfriend, girlfriend, neighbour, neighbor,
  colleague, coworker. A possessive form ("mum's") is read by its stem.

These are English-grammar constants of the checker, not story data. They live beside

## §4 — Item 2: ranking under the cap

### 4.1 The rule

```python
class OptionRow(NamedTuple):
    target: str
    label: str
    rank: tuple = ()          # lower first; () means "walk position only"

def _capped(rows: Sequence[OptionRow]) -> tuple[tuple[str, str], ...]:
    """At most _MAX_OPTIONS rows. At or under the cap: unchanged, in order. Over it: the best
    _MAX_OPTIONS by (row.rank, walk position), returned in ORIGINAL order."""
```

- **Rank only over the cap.** A list that fits keeps today's output byte for byte. The ranking
  chooses **which** rows survive and never reorders them.
- **Deterministic.** A rank key is built only from state values and authored data: tiers, prices,
  ids and counts, all ints. Ties go to the row's walk position. No RNG and no wall clock are
  involved, and no set or dict iteration leaks into the key.
- **One helper, one signature.** Every capped builder ends `IntentVerb(verb, _capped(rows))`. A
  builder whose verb needs no ranking (§4.3, "walk position") leaves `rank` empty, which reduces to
  today's `rows[:8]`.

### 4.2 The shape it builds on **[after v0.25.0, §6.2 and §7.2]**

v0.25.0 splits `_eat`, `_buy`, `_sell`, `_work` and `_craft` into uncapped row functions
(`_eat_rows(state)` and so on). They yield `(target, label)` in today's walk order. The builder then
takes `rows[:_MAX_OPTIONS]`, and `overlay_targets(state)` reads the row functions uncapped.

v0.26.0 changes that seam in three ways:
- The row functions yield `OptionRow`. `overlay_targets` maps them to `(target, label)` in its one
  comprehension, so the overlay's lists, labels and order are unchanged. This is asserted against
  v0.25.0's own recordings.
- Each builder that v0.25.0 split calls `_capped(rows)` in place of `rows[:_MAX_OPTIONS]`.
- The capped builders v0.25.0 did not split are split now, the same way, so that every capped verb
  passes through `_capped`.

**Assumption.** If v0.25.0 lands with a different row shape (for example a dict, or rows built
inside `overlay_targets`), T2 adapts the carrier and keeps the rule. The design depends only on an
uncapped row list existing in walk order before the cap.

`_sell` loses its `break` at eight in v0.25.0. Its row function walks the whole inventory and quotes
every carried item, which ranked `sell` needs anyway. That cost grows with pack size and is
in-process. T2 measures it on a 40-item pack, and the result goes in the CHANGELOG.

## §5 — Item 4: counterfactual echoes

"Echo" is the feature's name in the roadmap. In code, state, payload, data and docs the thing is
called a **counterfactual** everywhere (I3): `Epilogue.echoes` already names the authored closing
lines (`epilogue.py:80, 96`, drawn by `Ending.jsx`), and one word must not name two things in one
payload object.

### 5.1 The fork machinery (`engine/game/fork.py`, new)

The engine pieces, shared with item 5:
- `PreTurn`: `{state: dict, memory: dict}`, the captured pre-intent state (`to_save_dict()`) and
  ledger (`ledger.to_dict()`), §5.4 step 0.
- `fork(pre: PreTurn) -> Fork`: `type(state).from_dict(pre.state)` (the proven route) and
  `StoryLedger.from_dict(pre.memory)` (`engine/memory/ledger.py:540`), so a fork carries its own
  ledger copy and never reads the live one. The fork gets a **new** `session_id` (`fork-<hex>`), so
  it can never collide with the live session's entry in a `SessionStore`.
- `with forking():`, a `ContextVar` scope (classified in `tests/fixtures/module_state.yaml` as a
  per-context value, never a module bool). It does three things:
  1. **Guards.** These raise `ForkEscaped` if reached: `SessionStore` (registering a fork's state),
     `default_state._autosave`, `SaveStore.save`, `engine/social/saves.py:151` `mark_dirty`, media
     job submission, the scene's socket emit, and the `engine/llm` entry points (`backend.chat`,
     `chat_stream`, `chat_probe`). A fork that reaches any of them is a bug, and the guard turns it
     into a failed test, never a silent write.
  2. **Survival held.** Inside `clock.advance_time` itself, before `survival.tick`
     (`clock.py:205-207`) and before `encounter.check_death` (`:318`), one `ContextVar` read skips
     both for a forked state. This is the hot path of every story, so outside `forking()` the
     check is a single `ContextVar.get` that returns the default, and every story's turns are
     byte-identical (asserted by the goldens, §9.3's harness and `tests/test_fork.py`). The world
     still moves: clocks, doom, agendas, law, jobs, events, schedules, threads and `npc_sim`.
  3. **No unseeded dice.** A `dice` roll with no stream raises in a fork instead of falling back to
     `_UNSEEDED` (`dice.py:80-86`).
- `apply_intent(fork, intent) -> list[receipt]`: resolves one intent headless, through
  `tool_dispatcher.execute_intent` on a `GameEngine(fork.state)`, the way `scripts/simulate.py`
  builds its engine. **It is the intent alone** (I4): no `authored_choice.resolve` (only an
  opening's choice carries one, and the opening is never a candidate), no patrol, no deck dealing,
  no narration and no negotiation pipeline (there is no model, so NPC plans are not projected). The
  receipt says so (`projection: engine`).
- `step(fork, days) -> None`: calls `clock.advance_time(fork.state, cut)` in fixed steps of
  `cut = clamp(world.tick_hours, 1, 24)`, and 1 when the story declares `tick_hours: 0.0` or none
  (M5: The Wicked Garden, Dev Story and READ RECEIPTS declare 0.0, which the first draft divided
  by). Each time a step crosses an in-game midnight, it runs `QuestEngine.evaluate(fork.state,
  fork.ledger)` (`quests.py:1351`) once, so stages complete in a projection as they would in play.
  It never calls `WorldSim.on_tick` (trap 2), so no wall-clock value is read or written. It does
  **not** deal decks (`director.ensure_scene`) or run the patrol: a `forces_scene` beat or clock
  sets its flag in the fork but no card is dealt or played (NOT WIRED, §11).
- `diff(a, b) -> list[Delta]`: compares two forks stepped the same way, over a closed set of signals
  (§5.3).

The fork runs under the active story. That activation is process-global, and a fork for a save of
another story is refused with a reason (it matters only for Studio, §6.4).

### 5.2 The traps, ruled

| Trap | Ruling |
|---|---|
| 1. Survival is not cut-invariant | Fixed cut and survival held in the fork (§5.1). Both forks of a comparison are stepped identically, so they are directly comparable. The idle player neither starves nor eats, and the counterfactual measures **the world**. Nothing in the live game changes, and the engine-wide deferred row stays. Holding survival does **not** make the rest of the world cut-invariant: v0.25.0 records two cut-dependent paths (a fire-once `when:` event inside a multi-day step, and the rumour tier read at a step's end), so the cut is fixed per story, never tuned. |
| 2. Wall-clock tick events are not replayable | The fork never runs `on_tick`. **[after v0.25.0, §6.1]** The flagship's caravan, tinker and press are probabilistic declared events rolled per in-game day inside `advance_time`, on their own `event.<id>` streams, so the fork sees them. The Wicked Garden and Dev Story still use the three legacy slots, which only `on_tick` rolls, so a fork of theirs would never see those events. The opt-in validator refuses a story whose `schedules.yaml` configures a legacy slot (§5.6). |
| 3. Module state | Under `forking()` the guards raise on every session-reached side effect. `tests/test_fork.py` drives each guard. |
| 4. The phone | `social.events.advance` would fill the fork's `state.social` with due messages that no voicer answers. Phone stories may not opt in. Studio's what-if for READ RECEIPTS (§6.4) steps the world with the phone silent and says so. |
| 5. Unseeded dice | They raise in a fork (§5.1). |
| 6. The background tick | The pre-intent capture follows `_background_tick`, which reads real time. A projection therefore starts from the world as the player chose in it, with that turn's wall-clock tick already applied. It is deterministic given the capture; a test that replays a run pins the tick (§5.5). |

### 5.3 What a counterfactual compares

Two forks of the **pre-intent capture** are compared. Fork A applies the intent the player took,
and fork B applies the road not taken. Both are stepped N days with the same cut, and `diff(A, B)`
reads a closed set of signals, each with a player-facing name the engine already owns:

| Signal | Read from | Named by |
|---|---|---|
| `ending_closer` / `ending_further` | `endings.eligible(fork.state, ledger=fork.ledger).scores` (read-only; **not** `recompute`, which writes two tracks through `apply_effect`, `endings.py:624-636`); only for an ending whose tier is `unlocked` or `locked` in **fork A** (the live road) | the ending's `title` |
| `phase` | `evil_phase` | the phase's display name |
| `standing` | a faction's band (`factions.yaml` `bands`) | the faction name and band label |
| `moved` | `state.moved` entries present in one fork and not the other | the entry's own engine-written text |
| `thread` | a thread struck, sealed, broken or expired in one fork only | the thread template's text |
| `clock` | a clock beat fired in one fork only | the beat's authored text |
| `law` | a wanted band or custody in one fork only | the Law's band label |
| `stage` | a quest stage completed in one fork only (§5.1's per-day evaluation) | the stage's authored `complete_text` or `objective` |

A score that reads the ledger (`endings._score`, `:409`) reads the fork's own ledger copy, so no
opt-in needs refusing for it.

The divergence score is a weighted count:
- `ending_closer`/`ending_further`: 5 each;
- `phase`: 4;
- `thread`: 3;
- `stage`: 3;
- `standing`: 2;
- `clock`: 2;
- `law`: 2;
- `moved`: 1 each, at most 3.

These weights are **first values**. They decide which counterfactuals are kept, never a game
outcome, so rule 10 does not bind them. T6 still reports their distribution over the doors
harness's seeds.

No signal ever names an ending that fork A does not already show. So a counterfactual cannot reveal
a door that v0.25.0's "ways it ends" overlay hides (v0.25.0 §8.4).

### 5.4 Choosing pivotal turns

**0. Capture (C1).** In `default_state.run_turn`, after `_background_tick` and gossip and before
`execute_intent`, the turn takes `pre_turn = PreTurn(state.to_save_dict(), session.ledger.to_dict())`
— **only when something will read it**: the story has opted in (§5.6) and the turn's intent is a
candidate shape (an intent was chosen and the previous payload offered another intent-carrying
choice), or the turn writes a checkpoint (§6.3: the journal is on, the store keeps files, and the
turn is a checkpoint turn). Otherwise nothing is captured, and the turn is unchanged. This is a new
serialisation, not a reuse of the storyteller's `StateTransaction` (which holds the road already
taken, §1.4). It is handed to the counterfactual pass and to `_autosave`.

**1. A turn is a candidate when all of these hold:**
- the story has opted in (§5.6);
- the turn's chosen option carried an intent that **resolved** (`intent_resolved` true);
- the previous payload (`session.last_turn["choices"]`, read at the turn's start before it is
  replaced) offered **at least one other option with an intent**, and that intent is legal on a
  fork of the capture (`legal_intents`); an alternative that has gone illegal since is dropped;
- and at least one of these is true:
  - **(a) Authored:** the chosen card beat, or a quest stage completed this turn, carries
    `pivotal: true`. This is a new optional key. On a deck beat, `deck.bound_beats`
    (`engine/content/deck.py:205`) keeps only `id`, `text`, `label`, `gate` and `band` (plus `when`
    after v0.25.0 E5), so it is extended to keep `pivotal` **only when authored**, which leaves
    every shipped bound beat byte-identical (v0.25.0 E5's own argument; no shipped beat carries the
    key). A quest stage is read as a raw dict (`stage.get(...)`, `quests.py`), so `pivotal` needs
    no loader change there; the validator checks it is a boolean in both places.
  - **(b) A door:** the chosen beat is a beat of a door deck (it carries `ending_lock`), or the
    turn moved an ending's tier.
  - **(c) A structural move:** this turn's receipts include a thread struck or broken, an arc
    unlocked, a faction band crossed, or the doom phase changed.

**2. Bounded, ranked within a day (I1):**
- A **door** candidate (b) is always computed. A run deals few door beats (one per arming, v0.25.0
  §4), so this is bounded by the story's doors.
- Of the other two, at most **one** is computed per in-game day, and (a) outranks (c): a (c)
  candidate is computed only if the day has had no counterfactual computed yet; an (a) candidate is
  computed if the day has had none, or only a (c). So an evening's authored choice is never lost to
  a morning's thread strike.
- The day and class last computed are persisted in the state (`counterfactuals.day`, `.class`, below),
  so a resume cannot re-open a day.
- At most one computed counterfactual per turn.

**3. Computed:** for a candidate, the engine forks the capture. It takes the alternatives as **at
most two** other offered intents, in offered order, and builds three forks: taken, alt 1 and alt 2.
Each is stepped `horizon_days` days (default 7). The counterfactual for the candidate is the
alternative with the highest score against the taken fork. Ties go to the earlier alternative. A
score of 0 keeps nothing.

**4. Kept, through one writer (I3):** a new engine-only effect type, `counterfactual`, registered
with `register_effect_kind` (`effects.py:247`) and added to `ENGINE_ONLY_EFFECTS` (`:212`), so no
story file and no model plan can name it. Its two ops:
- `keep`: insert a receipt into `state.counterfactuals.kept`, which holds the **two**
  highest-scoring receipts of the run. Ties go to the earlier turn, and a new one replaces the
  lowest only if it scores strictly higher.
- `mark_day`: record `{day, class}` of the last computed counterfactual.

`GameState.counterfactuals: dict` is a new field, `{kept: [...], day: int, class: "a"|"b"|"c"}`.
`to_save_dict` pops it when empty, as it does for `social` (`state.py:497-500`). `to_client_dict` is
an allowlist (`state.py:509`), so it never reaches the payload.

The receipt is engine data only:

```yaml
{turn: 142, day: 19, horizon_days: 7, projection: engine,
 taken: {action: card, target: "the_count_kept/K1/keep_it", label: "Keep the count"},
 not_taken: {action: card, target: "the_count_kept/K1/let_it_go", label: "Let it go"},
 score: 9,
 deltas: [{signal: ending_closer, id: the_long_witness, taken: 0.6, not_taken: 0.2},
          {signal: phase, taken: stirring, not_taken: spreading}]}
```

**Labels are the engine's, never the narrator's (I2).** The offered choices' labels are
model-written (`merge_choices(agreed, storyteller_result.choices)`, `default_state.py:1541`), so
they never enter a receipt. A receipt's `label` is `intents.player_label(fork.state, action, target)`
(`intents.py:1060`) where that is non-empty, else the label of that `(action, target)` row in
`legal_intents(fork.state)` — the engine's catalogue label, built from data. Both are read on a fork
of the capture. The offered label is kept only in the local journal (§6.2).

### 5.5 Cost, bounded by steps, measured in time

The bound is a **step count, never wall time**. A wall-clock cutoff would make whether a
counterfactual exists depend on the machine, and so would break replay.

- Per candidate: three forks × `horizon_days` × 24 / `cut` `advance_time` steps, plus one quest
  evaluation per fork per day. For the flagship (`tick_hours: 2.0`, `config/default.yaml:856`) at
  the default 7 days: 3 × 84 = **252 steps** and 21 evaluations. A story at a 1-hour cut would pay
  504 steps.
- Per day: one non-door candidate, plus door candidates (§5.4).
- `counterfactuals.horizon_days` is clamped to `[1, 14]` and `max_alternatives` to `[1, 2]`, by the
  loader.
- The capture: one `to_save_dict()` and one `ledger.to_dict()` on a turn that needs it. On the
  flagship this is small; T6 measures it.

From the probe, the expected time is 3 × 39-109 ms plus 3 × 1-7 ms to fork, which is about
0.15-0.35 s per candidate on the flagship. T6 measures it over the doors harness's seeds and
records the median and p95 in the CHANGELOG.

`tests/test_fork_cost.py` (`slow`) asserts the step count exactly (252 for a flagship candidate),
which is deterministic everywhere, and holds the flagship's p95 under 750 ms **on the owner's
workstation only**: with `CI` set it reports the time and asserts nothing about it, the pattern of
`tests/test_social_turn_cost.py:168-171` (M10).

**When it runs (I1):** under the turn lock, after `_evaluate_quests` (`default_state.py:1528`) and
before the payload is built. So the turn's counterfactual is in the state the payload's epilogue
reads: a door's own counterfactual reaches the epilogue rendered on the turn the door locks
(`epilogue_module.for_state`, `:1613`), and the autosave writes it. **The choices wait for it**: on
a candidate turn, the streamed prose has arrived and the `turn_update` with the choices follows the
pass, about 0.15-0.35 s later, at most once per in-game day beside the doors. In hosted mode it
holds the worker's GIL for that time. Both are recorded in docs/HOSTING.md and the CHANGELOG.

**Determinism (M16).** Given a capture, a counterfactual is a pure function of it. A run's
counterfactuals are reproducible from its seed and choices only with the wall-clock tick pinned:
the tests patch `WorldSim.realtime_tick_hours` (or set `last_sim_tick_at`), as the jobs replay test
drives `_background_tick`.

### 5.6 Opting in, and the epilogue

The opt-in is a story's epilogue index (`data/epilogues/epilogue_index.yaml` or the file its
`paths` names):

```yaml
counterfactuals:
  horizon_days: 7
  max_alternatives: 2
  max_shown: 2                    # 1 or 2
  text:                           # authored templates; {fields} are engine-substituted
    ending_closer: "Had you chosen to {not_taken}, {ending} would have stood nearer by day {day_end}."
    phase: "Had you chosen to {not_taken}, the count would have reached {phase} by day {day_end}."
    standing: "Had you chosen to {not_taken}, {faction} would have held you {band}."
    moved: "Had you chosen to {not_taken}: {text}"
    thread: "..."
    stage: "..."
    clock: "..."
    law: "..."
```

- No `counterfactuals:` block means no capture for counterfactuals, no candidate, no fork, no
  `state.counterfactuals` and no payload key. That is byte-identical (§9.2).
- The validator checks the block in four ways:
  - every template's fields are known;
  - the clamps hold;
  - the story declares no legacy event slot (§5.2, trap 2);
  - the story has no `paths.social`.
- `Epilogue` gains `counterfactuals: list[str]`. `render` fills it from
  `state.counterfactuals.kept`, at most `max_shown`, highest score first. Each line takes the
  template for the receipt's strongest delta; `{not_taken}` is the receipt's engine label (§5.4).
  `to_dict` writes the key **only when the list is non-empty**. The story places it with
  `render_order` (it is not in `DEFAULT_ORDER`, `epilogue.py:61`).
- The words are the template's, the labels the engine's, and the facts the receipt's. **No model
  writes or sees them, and no model-written string reaches them.**

**The client (I10).** Core's `ui/src/core/screens/Ending.jsx` (`:30-87`) draws `title`, `card_m`,
`card_g`, `echoes`, `time_line` and `gallery_unlock`, and returns `null` for any other key in
`order`. T6 adds a generic `counterfactuals` case (a list of lines, the `echoes` case's markup
without a speaker) and the same block to the flagship's ending slot **[after v0.25.0, §8.6]**, with
a vitest each (`ui/tests/ending-counterfactuals.test.jsx`), and rebuilds the committed `dist`.
`npm run layout-check --prefix ui` covers the ending screen only where it already does.

**Who opts in (owner ruling 2): the flagship only [after v0.25.0].** Its events are day rolls and
its doors are cards, so a projection sees what matters to it. Its templates are a story content
edit in `games/clockwork-dark/` (its epilogue index, CHANGELOG and README). HUE & CRY renders its
endings through modules, not epilogue cards; The Wicked Garden waits for its slots to be converted
(v0.27.0); READ RECEIPTS has no ending.

**The endings gallery (owner ruling 3): no.** v0.25.0's "ways it ends" overlay is unchanged, and no
payload key or route carries a counterfactual to it.

## §6 — Item 5: the replay inspector and branch scrubber

It ships after items 1-4 and 6 (§14), and moves to its own minor if C1 finds the branch long
(owner ruling 4). It shares item 4's capture and fork, and needs item 6's model context for the
journal's `model` field.

### 6.1 What it shows

A Studio tab, **Runs**:
1. **A list of saves** for the active story.
2. **A timeline** of a chosen save's turns: day, hour, place, the choice label, and a marker for
   each of refused, evaluator flag, retry, fallback, pivotal and checkpoint.
3. **A turn panel** for the clicked turn:
   - **Intent**: action, target and label, with `intent_resolved`.
   - **Receipt**: `tool_receipts`, through the receipts summariser's lines, with raw JSON one click
     away.
   - **Plan/negotiation diff**: per agent, the plans proposed, accepted, refused and vetoed, drawn
     as a diff (proposed → committed). Private motives are already excluded by
     `PipelineResult.to_dict` and stay excluded.
   - **Evaluator**: the scorecard, `passed`, `notes` and `flag`; governance rule ids; `retries` and
     `finish_reason`; the model and profile.
   - **The narration**: joined from `transcript.jsonl` by turn number.
   - **Branch**: from a turn with a checkpoint (§6.3), either "Branch here as a new save" or
     "What-if: take <another offered choice>".

### 6.2 The turn journal (`turns.jsonl`)

`default_state._autosave` already appends `transcript.jsonl`. Beside it, it now appends one
`turns.jsonl` row per **narrated** turn (`engine/persistence/saves.py`, `append_turn`):

```
{turn, day, hour, location_id,
 offered: [{label, intent}],          # the choices the player chose from (the previous payload's)
 chosen: {label, intent},  intent_resolved,
 tool_receipts, evaluation, negotiation, governance, quest_events,
 finish_reason, llm_unavailable, retries, model, profile,
 tick_hours,                          # the background tick applied before this turn
 rng_counters_before,                 # the counters at the capture (pre-intent)
 checkpoint: true|false, pivotal: true|false, counterfactual: {score} | null}
```

**Where it hooks in (M12).** `_autosave` also serves phone saves (`record=False`) and phone actions
(`entry=`). So `_autosave` gains one keyword, `turn: Optional[TurnRecord] = None`, and only
`run_turn`'s narrated path passes it. A `TurnRecord` carries the payload's receipts, the offered
and chosen choices, `retries` (from `StorytellerTurnResult.retries`), `model`/`profile` (from item
6's context, §7.2), `tick_hours` and the pre-intent capture. They travel **beside** the payload, not
in it; the payload is unchanged (§9.2). A phone save or phone action passes none and writes exactly
what it does today.

**Only on a store that keeps files (I13).** `scripts/simulate_social.py`'s `_KeepsNothing`
(`:582-594`) has only `save` and `append_transcript`, and `_autosave` catches only `OSError` and
`ValueError` (`default_state.py:1122`). So `_autosave` writes the journal and checkpoints only when
`not engine.social.saves.keeps_nothing(session)` and the store is a `SaveStore`; a harness store
skips them, as `mark_dirty` already does. The capture (§5.4 step 0) is skipped there too, so the
harness, the `slow` simulator pins and `tests/test_social_turn_cost.py` (bound 400 ms,
`MEDIAN_BOUND_MS`, `:44`) do not pay it.

- `tick_hours` is `_background_tick`'s return value, which is discarded today. It is kept as a
  record only and replays nothing.
- No narration goes in this file; the transcript holds it. Offered labels are model-written and are
  kept here only, locally.
- **Bounded.** At `replay.journal.max_bytes` (default 8 MiB) the file rotates to `turns.jsonl.1`,
  keeping `replay.journal.keep` (default 2) rotated files. The rotation reuses the audit log's
  scheme (`engine/hosting/audit.py:263`'s `rotated_path` logic moves to `engine/persistence/jsonl.py`
  so both use it).
- **Local only.** `replay.journal.enabled` defaults to true. Hosted mode forces it off, and with it
  the checkpoints (§6.3). Studio is refused there anyway, and per-account files would be disk spent
  for no reader.
- **Phone stories.** One row per narrated world turn. Phone actions are already in
  `phone_archive.jsonl` and are not journalled again.

### 6.3 Checkpoints

A branch needs the exact pre-intent state and ledger: the capture (§5.4 step 0). A checkpoint is
that capture, gzipped. **Checkpoints are flat files beside `save.json`** (C3):
`checkpoint-<turn>.json.gz`, one JSON object `{"v": 1, "turn": n, "state": {...}, "memory": {...}}`,
written with `gzip.GzipFile(..., mtime=0)` so equal content gives equal bytes (I6). No subfolder is
made, so `SaveStore.delete` (`saves.py:895-903`, which unlinks each child and then removes the
folder) deletes them unchanged, and so do `reindex` and the hosted purge (`shutil.rmtree` of the
account folder, `engine/hosting/accounts.py:922`).

The thinning is bounded:
- **every** turn for the last 50 turns;
- every 10th turn back to turn `n − 500`;
- every 50th turn before that;
- plus turn 1;
- plus every pivotal turn (§5.4);
- capped at `replay.checkpoints.max_files` (default 200), dropping the oldest of the thinned set
  first.

Thinning runs at write time, and only on files the policy no longer keeps. It deletes inside the
save's own folder only, one file at a time, by an exact computed path
(`<folder>/checkpoint-<turn>.json.gz` for a turn the policy dropped); it never globs for deletion
and never removes the folder.

**Forced off in hosted mode**, with the journal (§6.2).

**Cost, recorded by T8.** Each checkpoint turn pays the capture (one `to_save_dict()` and one
`ledger.to_dict()`) plus gzip and a write. Since every one of the last 50 turns is kept, in local
play that is every narrated turn.
- Flagship-sized state: measured, budget 5 ms median.
- READ RECEIPTS' day-60 state (`to_save_dict` alone 13 ms, CLAUDE.md's whole-state serialisation
  row; `save.json` about 600-860 KB): its world turns only. T8 measures the capture, gzip time and
  size on a real `SaveStore`, against `tests/test_social_turn_cost.py`'s 400 ms median bound
  measured the same way (the test itself uses the harness store and does not pay it, §6.2). If the
  added median exceeds 15 ms, the phone story's checkpoint is every 5th world turn
  (`replay.checkpoints.every_phone`), and the capture is skipped on the others.

Saves are never rewritten, `save.json` is unchanged, and deleting a save deletes its checkpoints
(T8 asserts it, §6.7).

### 6.4 Branching, read-only against the original

- **Branch here as a new save** (I5). Read `checkpoint-<n>.json.gz`; write a **new** save through
  `SaveStore.save(state, memory=checkpoint.memory)` with a new `save_id` and a new `session_id`, so
  the branch has the ledger as it stood at turn n, not today's. Copy the original's
  `transcript.jsonl` and `turns.jsonl` rows with `turn < n` into the new folder (one streamed pass
  each, rotated journal files included), so its timeline shows its history. Checkpoints are not
  copied; the branch starts its own. Write a `branched_from: {save_id, turn}` row into its index
  entry. The original folder is opened read-only. `tests/test_replay_branch.py` asserts every file
  in it is byte- and mtime-identical after a branch. The new save is an ordinary save: it plays
  forward with the live model (or a stub) when the player resumes it.
  - **Its first resumed turn earns `tick_max_hours`** (`world_sim.py:100-124`): the branch keeps the
    checkpoint's `last_sim_tick_at`, as any resume does. Stated, not changed.
  - **READ RECEIPTS: refused** with "Branching a phone story is not wired". Its state references
    archived thread history by spill offset in `phone_archive.jsonl`, and its media live in the
    phone media folder; a branch would need a byte-exact archive prefix and the media copied (NOT
    WIRED, §11).
- **What-if.** For the chosen turn's checkpoint and one of its `offered` intents, run item 4's
  `fork` → `apply_intent` → `step` against a fork of the **taken** intent, with the horizon picked
  in the panel (1-14 days). Show `diff` as the deltas (§5.3). Nothing is saved.
  - It works for any story, opted in to counterfactuals or not, because it is a tool and not a
    payload.
  - A story with legacy event slots gets a banner: "the caravan, tinker and press slots are not
    projected (wall-clock)".
  - READ RECEIPTS gets a banner: "the phone is silent in a projection".
  - Every story gets the line "decks, the patrol and NPC plans are not projected".
- **Story activation.** A branch or what-if for a save of a story that is not active is refused
  with "Activate <story> first". Activation is process-global.

## §7 — Item 6: the per-model compliance report

Built on what exists: `EvaluationResult.to_dict()`, `StorytellerTurnResult.retries`,
`LMSResponse`'s fields, the voicer's `failures`, `check_scene_prose`'s result and the backend's
probe and starvation outcomes. **No new evaluator, no new check, no new heuristic, no new
subsystem.** The new parts are one `ContextVar`, one writer and one script.

### 7.1 The context (`engine/telemetry/compliance.py`, beside the oracle)

`compliance_context(role=..., story=..., turn=...)` is a context manager over a `ContextVar`. The
roles are:
- `storyteller` (the narrated turn);
- `assistant` (the companion's turn, `session.assistant.run_turn`, `default_state.py:1517`);
- `character` (`character.run_turn`, `:1513`);
- `planner` (an agent's plan in the negotiation pipeline);
- `texter` (a voiced reply);
- `scene` (scene prose);
- `world` (READ RECEIPTS' world narration);
- `summarizer`;
- `draft` (Studio drafting);
- `probe`;
- `unknown`: a call made outside any context is written with this role, never dropped.

It is entered:
- by `default_state.run_turn` around the storyteller, the character and the assistant, each with
  its own role;
- by `pipeline._gather` around each plan call. Its `ThreadPoolExecutor` workers get no copy of the
  caller's `ContextVar`s (`tests/fixtures/module_state.yaml`,
  `engine.agents.pipeline._gather#ThreadPoolExecutor`), so the context is carried in the way
  `carrying_held_lane` (`engine/llm/gate.py:723`) carries the lane: a wrapper reads the caller's
  context value and sets it inside the worker, with role `planner` (I9);
- by `voicer.run_pass` around each reply. `run_pass` runs inside the worker thread, so it enters
  the context there; nothing is copied;
- by `scene.py` around its draft;
- by the summarizer;
- by the probe.

**The session** is recorded as a 12-character hash of the session id under a salt, never the save
id or the account. The salt is a per-data-root file, `<data_root>/telemetry/compliance.salt`, 32
random bytes made once on first use (a double-checked lazy getter under its own leaf lock). It is
classified in `tests/fixtures/module_state.yaml` (locked). Hosted workers share the data root and
so share the salt: one session hashes alike across a worker restart, and across workers (M14).

### 7.2 The records

One JSONL file, `<data_root>/telemetry/compliance.jsonl`. In hosted mode it is one file per
process, `compliance-<process>.jsonl`, with the worker's slug or `frontdoor`, so no two processes
append to one file. Rotation follows §6.2's scheme (`telemetry.compliance.max_bytes` default 16 MiB,
`keep` default 4). The writer holds a new **leaf** lock, placed in `engine/locks.py`'s order and in
`tests/lock_order.py`'s copy, and registered with `renew_after_fork`. Every record has `v: 1`,
`ts`, `kind`, `role`, `story`, `session` (hash), `turn`, `provider`, `model` and `profile`.

**Every string field is from a closed set or reduced (I8):**
- `model`: the final path component of the server's id (split on `/` and `\`), capped at 80
  characters, so a llama-server file path never writes a home directory.
- `finish_reason`: mapped to `stop`, `length`, `tool_calls`, `content_filter`, `none` or `other`.
- `conformed_away`: a count, plus the dropped top-level keys that are in a closed set (the turn
  schema's known keys, the parser's `_PARSE_DEFAULTS` keys and `tool_calls`); any other key counts
  as `other` and its name is never written (a model can write `{"Maya smiles at you": ...}`,
  `storyteller.py:367-390`).
- Check names and rule codes come from the engine's own tables.

### 10.1 A: a heavy-first scheduler (the largest cut)

A unit's weight is `HEAVY_UNITS.get(scope, 0)` plus the sum of `HEAVY_UNITS.get(nodeid)` over its
tests (each node id stripped of xdist's `@group` suffix, `remote.py:241-250`). A fixture key matches

its component (`tier_plugin.py:302-340`), so a fixture key that a merge renames is caught by the
check below. The table carries only a weight: it changes no tier, timeout or selection.

**The scheduler: `HeavyFirstScheduling(LoadGroupScheduling)`**, in `tests/tier_plugin.py`, returned
by an `@pytest.hookimpl(optionalhook=True)` implementation of `pytest_xdist_make_scheduler`
(`xdist/newhooks.py:96`, `firstresult`). xdist's own implementation is `trylast`
(`dsession.py:108-127`), so ours is asked first. It returns the subclass only when `dist ==
"loadgroup"` and `HEAVY_UNITS` is non-empty, else `None` (xdist's default). `tier_plugin` is
registered by the conftest's `pytest_addoption` (`tests/conftest.py:798-806`), which runs in the
controller too. It overrides two methods of `LoadScopeScheduling` (3.8.0):
- **`schedule()`**: the same steps as the base (`loadscope.py:330-402`), but the queue is built as
  the heavy units by weight descending (ties by scope string), then the rest in the base's order
  (size descending under `loadscopereorder`). The initial loop then deals the heaviest W units to
  the W nodes, one each.
- **`_assign_work_unit(node)`**: when the node already holds a heavy unit with a pending test, it
  takes the **first non-heavy** unit in the queue instead of the head; otherwise it takes the head.
  So no node ever holds two pending heavy units, a free node takes the longest heavy unit left
  (list scheduling, longest first), and the base's "two pending tests" prefetch (`_reschedule`)
  continues to keep each node fed with light work behind its heavy one. `remove_node`'s crash path
  re-queues work through the same method, so it keeps the rule.

The order and the dealing are deterministic given the collection, which every worker sends equal
(`_check_nodes_have_same_collection`). A bare or serial `pytest`, and any `--dist` other than
`loadgroup`, are untouched.

**Guards.**
- `tests/test_heavy_scheduler.py` (new, a **scheduler-level unit test**): fake nodes (the shape
  xdist's own tests use: `gateway.id`, `send_runtest_some`, `shutdown`, `shutting_down`), a fake
  config, and a synthetic collection of light tests, groups and sixteen heavy units with §10.1's
  weights. It asserts:
  - the first W heavy units land on W different nodes, heaviest first, for W = 2 and 6;
  - the heaviest two never share a node;
  - no node ever holds two pending heavy units, across a simulated completion order;
  - a node that finishes its heavy unit takes the longest heavy unit left;
  - every test is sent exactly once, including after a simulated node crash;
  - with `dist` other than `loadgroup`, the hook returns `None`.
- `tests/test_suite_tiers.py` gains: every `HEAVY_UNITS` key names a file that exists and a test or
  fixture function defined in it (read by AST, no collection), and every weight is a positive int.
- `scripts/run_tests.py` gains an **advisory** in its summary: a test over 120 s in the run's
  `--durations` output whose unit is not in `HEAVY_UNITS` is listed as "add to HEAVY_UNITS". It
  reads only what `--durations` prints, so `full` raises its count from 25 to 50
  (`run_tests.py:213`, `:466`); anything slower than the 50th is not seen, and the advisory says so
  (M3). It is a printed line, not a failure, because timings move with the machine.
- T3 confirms each fixture key against a real `loadgroup` collection once (the group names
