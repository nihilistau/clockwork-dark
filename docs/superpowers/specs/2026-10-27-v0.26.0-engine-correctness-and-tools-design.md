## Goal

When v0.26.0 ships:

1. **A negation cancels a claim only inside its own clause** (item 1). In `engine/social/agreement.py`
   the fixed three-word window is gone from all five sites. Each site gets the scope that is right
   for it: the clause prefix for a negation, and the nearest person-word for "who is the subject".
   Long negations ("I really don't think I can see you tonight") stop failing agreement. A negation
   in an earlier clause ("can't do lunch but I'll text you tonight") still fails. Both are pinned by
   fixtures, and the change is measured.
2. **The cap of eight keeps the best eight** (item 2). Every capped intent verb ranks its rows
   before slicing, but only when they are over the cap. The survivors keep their original order, so
   a list that fits today is byte-identical. `sell` puts hot goods a fence here will take first, then
   any sale the vendor would make, then the dearest. Every other verb has a stated, deterministic
   ranking (§4.3).
3. **`run_tests.py full` takes about half as long on Windows, with the same tests at the same
   lengths** (item 3). The heavy tests start first, one per worker, longest first. READ RECEIPTS'
   per-action recomputation is cached where the cache is a pure function of its inputs.
4. **An ending can tell you one or two things that would have been different** (item 4). The engine
   forks a `GameState` at a pivotal turn, plays the road not taken and the road taken forward N
   in-game days with no model, and keeps the difference as a receipt. An opted-in story's epilogue
   words that receipt through authored templates. The fork never touches the live state or its RNG,
   its cost is bounded by a step count, and how pivotal turns are chosen is spelled out (§5.4).
5. **Studio can inspect a run turn by turn and branch a what-if from a past turn** (item 5). A
   timeline lists the turns. Each one shows its Intent, its Receipt, the plan/negotiation diff and
   the evaluator's result. From a turn with a checkpoint you can branch a new save, or run a what-if
   preview on item 4's fork machinery. The original save is never written.
6. **Model compliance is recorded per profile and model, and summarised** (item 6). The existing
   evaluator, voicer and LLM client results are joined to the model and profile that produced them.
   They go to a bounded JSONL with no play text, and `scripts/compliance_report.py` summarises
   compliance per role, profile and model.
7. **Every untouched story is byte-identical, and saves from v0.25.0 load and play** (§9).

Item 7 of the owner's list is already done: the NEON CITY notes are parked (§8).

## Non-goals

These are the owner's exclusions from the review, kept out:
- No LLM "decision model".
- No parallel `agents.yaml` pipeline.
- No naming zones.

This release's own limits:
- **No balance constant moves** (rule 10). Items 1 and 2 change correctness and selection, not a
  number the simulators measure. §9.4 lists the one data edit that touches a pinned balance file,
  which is an owner question.
- **The cap stays eight.** `_MAX_OPTIONS` is unchanged, and so is the narrator's grammar shape.
- **No replay-forward of narrated turns.** A turn's effects include what the negotiation pipeline
  committed from model-written plans, and those cannot be re-derived without the model. So the
  inspector branches only from **checkpoints** of real pre-turn states (§6.3). It never re-simulates
  a past turn and calls the result that turn.
- **Echoes do not narrate.** No model writes, completes or embellishes a counterfactual. The model
  is not told about echoes either (no prompt changes).
- **No echoes for phone stories in this release.** A fork's people would go quiet without the
  voicer (§5.2, trap 4). READ RECEIPTS has no ending and no epilogue anyway. A NOT WIRED row is
  added (§11).
- **No new hosted-metrics kinds and no admin tab.** The compliance JSONL is written in hosted mode
  too (one file per worker process), but `engine/hosting/metrics_schema.py`'s closed schema and the
  admin panel do not change.
- **Studio stays local-only.** Hosted mode refuses it (§6.7 of v0.20.0), and the inspector's routes
  live under Studio.
- **Survival's cut-invariance is not fixed engine-wide.** The fork sidesteps it (§5.2) and does not
  repair it.
- **The overlay lists (v0.25.0 §7.2) are not ranked.** They are uncapped and keep their walk order.
- **No sound, art or story content beyond §9.4.**

### 1.1 Item 1: the negation and third-party window

`NEGATION_WINDOW = 3` (`engine/social/agreement.py:111`) is the only scope at five sites:

| Site | Function | What it decides | Who reaches it |
|---|---|---|---|
| A | `_negated` (`:470`) | whether a claim phrase is cancelled by a negation before it | every claim check (`_claim`, `_said`, `_plan_claim`, `_step_claim`, `_arrival_claim`, `_promise_hits`'s quotes), `stated`/`stated_promise`, and so **the player's own typed words** too: `promises.phrase_match` (`promises.py:235`) and `plans.on_player_text` (`plans.py:720`) |
| B | `_someone_else` (`:714`) | scene prose: whether a hit's subject is someone other than the companion | `_said`, `_promise_hits`, `_third_party` over prose, and the arrival check over prose |
| C | `_promise_hits` (`:775`) | scene prose: whether a narrated promise is the player's or the companion's | `_scene_promises` |
| D | `_third_party` (`:1013`) | texter replies: whether a third-person word makes a `meet_claims`/`commitment` phrase someone else's | `_step_claim`, `_commitment_claimed` |
| E | `_someone_elses_arrival` (`:1073`) | arrival claims: "my mum just got here" | `_arrival_claim` |

The research corpus (`tests/test_zz_negation_probe.py` in the worktree) measured the window
against the prototype:

| Variant (negation / subject) | Long negations N (14) | Earlier-clause negations F (18) | Third parties S (10) | Scene prose (18) | Existing fixtures (85 bad / 109 good) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| window / window (**v0.24.1**) | **0/14** | **10/18** | **5/10** | **16/18** | 85/85, 109/109 |
| clause_restart / nearest (**prototype**) | **14/14** | **18/18** | **9/10** | **17/18** | 85/85, 109/109 |

So the window was wrong both ways:
- **It gave 14 false positives.** Each one costs a correction retry: a second model call, then a
  dropped bubble or the fallback after two failures (`voicing.py:1477-1488`).
- **It gave 8 false negatives:** F2, F3, F4, F5, F6, F7, F10 and F16. Examples are "can't wait,
  see you tonight" and "Not tonight, see you tomorrow". Each of these is an earlier negation that
  cancelled a later claim.

Two cases stay wrong after the prototype:
- **S2**, "my sister keeps saying see you tomorrow". Site D does not read "my <noun>" as a third
  party; only site E does.
- **C4**, "She says she really doesn't think she can see you tomorrow". This one is a **data gap**:
  READ RECEIPTS' `relationships.yaml` `negations` has no "doesn't", "cannot", "aren't", "couldn't",
  "wouldn't" or "shouldn't".

The idiom exclusions are needed. Without them the plain clause scope misses `stage_claimed.yaml`
bad[0], "can't wait to tell people you're my girlfriend".

`scripts/simulate_social.py` cannot measure this change. Its `StubVoice` writes "sure, sounds
good", and its player texts hold no claim phrase. Before and after gave byte-identical reports:
6,712 checks and 0 failures.

### 1.2 Item 2: the cap

`_MAX_OPTIONS = 8` (`engine/game/intents.py:80`). Only `lift` is ranked on purpose
(`thievery.marks` puts the scheduled cast first, `tests/test_thievery.py:236`). Every other capped
builder slices its walk order: `_eat`, `_check`, `_buy`, `_flag`, `_challenge`, `_set_piece`,
`_work`, `_forage`, `_craft`, `_case`, `_burgle`, `_flashback`, `_job_verbs`, `_guise`, `_sell` (a
`break` at eight), `_bargain`, `_discharge` and `_card`.

Where the cap bites in the shipped stories (measured, 999 gold):

| Story | Place | Rows | Hidden today |
|---|---|---|---|
| clockwork-dark | `edgewood_square` (Odran) | 12 | tinderbox, travel_pack, whetstone, wool_cloak |
| clockwork-dark | `tinker_caravan` (Ilya) | 12 | snare_wire, sympathy_charm, tinker_kit, tinker_map |

**The key measurement is this.** Ranking only when a list is over the cap, and keeping the

Re-ranking **every** list would move two recordings:
- `golden_lmstudio/{shipped,legacy}/02_turn_streamed_hue_and_cry.requests.json` (Dock Mag's three
  rows);
- the `check` enums in scenarios 01, 03, 04 and 16, with the matching `tests/fixtures/local_mode/`
  payloads.

That is why the design ranks only over the cap.

### 1.3 Item 3: the suite's runtime

The v0.24.0 release run on Windows took 93.4 min: parallel 83.0 and serial 10.4. CI's v0.24.1 run
on 2 workers took 99.0 min.

**The cause is scheduling, not length.** The three `tests/test_social_save_size.py` params took
2,072 s, 1,855 s and 675 s. All three ran back to back on ONE xdist worker (gw5, until +83 min).
The other five workers finished everything else by about +20 min. The params are in no xdist group.

xdist's `LoadScopeScheduling` (`--dist loadgroup`) hands units out round-robin in collection order,
and each worker prefetches more units. So a worker that takes the first heavy test already holds
the next two, and no other worker can steal them.

**The second cause is READ RECEIPTS' per-action recomputation.** cProfile of the save-size harness
at 20 days (indicative shares, which overlap):

| Hotspot | Share |
|---|---|
| `state.to_client_dict` → `payload.phone_block`, built 2.3 times per action | 52% |
| `messaging._offered_asks` → `media.ask_refusal`, about 1 M calls | 20% |
| `population.profile` → `person` → `_player_key` | 10.5% |
| `apps._label_of` (a linear table scan per label, 2.16 M calls) | 10% |
| `agreement._phrases` (tuples rebuilt per call) | 9% |
| `apps.order` (reshuffles the whole day's pool per top-card read) | 8.4% |
| `agreement._another_person` → `name_forms` → `_norm` | 7-8% |

Things that are **not** hot:
- `copy.deepcopy` (2.4% in all; `ScopedTransaction` is not in the top 70);
- YAML loading (0.2 s, once);
- subprocesses (the simulator starts none).

Three of the hotspots are already CLAUDE.md deferred rows (the phone block, the agreement name
forms, the deck reshuffle).

### 1.4 Item 4: forking is already possible

`type(state).from_dict(state.to_save_dict())` is the path every turn already takes
(`StateTransaction`, `engine/game/transaction.py:52-70`). An RNG stream is
`(rng_seed, name, rng_counters[name])` (`engine/game/rng.py:120-131`), so a fork's draws advance
only the fork's counters.

The probe (`tests/test_zz_fork_probe.py`) checked four things on every row: the live save dict was
unchanged, the live counters were unchanged, a second fork came out equal, and the fork took 1-7 ms.

| Story | 7 days, 24 h cut | 7 days, 1 h cut | 30 days, 24 h cut |
|---|---|---|---|
| clockwork-dark | 109 ms, ends day **10** | 39 ms, ends day **8** | 11 ms |
| hue-and-cry | 141 ms (agenda +10 draws) | 184 ms | 630 ms |
| wicked-garden / neon-city / read-receipts | 11 / 28 / 14 ms | | |

The probe found two traps:
1. **The cut is not invariant.** The same 7 days ends on day 10 when stepped 24 hours at a time and
   on day 8 when stepped an hour at a time. A 24-hour lump of hunger damage knocks the idle player
   out, and `encounter.check_death` then spends more hours (CLAUDE.md, "Survival's hunger/death is
   not cut-invariant").
2. **The legacy wall-clock tick sits outside `advance_time`.** `WorldSim.on_tick`
   (`world_sim.py:216-277`) draws the caravan, tinker and militia streams on wall-clock tick hours
   that are recorded nowhere.

Nothing in `advance_time`'s passes writes process state. Saves, media and socket emits are reached
only through a session. So a fork that is never handed to a `SessionStore` touches no file, socket
or model.

## §2 — The design in one page

- **Item 1** (§3): one clause scanner shared by all five sites, with two scopes. **Negation** looks
  back to the clause boundary, which is a comma-like mark or a coordinator (`and/but/so/or/because/
  ...`). A subject restart after the negation also cuts it, unless a complement word comes between
  them, and idioms are excluded. **The subject** is the nearest person-word, which carries across
  `and`/`or`. The switches are gone, and the prototype becomes the shipping code. There are two
  follow-ups: "my <noun>" counts as a third party at site D, and the six missing negations are
  added to the data (an owner question, §9.4).
- **Item 2** (§4): one helper, `_capped(rows, rank)`, sits in each capped builder over v0.25.0's
  uncapped row functions **[after v0.25.0, §7.2]**. It ranks only over the cap, keeps the
  survivors' order, and breaks ties by position. A per-verb ranking table, and a validator advisory
  for authored lists that can exceed eight.
- **Item 3** (§10): heavy tests first, one per worker,
  longest first, through a `heavy(minutes=)` marker read in `tests/tier_plugin.py`. Then four pure
  caches in READ RECEIPTS, each keyed by its full input. The shared phone block per action comes
  last, and only if its measurement proves it safe.
- **Item 4** (§5): `engine/game/fork.py`, which forks, applies an intent headless, and steps with a
  fixed cut, survival held, and a fork guard. Pivotal turns are chosen by rule. Echo receipts are
  kept in `state.echoes`, which is written only when non-empty. An opted-in story's epilogue gains a
  `counterfactuals` section worded from authored templates.
- **Item 5** (§6): a per-turn journal (`turns.jsonl`) and per-turn checkpoints with a bounded
  thinning, both beside the save and both local-only. Studio's routes read them. "Branch here"
  writes a NEW save, and "what-if" runs item 4's fork.
- **Item 6** (§7): one `ContextVar` names the role and turn. The backend writes one `call` record
  per model call; the storyteller, the voicer and the scene write one `turn`/`reply`/`scene` record
  each. The records go to a rotated JSONL with no play text, and `scripts/compliance_report.py`
  reads them.
- **One minor release** (§13), with a checkpoint after items 1-4 and 6. Item 5 is built last.

## §3 — Item 1: negation and the subject, scoped to the clause

### 3.1 The scanner

The prototype's switches (`NEGATION_SCOPE`, `SUBJECT_SCOPE`) are removed. Its `clause_restart` and
`nearest` behaviours become the only behaviours. The shipping shape, in `engine/social/agreement.py`:

- `_Clause(str)`: a normalised clause, equal to `_norm(raw)` as a string, that remembers the word
  indices of its comma-like breaks (`[,;:]`, a spaced dash, an en or em dash). `_clauses` yields
  `_Clause`s. `_step_claim` already splits on `,`/`;` before normalising (`agreement.py:1027`), so
  its parts are plain `str` with no breaks, and the same scan applies inside each part.
- `_scope(clause, start, stops) -> list[str]`: the words before `start`, back to the nearest break
  or stop word.
- `CLAUSE_BREAK_WORDS = {and, but, so, or, because, cos, cause, coz, although, though, anyway,
  plus}`.
- `SUBJECT_BREAK_WORDS = CLAUSE_BREAK_WORDS - {and, or}`. A subject carries across a coordinator:
  in "her brother laughs and says see you tomorrow" the brother is still the subject.
- `COMPLEMENT_WORDS`: think, sure, know, believe, reckon, expect, guess, suppose, imagine, promise,
  able, going, gonna, want, wanna, mean, plan, planning, say, said, saying, certain, feel, remember,
  if, whether, that, thought, knew, expected, told and tell.
- `FIRST_SECOND`: the I, we and you forms, including `let's` and `u`.
- `NEGATION_IDIOMS = {wait, stop, worry, mind, help, only, me}`, plus the pair "gonna lie". A
  negation directly followed by one of these is not a negation of anything after it ("can't wait",
  "don't worry", "never mind", "not me ...", "not gonna lie", "not only").

These are English-grammar constants of the checker, not story data. They live beside
`THIRD_PARTY_WORDS` and `NARRATION_THIRD`, which are already engine constants. Story data keeps
what it has today: the phrase tables, `negations` and `ordinary_names`.

### 3.2 Each site on its own terms

| Site | Scope | Rule |
|---|---|---|
| A `_negated` | clause prefix, cut at `CLAUSE_BREAK_WORDS` and breaks | Take the **last** negation in scope that is not an idiom. If there is none, the claim stands. Otherwise look at the words after it: if an I/we/you word appears there with no `COMPLEMENT_WORDS` word before it, a new clause has started and the claim stands ("didn't sleep much last night I'll text you tonight", F18). Otherwise the claim is cancelled ("I don't think I'll be able to message you", N3). |
| B `_someone_else` | nearest person-word, cut at `SUBJECT_BREAK_WORDS` and breaks | Scan backwards. A possessive followed by more words ("her brother ...") → someone else. `X's` where X is another contact, or the companion followed by more words → someone else. The companion's own words, a first/second-person word, or `your` → not someone else. A third-person narration word or another contact's name → someone else. Nothing found → not someone else. |
| C `_promise_hits` (who) | nearest of a `_YOU_WORDS` word or a companion word, in the subject scope | The nearer one decides whether the promise is the player's or the companion's; "" if neither. |
| D `_third_party` | nearest person-word, subject scope | A `THIRD_PARTY_WORDS` word → True. A `FIRST_SECOND` word → False. **New: `my` followed by more words → True** (closes S2, "my sister keeps saying see you tomorrow"). Nothing found → False. |
| E `_someone_elses_arrival` | with a companion (scene prose) → site B's rule; otherwise nearest person-word | A third-party word, a contact's name word, or `my` → True. A `FIRST_SECOND` word → False. |

Site A also governs the **player's typed words**, through `stated` and `stated_promise`. The change
there is a correction too. "I don't think I'll be running late" no longer extends a plan
(`plans.on_player_text`), and "I can't wait, I'll text you tonight" now records the promise it
makes (`promises.phrase_match`).

### 3.3 Out of scope, recorded

`games/read-receipts/data/world/activities.yaml`'s `negations` belong to the typed-line matcher
(`engine/social/typed.py`). That matcher refuses a whole line if it holds any of them, a
deliberately conservative whole-line rule (addendum 4). It is not the agreement window and is not
changed.

### 3.4 Regression fixtures

Rows are added to `tests/fixtures/social_agreement/*.yaml` (`promise_claimed`, `plan_claimed`,
`commitment_claimed`, `media_claimed`, `arrival_claimed`). Their tests assert `len >= 6`, so new
rows break nothing. A new parametrised file, `tests/test_social_agreement_scope.py`, carries the
corpus with each case's expected result:
- **N1-N14**, the long negations: must **pass** (no failure). For example, "I really don't think I
  can see you tonight" and "I'm not even sure I'll get the chance to text you tonight".
- **F1-F18**, earlier-clause negations: must still **fail**. For example, "can't do lunch but I'll
  text you tonight", "Not tonight, see you tomorrow", "don't worry about it I'll text you tonight"
  (no punctuation) and "didn't sleep much last night I'll text you tonight".
- **The ten S cases** (S1-S3, S5-S10, S12; the research numbering), third parties in texter
  replies. S1, S2, S3, S7, S8, S9 and S12 pass; S5, S6 and S10 fail. S2 is pinned by §3.2 site D's new `my` rule.
- **C1-C5** and T1-T13: scene prose (companion Maya, Priya another contact), added to
  `tests/test_social_companion.py`.
- **Player text**: three cases for `stated` (typed by the player). A long negation records no
  promise. An earlier-clause negation still records one. "I don't think I'll be running late" does
  not extend a plan.
- **C4** ("She says she really doesn't think she can see you tomorrow") is in the corpus. It is
  expected to **pass only if §9.4's data change lands**. If the owner declines that change, it is
  marked as a known miss in the file with the reason; it is never `xfail`.

Every case gets a view in which the claim is **not** allowed: no plan open, no promise window, no
willing step, no media, and a plan still on its way. The fixtures are written by hand, so they are
targeted cases rather than a sample of model output. The live sample is item 6's report (§7.5).

### 3.5 Measuring it

T1 writes this table into the CHANGELOG and the module's header:
1. **The corpus**, before (v0.25.0 code) and after, per class (N, F, S, C/T, player text) and per
   site. The prototype's numbers (§1.1) are the expectation, not the result.
2. **The existing fixtures**: 85/85 bad caught and 109/109 good passing, before and after.
3. **Retry-rate under a claimful voice.** `scripts/simulate_social.py` gains `--voice corpus`, a
   stub voice that writes its bubbles from the regression corpus's texter rows, drawn on the
   harness's own stream. It runs at the owner's scale (6 sessions, 10 seeds, `attentive`, 15 s)
   before and after. The table reports agreement failures by check name, correction retries, and
   dropped bubbles and fallbacks per 1,000 replies. This is a harness option, never the default, so
   `FULL_MEASURED` and its pins do not move.

## §4 — Item 2: ranking under the cap

### 4.1 The rule

```python
def _capped(rows: Sequence[OptionRow]) -> tuple[tuple[str, str], ...]:
    """At most _MAX_OPTIONS rows. At or under the cap: unchanged, in order. Over it: the best
    _MAX_OPTIONS by row.rank (lower first; ties keep their place), returned in ORIGINAL order."""
```

- **Rank only over the cap.** A list that fits keeps today's output byte for byte. The ranking
  chooses **which** rows survive and never reorders them.
- **Deterministic.** A rank key is built only from state values and authored data: tiers, prices,
  ids and counts. Ties go to the row's walk position. No RNG and no wall clock are involved, and no
  set or dict iteration leaks into the key.
- **One helper.** Every capped builder ends `IntentVerb(verb, _capped(rows))`. Builders that need
  no ranking (§4.3, "authored order") pass a rank of their walk position, which reduces to today's
  `rows[:8]`.

### 4.2 The shape it builds on **[after v0.25.0, §6.2 and §7.2]**

v0.25.0 splits `_eat`, `_buy`, `_sell`, `_work` and `_craft` into uncapped row functions
(`_eat_rows(state)` and so on). They yield `(target, label)` in today's walk order. The builder then
takes `rows[:_MAX_OPTIONS]`, and `overlay_targets(state)` reads the row functions uncapped.

v0.26.0 changes that seam in three ways:
- The row functions yield `OptionRow(target, label, rank)`, a `NamedTuple`. `overlay_targets` maps
  them to `(target, label)` in its one comprehension, so the overlay's lists, labels and order are
  unchanged. This is asserted against v0.25.0's own recordings.
- Each builder that v0.25.0 split calls `_capped(rows)` in place of `rows[:_MAX_OPTIONS]`.
- The capped builders v0.25.0 did not split are split now, the same way, so that every capped verb
  passes through `_capped`.

**Assumption.** If v0.25.0 lands with a different row shape (for example a dict, or rows built
inside `overlay_targets`), T2 adapts the carrier and keeps the rule. The design depends only on an
uncapped row list existing in walk order before the cap.

`_sell` loses its `break` at eight in v0.25.0. Its row function walks the whole inventory and quotes
every carried item, which `ranked sell` needs anyway. That cost grows with pack size and is
in-process. T2 measures it on a 40-item pack, and the result goes in the CHANGELOG.

### 4.3 The rankings (lower first)

| Verb | Rank key | Why |
|---|---|---|
| `sell` | `(tier, -unit_price)`. Tier 0 is hot or stolen goods a fence **here** will take (`quote.ok and unit_kind == "hot" and quote.fence`); tier 1 is any sale this vendor would make (`quote.ok`); tier 2 is refused. | The owner's rule: hot goods and vendor-relevant goods first, then by unit value. This closes "bench makings crowd loot out of Marrow's list". |
| `buy` | `(held, vendor_row_index, price)`: things not carried first, then a round robin across the vendors here, then the cheapest | A second vendor's stock is not starved by the first; NEON CITY's food at `the_grid` is no longer cut by id order alone. |
| `eat` | `(food_value hunger, most negative first)` | The most filling food survives. |
| `work` | `(-pay_per_hour)` | The best-paid shift survives. |
| `craft` | `(output_carried, -output_value)` | Something new first, then the most valuable. |
| `forage` | `(worked_today, id)` | Unworked nodes first. |
| `discharge` | `(due_in_days)` | A debt about to break is never the one cut. |
| `case` | `(-unknown_lines)` | Houses with the most still to learn. |
| `burgle` | `(not_cased, -premise_value)` | Cased houses first. |
| `flag` | quest stage order (data order) | Matches the order a stage is reached in. |
| `check` | `(-skill_rank, declared order)` | It never bites in a shipped story (at most 7 skills). Stated so it is defined. |
| `lift` | unchanged (`thievery.marks`, already ranked and pinned) | |
| `guise` | walk position | It cannot exceed the declared guises. |
| `bargain`, `set_piece`, `challenge`, `card`, `job` (`_job_verbs`), `flashback` | walk position: **authored order is the ranking** | The author's order is the meaning. Each gets a **validator advisory** (`engine/games/validation.py`) when authored content can offer more than eight at once (a menu card's beats, a location's set-pieces, a premise's jobs), naming the rows that would be cut. This closes the deferred row's "the validator gives no advisory". |

Every rank key is computed from data the row function already reads. T2 adds no new lookups beyond
`inventory.holds` for `buy` and `craft`.

## §5 — Item 4: counterfactual echoes

### 5.1 The fork machinery (`engine/game/fork.py`, new)

The engine pieces, shared with item 5:
- `fork(state) -> GameState`: `type(state).from_dict(state.to_save_dict())`, the proven route. The
  fork gets a **new** `session_id` (`fork-<hex>`), so it can never collide with the live session's
  entry in a `SessionStore`.
- `with forking():`, a `ContextVar` scope (classified in `tests/fixtures/module_state.yaml` as a
  per-context value, never a module bool). It does three things:
  1. **Guards.** `SessionStore`, `saves.mark_dirty`, the autosave, `media` job submission, socket
     emits and every `engine/llm` entry point **raise `ForkEscaped`** if reached. A fork that reaches
     any of them is a bug, and the guard turns it into a failed test, never a silent write.
  2. **Survival held.** Survival's hunger and stamina pass, and the death check, are skipped for the
     fork's player (trap 1, §5.2). The world still moves: clocks, doom, agendas, law, jobs, events,
     schedules, threads and `npc_sim`.
  3. **No unseeded dice.** A `dice` roll with no stream raises in a fork instead of falling back to
     `_UNSEEDED` (`dice.py:80-86`).
- `apply_intent(fork_state, intent) -> list[receipt]`: resolves one intent headless, through
  `tool_dispatcher.execute_intent` on a `GameEngine(fork_state)`, the way `scripts/simulate.py`
  builds its engine. No narration runs, and no negotiation pipeline runs: there is no model, so NPC
  plans are not projected. The echo says so in its receipt (`projection: engine`).
- `step(fork_state, days, *, cut_hours) -> None`: calls `clock.advance_time` in fixed steps of
  `cut_hours`. The default is the story's `world.tick_hours`, or 1 if it declares none. It never
  calls `WorldSim.on_tick` (trap 2), so no wall-clock value is read or written.
- `diff(a, b) -> list[Delta]`: compares two forks stepped the same way, over a closed set of signals
  (§5.3).

The fork runs under the active story. That activation is process-global, and a fork for a save of
another story is refused with a reason (it matters only for Studio, §6.4).

### 5.2 The traps, ruled

| Trap | Ruling |
|---|---|
| 1. Survival is not cut-invariant | Fixed cut (`cut_hours`) and survival held in the fork (§5.1). Both forks of a comparison are stepped identically, so they are directly comparable. The idle player neither starves nor eats, and the echo measures **the world**. Nothing in the live game changes, and the engine-wide deferred row stays. |
| 2. Wall-clock tick events are not replayable | The fork never runs `on_tick`. **[after v0.25.0, §6.1]** The flagship's caravan, tinker and press are probabilistic declared events rolled per in-game day inside `advance_time`, on their own `event.<id>` streams, so the fork sees them. The Wicked Garden and Dev Story still use the three legacy slots, which only `on_tick` rolls, so a fork of theirs would never see those events. **Echoes are therefore opt-in, and only a story with no legacy event slot may opt in.** The opt-in validator refuses a story whose `schedules.yaml` configures a legacy slot (§5.6). The Garden can opt in after its v0.27.0 overhaul converts its slots. |
| 3. Module state | Under `forking()` the guards raise on every session-reached side effect. `tests/test_fork.py` drives each guard. |
| 4. The phone | `social.events.advance` would fill the fork's `state.social` with due messages that no voicer answers. Phone stories may not opt in (Non-goals). Studio's what-if for READ RECEIPTS (§6.4) steps the world with the phone silent and says so. |
| 5. Unseeded dice | They raise in a fork (§5.1). |

### 5.3 What an echo compares

Two forks of the **pre-turn** state are compared. Fork A applies the intent the player took, and
fork B applies the road not taken. Both are stepped N days with the same cut, and `diff(A, B)`
reads a closed set of signals, each with a player-facing name the engine already owns:

| Signal | Read from | Named by |
|---|---|---|
| `ending_closer` / `ending_further` | `endings.recompute` scores; only for an ending whose tier is `unlocked` or `locked` in **fork A** (the live road) | the ending's `title` |
| `phase` | `evil_phase` | the phase's display name |
| `standing` | a faction's band (`factions.yaml` `bands`) | the faction name and band label |
| `moved` | `state.moved` entries present in one fork and not the other | the entry's own engine-written text |
| `thread` | a thread struck, sealed, broken or expired in one fork only | the thread template's text |
| `clock` | a clock beat fired in one fork only | the beat's authored text |
| `law` | a wanted band or custody in one fork only | the Law's band label |

The divergence score is a weighted count:
- `ending_closer`/`ending_further`: 5 each;
- `phase`: 4;
- `thread`: 3;
- `standing`: 2;
- `clock`: 2;
- `law`: 2;
- `moved`: 1 each, at most 3.

These weights are **first values**. They decide which echoes are kept, never a game outcome, so
rule 10 does not bind them. T6 still reports their distribution over the doors harness's seeds.

No signal ever names an ending that fork A does not already show. So an echo cannot reveal a door
that v0.25.0's "ways it ends" overlay hides (v0.25.0 §8.4).

### 5.4 Choosing pivotal turns

A turn is chosen at the end of the turn, deterministically, from data already in hand. No fork is
involved in choosing.

**1. A turn is a candidate when all of these hold:**
- the story has opted in (§5.6);
- the turn's chosen option carried an intent that **resolved** (`intent_resolved` true);
- the turn offered **at least one other option with an intent** that was legal at that moment. The
  offered set is the previous payload's `choices`, which the journal also records (§6.2);
- and at least one of these is true:
  - **(a) Authored:** the chosen card beat, or the quest stage completed this turn, carries
    `pivotal: true`. This is a new optional key on a deck beat and on a quest stage, read by the
    validator, ignored by every other reader, and changing no bound beat unless authored (as with
    v0.25.0's E5).
  - **(b) A door:** the chosen beat is a beat of a door deck (it carries `ending_lock`), or it moved
    an ending's tier this turn.
  - **(c) A structural move:** this turn's receipts include a thread struck or broken, an arc
    unlocked, a faction band crossed, or the doom phase changed.

**2. Bounded:** at most **one** candidate per in-game day. The first one of the day wins, and a
later candidate that day is ignored. On top of that there is at most **one** computed echo per
turn.

**3. Computed:** for a candidate, the engine forks the turn's pre-turn snapshot. That is the
`StateTransaction` snapshot dict the turn already took, so it is not a second serialisation. It
takes the alternatives as **at most two** other offered intents, in offered order, and builds three
forks: taken, alt 1 and alt 2. Each is stepped `horizon_days` days (default 7). The echo for the
candidate is the alternative with the highest score against the taken fork. Ties go to the earlier
alternative. A score of 0 keeps nothing.

**4. Kept:** `state.echoes` holds the **two** highest-scoring echoes of the run. Ties go to the
earlier turn, and a new echo replaces the lowest only if it scores strictly higher.

The receipt is engine data only:

```yaml
{turn: 142, day: 19, horizon_days: 7, projection: engine,
 taken: {action: card, target: "the_count_kept/K1/keep_it", label: "Keep the count"},
 not_taken: {action: card, target: "the_count_kept/K1/let_it_go", label: "Let it go"},
 score: 9,
 deltas: [{signal: ending_closer, id: the_long_witness, taken: 0.6, not_taken: 0.2},
          {signal: phase, taken: stirring, not_taken: spreading}]}
```

Labels are the offered choices' own labels, never a raw id (they came from the payload).

### 5.5 Cost, bounded by steps, measured in time

The bound is a **step count, never wall time**. A wall-clock cutoff would make whether an echo
exists depend on the machine, and so would break replay.

- Per candidate: three forks × `horizon_days` × 24 / `cut_hours` `advance_time` steps. At the
  defaults (7 days, 1-hour cut) that is 504 steps.
- Per run: at most one candidate per in-game day (§5.4).
- `echoes.horizon_days` is clamped to `[1, 14]` and `echoes.max_alternatives` to `[1, 2]`, by the
  loader.

From the probe, the expected time is 3 × 39-184 ms plus 3 × 1-7 ms to fork, which is about
0.15-0.6 s per candidate. T6 measures it on the flagship over the doors harness's seeds and records
the median and p95 in the CHANGELOG. `tests/test_fork_cost.py` (`slow`) holds the flagship's p95
under 750 ms, a first value set from that measurement.

**When it runs:** after the turn's narration has been emitted and before its autosave, under the
turn lock. That ordering makes it deterministic: the echo is part of the turn's state, and the next
turn starts after it. The player is reading the prose while it runs. In hosted mode it holds the
worker's GIL for that time, at most once per in-game day per account. That is recorded in
docs/HOSTING.md.

### 5.6 Opting in, and the epilogue

The opt-in is a story's epilogue index (`data/epilogues/epilogue_index.yaml` or the file its
`paths` names):

```yaml
counterfactuals:
  horizon_days: 7
  max_alternatives: 2
  max_shown: 2                    # 1 or 2
  text:                           # authored templates; {fields} are engine-substituted
    ending_closer: "Had you chosen to {not_taken}, {ending} would have stood nearer by day {day_end}."
    phase: "Had you chosen to {not_taken}, the count would have reached {phase} by day {day_end}."
    standing: "Had you chosen to {not_taken}, {faction} would have held you {band}."
    moved: "Had you chosen to {not_taken}: {text}"
    thread: "..."
    clock: "..."
    law: "..."
```

- No `counterfactuals:` block means no candidate, no fork, no `state.echoes` and no payload key.
  That is byte-identical (§9.2).
- The validator checks the block in four ways:
  - every template's fields are known;
  - the clamps hold;
  - the story declares no legacy event slot (§5.2, trap 2);
  - the story has no `paths.social`.
- `Epilogue` gains `counterfactuals: list[str]`. `render` fills it from `state.echoes`, at most
  `max_shown`, highest score first. Each line takes the template for the echo's strongest delta.
  `to_dict` writes the key **only when the list is non-empty**. The story places it with
  `render_order` (it is not in `DEFAULT_ORDER`).
- The words are the template's, and the facts are the receipt's. **No model writes or sees them.**

**Who opts in this release** is an owner question (§15). The proposal is the flagship only
**[after v0.25.0]**: its events are day rolls and its doors are cards. Its eight epilogue cards are
v0.25.0's, so the templates are a story content edit in `games/clockwork-dark/`. HUE & CRY renders
its endings through modules, not epilogue cards, and is not opted in.

**v0.25.0's endings gallery [after v0.25.0, §8.4].** The "ways it ends" overlay shows only
`unlocked` rows and the locked ending. An echo could add one line under a shown row ("Had you ... on
day 19, this stood nearer"). That would need the overlay to read `state.echoes` through a payload
key or a route, which is a client change. It is offered as an option (§15) and not designed in by
default.

### 5.7 Tests (`tests/test_fork.py`, `tests/test_echoes.py`, new)

- **Isolation.** For every story with no `paths.social` plus READ RECEIPTS:
  - fork and step 7 days;
  - the live state's `to_save_dict()` and `rng_counters` are byte-equal before and after;

- **Guards.**
  - Under `forking()`, every guarded entry point raises `ForkEscaped`. Each one is canary-checked by

  - A fork state handed to a `SessionStore` raises.

- **Pivotal selection.**
  - Each rule (a), (b) and (c) makes a candidate.
  - No other-option-with-intent means no candidate.
  - Only one per day.
  - Top-two retention, and tie order.
- **Receipts.**
  - An echo's deltas name only endings shown in fork A.

  - A story with no block: `Epilogue.to_dict()` is identical to v0.25.0's for every ending.
  - An opted-in story: its lines are rendered from its templates.
- **Determinism.** The same seed and choices give the same `state.echoes`, run twice.

## §6 — Item 5: the replay inspector and branch scrubber

It ships after items 1-4 (§14). It shares item 4's fork and needs item 6's model context for the
journal's `model` field.

### 6.1 What it shows

A Studio tab, **Runs**:
1. **A list of saves** for the active story.
2. **A timeline** of a chosen save's turns: day, hour, place, the choice label, and a marker for
   each of refused, evaluator flag, retry, fallback, pivotal and checkpoint.
3. **A turn panel** for the clicked turn:
   - **Intent**: action, target and label, with `intent_resolved`.
   - **Receipt**: `tool_receipts`, through the receipts summariser's lines, with raw JSON one click
     away.
   - **Plan/negotiation diff**: per agent, the plans proposed, accepted, refused and vetoed, drawn
     as a diff (proposed → committed). Private motives are already excluded by
     `PipelineResult.to_dict` and stay excluded.
   - **Evaluator**: the scorecard, `passed`, `notes` and `flag`; governance rule ids; `retries` and
     `finish_reason`; the model and profile.
   - **The narration**: joined from `transcript.jsonl` by turn number.
   - **Branch**: from a turn with a checkpoint (§6.3), either "Branch here as a new save" or
     "What-if: take <another offered choice>".

### 6.2 The turn journal (`turns.jsonl`)

`default_state._autosave` already appends `transcript.jsonl`. Beside it, it now appends one
`turns.jsonl` row per narrated turn (`engine/persistence/saves.py`, `append_turn`):

```
{turn, day, hour, location_id,
 offered: [{label, intent}],          # the choices the player chose from (the previous payload's)
 chosen: {label, intent},  intent_resolved,
 tool_receipts, evaluation, negotiation, governance, quest_events,
 finish_reason, llm_unavailable, retries, model, profile,
 tick_hours,                          # the background tick applied before this turn
 rng_counters_before,                 # the counters at the turn's start
 checkpoint: true|false, pivotal: true|false, echo: {score} | null}
```

- `retries` comes from `StorytellerTurnResult.retries`, and `model`/`profile` from item 6's
  context (§7.2). Both are carried to `_autosave` beside the payload, **not in it**. The payload is
  unchanged (§9.2).
- `tick_hours` is `_background_tick`'s return value, which is discarded today. It is kept as a
  record only and replays nothing.
- No narration goes in this file; the transcript holds it. The `turns.jsonl` row holds the
  receipts the save already implies.
- **Bounded.** At `replay.journal.max_bytes` (default 8 MiB) the file rotates to `turns.jsonl.1`,
  keeping `replay.journal.keep` (default 2) rotated files. The rotation reuses the audit log's
  scheme (`engine/hosting/audit.py`'s `rotated_path` logic moves to `engine/persistence/jsonl.py`
  so both use it).
- **Local only.** `replay.journal.enabled` defaults to true. Hosted mode forces it off. Studio is
  refused there anyway, and a per-account journal would be disk spent for no reader.
- **Phone stories.** One row per narrated world turn. Phone actions are already in
  `phone_archive.jsonl` and are not journalled again. Branching from a phone action is NOT WIRED
  (§11).

### 6.3 Checkpoints

A branch needs the exact pre-turn state. The turn already serialises it (the `StateTransaction`
snapshot), so a checkpoint costs only gzip and a write. Checkpoints are written to
`checkpoints/<turn>.json.gz` in the save's folder. The thinning is bounded:
- **every** turn for the last 50 turns;
- every 10th turn back to turn `n − 500`;
- every 50th turn before that;
- plus turn 1;
- plus every pivotal turn (§5.4);
- capped at `replay.checkpoints.max_files` (default 200), dropping the oldest of the thinned set
  first.

Thinning runs at write time, and only on files the policy no longer keeps. It deletes inside the
save's own folder only, by an exact computed path; the folder is never removed.

**Cost, recorded by T8:**
- Flagship-sized state: the gzip and write per turn, measured, budget 5 ms median.
- READ RECEIPTS' day-60 state (`save.json` about 600-860 KB): its world turns only. T8 measures the
  gzip time and size. If the median exceeds 15 ms, the phone story's checkpoint is every 5th world
  turn (`replay.checkpoints.every_phone`).

Saves are never rewritten, `save.json` is unchanged, and deleting a save deletes its folder
(already the case; T8 asserts the checkpoints go with it).

### 6.4 Branching, read-only against the original

- **Branch here as a new save.** Copy the checkpoint into a **new** save folder with a new
  `save_id` and a new `session_id`, and write a `branched_from: {save_id, turn}` row into its index
  entry. The original folder is opened read-only. `tests/test_replay_branch.py` asserts every file
  in it is byte- and mtime-identical after a branch. The new save is an ordinary save: it plays
  forward with the live model (or a stub) when the player resumes it.
- **What-if.** For the chosen turn's checkpoint and one of its `offered` intents, run item 4's
  `fork` → `apply_intent` → `step` against a fork of the **taken** intent, with the horizon picked
  in the panel (1-14 days). Show `diff` as the echo deltas (§5.3). Nothing is saved.
  - It works for any story, opted in to echoes or not, because it is a tool and not a payload.
  - A story with legacy event slots gets a banner: "the caravan, tinker and press slots are not
    projected (wall-clock)".
  - READ RECEIPTS gets a banner: "the phone is silent in a projection".
- **Story activation.** A branch or what-if for a save of a story that is not active is refused
  with "Activate <story> first". Activation is process-global.

### 6.5 Routes (Studio blueprint, local only)

| Route | Returns |
|---|---|

## §7 — Item 6: the per-model compliance report

Built on what exists: `EvaluationResult.to_dict()`, `StorytellerTurnResult.retries`,
`LMSResponse`'s fields, the voicer's `failures`, `check_scene_prose`'s result and the backend's
probe and starvation outcomes. **No new evaluator, no new check, no new subsystem.** The new parts
are one `ContextVar`, one writer and one script.

### 7.1 The context (`engine/telemetry/compliance.py`, beside the oracle)

`compliance_context(role=..., story=..., turn=...)` is a context manager over a `ContextVar`. The
roles are:
- `storyteller` (the narrated turn);
- `assistant`;
- `texter` (a voiced reply);
- `scene` (scene prose);
- `world` (READ RECEIPTS' world narration);
- `summarizer`;
- `draft` (Studio drafting);
- `probe`.

It is entered:
- by `default_state.run_turn` around the storyteller;
- by `voicer.run_pass` around each reply (inside the worker thread; the voicer's workers start with
  `contextvars.copy_context()`);
- by `scene.py` around its draft;
- by the summarizer;
- by the probe.

The session is recorded as a 12-character salted hash, never the save id or the account. It is
classified in `tests/fixtures/module_state.yaml` as per-context.

### 7.2 The records

One JSONL file, `<data_root>/telemetry/compliance.jsonl`. In hosted mode it is one file per
process, `compliance-<process>.jsonl`, with the worker's slug or `frontdoor`, so no two processes
append to one file. Rotation follows §6.2's scheme
(`telemetry.compliance.max_bytes` default 16 MiB, `keep` default 4). The writer holds a new
**leaf** lock, placed in `engine/locks.py`'s order and registered with `renew_after_fork`. Every
record has `v: 1`, `ts`, `kind`, `role`, `story`, `session` (hash), `turn`, `provider`, `model`
and `profile`.

| `kind` | Written by | Fields |
|---|---|---|
| `call` | `engine/llm/backend.py` `chat`, `chat_stream`, `chat_probe`: once per HTTP call, the retries the nets make included | `transport`, `rung` (1-3), `latency_ms`, `input_tokens`, `output_tokens`, `reasoning_tokens`, `finish_reason`, `starved` (bool), `net` (`""`, `reasoning_off`, `measured_room`, `think_false`), `outcome` (`ok`, `starved`, `unparsed`, `error`, `unavailable`), `conformed_away` (the stripped key **names**, from `schemas.CONFORMED_AWAY`) |
| `turn` | `default_state.run_turn`, after the evaluator | `evaluation` (the subscores and `passed`, from `to_dict()`, with `notes` replaced by their **rule codes**: each evaluator note is mapped to a stable code by a table in `evaluator.py`, and an unmapped note is counted as `other`), `retries`, `retry_reasons` (codes), `fallback` (`llm_unavailable` or the fallback narration used), `governance` (rule ids), `intent_declared`, `intent_resolved`, `intentless_action_choices` (the count of offered choices with an action verb in their label and no `intent`, the CLAUDE.md deferred row's measure), `schema_valid` |
| `reply` | `engine/social/voicing.py`, per reply | `attempts`, `failures` (check **names**: UNPARSED, EMPTY, `plan_claimed` and so on), `outcome` (`first_pass`, `corrected`, `dropped_bubble`, `fallback`) |
| `scene` | `engine/social/scene.py` at `:1670` | `attempts`, `failures` (check names), `outcome` |

**No play text.** No record holds narration, a message, a label, a name, a choice's words or a save
id. `tests/test_compliance_no_play_text.py` (modelled on `tests/test_admin_no_play_text.py`) runs a
flagship turn and a READ RECEIPTS reply with sentinel strings in every player-visible text and
asserts that none reaches the file.

The **payload is unchanged.** `retries`, `model` and `profile` travel beside it to `_autosave` and
the writer. They are not added to `turn_payload`, so the local-mode golden does not move (§9.2).

**Off switch.** `telemetry.compliance.enabled` defaults to true. Off, nothing is written and no
context is entered beyond the `ContextVar` set. Under the test sandbox the file lands in the
sandbox's temp storage root, like every other storage write.

### 7.3 The report (`scripts/compliance_report.py`)

`python scripts/compliance_report.py [--since DATE] [--story SLUG] [--role ROLE] [--by role,profile,model] [--json]`
reads `compliance*.jsonl*` under the data root (or `--path`) and prints one row per group:

| Column | From |
|---|---|
| calls, turns/replies | counts |
| schema pass | `call.outcome != unparsed` and `turn.schema_valid` |
| agreement first-pass / corrected / dropped / fallback | `reply.outcome`, plus the top three failing check names |
| scene-prose first-pass | `scene.outcome` |
| evaluator pass, and mean of each subscore | `turn.evaluation` |
| retries per turn (mean, p95), and the top retry reasons | `turn.retries`, `retry_reasons` |
| fallback rate | `turn.fallback`, `reply.outcome == fallback` |
| refusal rate | `intent_resolved` false over `intent_declared` |
| intentless action choices per turn | `turn.intentless_action_choices` |
| starved / net fired | `call.starved`, `call.net` |
| latency p50 / p95 (ms) | `call.latency_ms` |
| tokens out (mean), reasoning share | `call.*_tokens` |

It reads with a streaming parser and skips a malformed line, counting it in a footer. The report
runs no model and writes nothing. `tests/test_compliance_report.py` runs it on a synthetic file with
known groups and checks every column.

### 7.4 Hosted mode

Hosted mode writes the same records. The admin panel and the closed metrics schema are unchanged

## §10 — Item 3: the suite's runtime

**Profile first** is done (§1.3). This section fixes the two causes the profile found, with no test
removed, shortened, re-tiered or re-seeded. Rule 10's "a balance test keeps its length" holds.

### 10.1 A: heavy tests first, one per worker (the largest cut)

- **A `heavy(minutes=N)` marker** (registered in `tests/tiers.py`) on every test or module fixture
  group measured over 2 minutes in the v0.24.0 release run (§1.3's table, rows 1-16). `minutes` is
  the release run's figure, rounded up. The marker carries only a weight: it changes no tier, no
  timeout and no selection.
- **Ordering** (`tests/tier_plugin.py`, in the existing `tryfirst` `pytest_collection_modifyitems`,
  after the groups are marked). The scheduling unit is an `xdist_group` or a single test. Units
  carrying `heavy` move to the front of the collection, ordered by weight descending, ties by node
  id. The rest keep their order.
  - Every worker applies the same deterministic order, which xdist requires.
  - `LoadScopeScheduling` hands out its initial units round-robin over the workers. So the first
    *W* heavy units land on *W* different workers, and the heaviest go first.
  - The order applies only in an xdist `loadgroup` run (`_groups_wanted`). A bare or serial
    `pytest` keeps collection order.
- **Guards.**
  - `tests/test_suite_tiers.py` gains: heavy units sort first by weight, and the relative order of
    non-heavy tests is unchanged.
  - `scripts/run_tests.py` gains an **advisory** in its summary: any test over 120 s in the run's
    `--durations` output that does not carry `heavy` is listed as "add heavy(minutes=…)". It is a
    printed line, not a failure, because timings move with the machine.
- **Expected:** the parallel phase falls from about 83 min to about 38-42 min on Windows, bounded
  by `[attentive]`. The serial phase is unchanged. CI on 2 workers is throughput-bound and gains
  0-10 min from A.

### 10.2 B: READ RECEIPTS' per-action caches (the floor, and CI's throughput)

Each cache is a **pure function of its full input**, so it cannot go stale. That rules out keying
on a revision counter a writer might forget to bump.

| # | Hotspot | Cache | Classified as |

| # | Risk | Ruling |

| R1 | Item 1's clause rules are English heuristics, and a model's phrasing finds a new false positive or negative. | The corpus is the floor, not the ceiling.
