## Goal

The brief, as a list of things a player can do on the day v0.22.0 ships:

1. Choose **male or female**, an age, attributes, hobbies and what they are
   **looking for** (friendship, something short, something long, unsure).
2. Open **two dating apps with different cultures**, populated by generated
   members of the opposite sex, each with a bio, photos, traits, hobbies and
   an intent of their own; swipe or like, and **match only when it is
   mutual**.
3. Talk to as many people as they like, across **in-app chat, texts and an
   instant messenger**, and send or ask for **voice messages, selfies and
   video messages** -- rare, earned, sometimes declined.
4. Play **two two-player games**: a word game (Tile Talk) and *This or
   That*, with a match or a contact.
5. Meet people the way online dating really goes: **many never match, some
   match and never write, some ghost, some drift, some take a turn the player
   does not want**, and some become friends, flings, partners.
6. Close the game, come back tomorrow, and find that **their phone has been
   living without them**: replies, double texts, a match that expired, a
   "hey stranger" from someone who went quiet weeks ago.
7. Never hit an ending. The relationships **remain, grow and change**.

Every one of those is an ENGINE mechanic resolved from the seed and the
player's structured actions (rule 1). The model writes what people SAY, and
proposes -- inside engine bounds -- how each exchange moved them.

**One release.** Everything above ships in v0.22.0 (§24). There is no
v0.22.1.

## Non-goals

- **Any content-rating, intensity, boundary or "safety" layer, in any form**
  (AGENTS.md rule 12). Not in the texter prompt, the summary prompt or the
  date recap, not in the ComfyUI request builder, not in any drafting prompt
  (`scripts/author.py` gets no new prompt for this story), not as a
  "declines explicit requests" behaviour. A person declines a request because
  of WHERE THE RELATIONSHIP IS (stage, trust, mood, budget, how often they
  have been asked, their personality) -- never because of what was asked for.
  The register belongs to `games/read-receipts/prompts/texter.md` and the
  owner's model. §11.6 pins every composed prompt's blocks by test.
- In-person scenes played beat by beat. A meet-up is an off-screen event with
  an engine-resolved outcome and a short recap (§14); the game is the phone.
- Voice or video CALLS (live). Messages only (NOT WIRED, §22).
- Jealousy, discovery or "cheating" mechanics between relationships (§22).
- An authored friend contact (§22): the generated cast is the brief's core.
- A real-world-clock-aligned calendar (§15.4).
- Same-sex or any-sex populations in the shipped data. The brief says the
  apps hold "members of the opposite sex"; the filter is DATA (§4.2), so the
  owner's later change is a one-line edit.
- Retrofitting the phone to the other six stories.

## §1 — The story

**READ RECEIPTS.** Port Merrow, a fictional harbour city of the present day:
eleven neighbourhoods with a distance table (for the apps' distance filter and
meet-up venues) and a dozen venues. The player has just moved there, and
their phone is the whole stage. There is no plot, by the brief: "the people
and their identities, traits, personalities, hobbies, behaviors ... are
populated and then the system runs them."

| App | What it is | Culture |
|---|---|---|
| **Matchstick** | Swipe dating: photos first, one-line bios, a big pool | Fast and flaky. Many likes, a low match rate, short openers, more ghosting, more people after something short |
| **Front Porch** | Like-a-prompt dating: full profiles, written prompts, a daily allowance of likes, a "Likes you" list | Slow and deliberate. Fewer, better matches; more people looking for something long; voice notes allowed |
| **Texts** | SMS, per person once numbers are swapped | "Delivered", no read receipts, no typing dots; text and photos |
| **Natter** | Instant messenger, same unlock | Read receipts (the player may turn theirs off), typing dots, voice notes, photos, video messages |
| **Games** | Tile Talk and This or That, against a match or a contact | Asynchronous: each side plays on their own clock |
| **Photos** | The player's own camera roll and everything received | -- |
| **Calendar** | Meet-ups agreed | -- |
| **Contacts** | Everyone with a thread; each person's card | -- |
| **Settings** | Profiles, discovery filters, read receipts | -- |

The app names are invented; no real product's name, logo or wording is used.

## §2 — The engine/story seam

### 2.1 One path key: `paths.social`

Generic engine code under **`engine/social/`**, declared by ONE manifest key,
**`paths.social`**, naming a directory whose files are found by fixed name
(the `paths.rules` convention, AUTHORING §2.2):

| File | Read by | Holds |
|---|---|---|
| `creation.yaml` | `creation.py` | The creation form |
| `population.yaml` | `population.py` | The filter, name pools, ages, jobs, neighbourhoods, hobbies, traits, appearance, intents, texting styles, bio banks, photo sets, fixed per-person flags (`has_pet` ...) |
| `behaviours.yaml` | `behaviour.py` | Archetypes: pacing, ghosting, initiations, arcs, media habits, game skill |

| `relationships.yaml` | `relationships.py`, `meets.py` | Stages and gates, `regard:`, `interest:`, `mood:`, `proposals:`, `meets:` |

| `venues.yaml` | `meets.py` | Where meet-ups happen |
| `games.yaml`, `words.txt` | `games/` | Game rules and banks; the word list |

A story that declares no `paths.social` pays nothing: `engine/social` is never
imported on its turns, its app registers no phone route or event, its payload
carries no `phone` key, and its turns stay byte-identical, asserted by test
for every shipped story. `config/default.yaml`'s `paths:` inventory gains
`social: ""` (35 keys).

Every content fault is a `ValueError` naming file and key, raised at load (the
`agendas.py` rule): a behaviour gated on an undeclared stage, an app
capability nothing reads, a media subject gated on an unknown profile flag.
`engine/games/validation.py` gains `check_social`; `scripts/doctor.py` a row.

## §3 — Player creation

### 3.1 The form is data

`creation.yaml` declares every field; the client renders whatever it says:

```yaml
fields:
  name:        {kind: text, max: 24, default: "Sam"}
  sex:         {kind: choice, of: [male, female], default: female}
  age:         {kind: int, min: 18, max: 75, default: 29}
  looking_for: {kind: choice, of: [friendship, short_term, long_term, unsure], default: unsure}
  hobbies:     {kind: multi, of_table: hobbies, min: 2, max: 6}
  attributes:
    height:    {kind: choice, of: [short, average, tall]}
    build:     {kind: choice, of: [slim, average, athletic, curvy, broad, heavy]}
    hair:      {kind: choice, of_table: hair}
    style:     {kind: choice, of_table: style}
    job:       {kind: choice, of_table: jobs}
    drinks:    {kind: choice, of: [never, socially, often]}
    smokes:    {kind: choice, of: [no, sometimes, yes]}
    kids:      {kind: choice, of: [have, want, dont_want, unsure]}
  personality: {kind: sliders, of: [outgoing, playful, romantic, adventurous, homebody], min: 1, max: 5}
  discovery:
    age_min:   {kind: int, min: 18, default: 25}
    age_max:   {kind: int, max: 75, default: 38}
    distance:  {kind: choice, of: [near, city, anywhere], default: city}
  photos:      {kind: multi, of_table: photo_set, min: 1, max: 6}
```

`of_table` names a table in `population.yaml`, so the player picks from the
lists the population is drawn from. `18` is the apps' own minimum, as on every
real dating app: data, not an engine gate.

### 3.2 How it reaches the engine

- `GET /api/social/creation` (`routes.py`, registered only when declared): the
  resolved form, tables expanded to `{id, label}`.
- `POST /api/game/new` gains an optional `creation` object, passed through
  `SessionStore.create(..., creation=...)` to
  `procgen.new_game_state(..., creation=...)`, which calls
  `social.creation.apply(state, payload)` after `populate_state`.
- `apply` validates (unknown keys refused, enums enforced, an out-of-range
  value a 400 naming the field), fills defaults, and writes
  `state.social.player` through one engine-only effect, `social_profile`.
- An omitted `creation` (tests, the simulator, a harness) means all defaults.
- In a story with no `paths.social`, `creation` in the body is a 400 ("this
  story has no creation form"), never ignored. (`/api/game/new` exists in
  every story; this refusal is a branch inside it, so `app_shape.json` does
  not move.)

### 3.3 The player's photos and bios

`photo_set` is a table of described photos ("on a hike, squinting at the
camera", "a mirror selfie in a good jacket"). The player picks one to six;
they are the profile photos and the camera roll. Each is a media job (§13)
with the player's appearance as subject; procedural tiles carrying the caption
when ComfyUI is off. The count feeds profile completeness (§8.2). Bios are
free text per app, written in Settings (`edit_profile`, §7).

## §4 — The population

### 4.1 Lazy and seeded

A person is a pure function of `(seed, index)`:
`population.person(seed, index)` draws from
`stable_rng(seed, f"{SOCIAL_POPULATION}:{index}")` (no state counter), so a
person is the same whatever order anyone was met in. Nothing static is stored;
`state.social.people[pid]` holds only DYNAMIC state once the player touches
them (§10.1). The static profile is regenerated on read and cached per process
(a per-seed LRU under its own lock, classified in
`tests/fixtures/module_state.yaml`, its lock in `engine/locks.py`'s leaf list
and `tests/lock_order.py`). The lock is held only to look up and to insert:
a profile is GENERATED outside it, because generation reads story data
through cached loaders that take ordered locks; two threads generating the
same person race harmlessly (identical results, first insert wins).

`pid` is `p<index>` (`p0412`). Pools come from `apps.yaml` (starting point
Matchstick 900, Front Porch 400; data is authority, §20: v0.22.0 T26 made
Matchstick's 1800), disjoint index ranges plus a 60-person overlap band, so the
same person can turn up on both apps; the contact card says "Also on Front
Porch", and a match on one app does not match the other.

### 4.2 The filter

```yaml
# population.yaml
filter:
  sex: opposite        # opposite | same | any   -- the brief: opposite
  age_from_discovery: true
```

`same` and `any` are implemented and tested now, so the owner's later change
is this one value. A person's sex is drawn after the filter, so changing it
reshuffles nothing else about anyone.

## §5 — Behaviour: the engine runs them

### 5.1 Archetypes

`behaviours.yaml` declares archetypes; each person has one, drawn by app
weight:

| Archetype | What it does |
|---|---|
| `romantic` | Warm and fast, good-morning texts, warmth grows quickly, offers media sooner |
| `slow_burner` | Slow replies, long messages, slow warmth, rarely drifts |
| `banterer` | Fast, short, playful; loves the games; plateaus unless matched in energy |
| `dry_texter` | "people who don't connect": short replies, few questions, warmth capped low |
| `needy` | "people who are annoying": double and triple texts, sensitive to being left on read |
| `ghoster` | Stops replying at a seeded point; some come back weeks later ("hey stranger") |
| `breadcrumber` | Intermittent warmth, never moves forward unless pressed, then often goes quiet |
| `drifter` | Starts well; interest decays over weeks |
| `turner` | A seeded **turn** (§5.3) changes the relationship |
| `steady` | Consistent; the long-term partner archetype |
| `busy` | Slow on weekdays, present at weekends; reschedules meet-ups |
| `night_owl` | Replies late, initiates after 22:00 |

An archetype is NUMBERS and moves:

```yaml
archetypes:
  needy:
    reply_minutes: {median: 6, spread: 1.0}       # lognormal
    read_minutes:  {median: 2, spread: 0.8}
    reply_prob: 0.97
    opener_prob: 0.6
    ghost_per_exchange: 0.002
    left_on_read_sensitivity: 2.0
    warmth_mult: 1.2
    warmth_cap_band: null
    cool_off_step: 3                               # §10.5
    flake_prob: 0.05                               # §14.2
    double_text: {after_hours: 1.5, prob: 0.6, max_in_row: 3}
    initiations:
      - id: good_morning
        at_hours: [7, 8, 9]
        when: {stage_min: numbers}
        prob_per_day: 0.5
        kind: text
      - id: did_i_do_something
        when: {hours_since_player: 20, stage_min: talking}
        prob: 0.4
        kind: text
        instruction: anxious_check_in              # §11.3's situation line
    media: {offer_mult: 1.0, accept_mult: 1.1}
    games: {skill: 0.4, invite_prob_per_day: 0.05}
    arc: null
```

Every number is a balance constant (§20). Data headers read **"unmeasured
until T26"** until the full simulator pass writes the measured tables.

### 5.2 Schedules

Each person has a week from their job's shape: asleep, at work, free, out.
`behaviour.status_at(person, hour)` is derived, never stored, and consumes no
RNG (the `npc_sim` rule). It shapes latency, initiations and what the voice is
told they are doing.

### 5.3 Arcs: drift and turns

```yaml
arcs:
  turner_wants_different:
    at: {exchanges: [25, 60]}
    beat:
      reveal: wants_something_else     # a canon row from data (§12.1)
      instruction: reveal_turn
      then: {stage: winding_down, warmth: -10}
  drifter_fade:
    from: {days_since_matched: [14, 40]}
    each_day: {interest: -2}
```

A turn is an engine event: it fires at its point, writes its canon and effects
through `apply_effect`, and tells the voice what this person is about to say.

### 5.4 Streams

New named streams in `engine/game/rng.py`: `SOCIAL_POPULATION` (stable, per
person), `SOCIAL_MATCH`, `SOCIAL_PACE`, `SOCIAL_BEHAVIOUR`, `SOCIAL_MEDIA`,
`SOCIAL_MEET`, `SOCIAL_GAME`. Per-person draws use
`stable_rng(seed, f"{STREAM}:{pid}:{n}")` with `n` a per-person counter in
their dynamic state, so one person's history never shifts another's.
Generation seeds for media come from `rng._mix` (blake2b), never Python's
salted `hash` (§13.3).

### 7.2 The channel, registered only where it exists

- **Socket:** OUTBOUND **`phone_action`** `{session_id, action, target, args,
  text}`. Registered in `default_scene.register()` through `FlaskScene.on`
  ONLY WHEN `social.declared()` at app creation (the active story is fixed
  per process), so the hosted gate wraps it and the flagship's
  `app_shape.json` handlers do not move. In a story without a phone the event
  is simply unregistered.
- **HTTP twin:** `POST /api/phone/action`, and every other phone route
  (`/api/social/creation`, `/api/phone/thread/...`, `/api/phone/media/...`),
  registered by `routes.py`'s blueprint only when declared.
- `player_choice` in this story answers a `turn_error` ("Read Receipts is
  played on the phone"); a branch inside the existing handler.

### 7.3 Execution

`engine/agents/tool_dispatcher.py::execute_phone_intent(intent, engine)`
normalises (action and target as strings, args against
`intents.PAYLOADS[action]`, text stripped and capped), finds the verb in the
catalogue rebuilt NOW, and on a miss returns `intents.refusal(intent,
reason)` in the engine's words ("Kai unmatched you", "No likes left today",
"Texts can't send voice notes"). A refusal reaches the client as a system line
or a toast, never as nothing.

Each action resolves through a `@skill` in `engine/skills/builtin/social.py`
(`social_swipe`, `social_send`, `social_ask`, `social_answer`,
`social_propose_meet`, `social_meet`, `social_game_move`, ...), so
`tests/test_reachability.py` sees a production caller with no new allowlist
row.

**The message text is payload, not mechanic.** The action (a message sent to
Kai on Natter at 21:14, two minutes spent) is structured and engine-executed;
its words are what the people respond to, and their effect is a bounded model
proposal the engine clamps (§10.2). Typed text in other stories stays
intent-less; this is a new verb with a payload.

## §8 — Matching

All numbers below live in `apps.yaml`'s `matching:` block (rule 10).

### 8.1 Compatibility

`matching.compatibility(player, person) -> float in [0, 1]`, deterministic:

```yaml
matching:
  weights: {looking_for: 0.30, hobbies: 0.20, personality: 0.20, preferences: 0.20, distance: 0.10}
  looking_for_table: {long_long: 1.0, long_short: 0.2, friendship_any: 0.5, ...}
  dealbreaker_mult: 0.1
  chemistry: [-0.15, 0.15]
  like_back_base: {matchstick: 0.35, front_porch: 0.5}   # starting point; data is authority (§20)
  compat_offset: 0.35
  completeness: [0.6, 1.1]
  super_like_mult: 1.6
  see_like_hours: {median: 6, spread: 1.2}
```

Hobbies by Jaccard over tags; personality per slider (similar on `homebody`/
`adventurous`, complementary allowed on `outgoing`); preferences (age range,
dealbreakers); distance. Plus the hidden chemistry. The player never sees the
number; Front Porch shows a "You both like climbing" line, a fact.

### 8.2 Like-back

Drawn ONCE per pair from `stable_rng(seed, f"{SOCIAL_MATCH}:{pid}")`, so
un-liking and liking again cannot re-roll it:

```
p = like_back_base[app] x (compat_offset + compatibility)
      x max(0, 1 - selectivity x culture.selectivity_mult)
      x completeness_mult(player profile)
      x (super_like_mult if super)
p = clamp(p, 0, 1)
```

A true draw becomes a match after a `see_like` event (minutes to days), so
matches arrive over time.

### 8.3 Likes you, openers, expiry, unmatch

- **Likes you** (Front Porch): daily events, `likes_you_per_day` x
  completeness, each with a templated comment from the liker's banks.
- **Openers**: on a match, the person writes first with `opener_prob`, at a
  delay from their pace; the opener is a due message (§9.1).
- **Expiry**: no message in `match_expiry_days` → expired, an engine line.
- **Unmatch**: either side; a person's comes from an archetype or arc. The
  thread vanishes from the app, with "This match is no longer available" in
  the contact card's history. Block is the player's only.

## §9 — Messaging and pacing

### 9.1 The event queue

`state.social.queue`: `{at, seq, pid, kind, ...}`, ordered by **`(at, seq)`**,
`at` absolute in-game hours (a float: minute resolution), `seq` a monotonic
counter in `state.social.seq`, so equal times replay in one order. Kinds:
`see_like`, `read`, `reply`, `initiate`, `double_text`, `expire`, `likes_you`,
`game_move`, `meet_start`, `arc_beat`, `daily` (likes reset, drift, budgets
refill, mood decay).

`engine/game/clock.py::advance_time` gains one call, after the agendas walk
finishes and before the death check:

```python
try:
    from engine import social
    if social.declared():
        social.events.advance(state, from_hours, state.world_clock_hours)
except ImportError:
    pass
```

`advance` pops every event with `at <= to` in `(at, seq)` order and applies it
through `apply_effect` (engine-only `social_event` kinds); an event may queue
new ones, bounded at `social.max_events_per_advance` (config, 2000) with an
ERROR and a stop. Inert for every other story.

An event that needs WORDS (a reply, an initiation, an opener, a game move's
banter, a post-meet text) does not call the model. It lands as a **due
message**: a message row with time, sender, kind, app, the engine's decision
(text, voice, photo, video, decline, reveal ...) and the situation id,
`voiced: false`, no text. Its words come from the voicing pass (§16.2). So the
mechanics -- who wrote, when, where, whether it carried a photo -- are decided
here, from the seed and inputs.

### 9.2 Sending, and the reply it schedules

`social_send` appends the player's message, costs `cost_minutes` through
`advance_time`, and schedules for the person:

1. `read` at `now + read_latency` (status-aware: asleep → their wake time);
2. on the read, a reply decision: `reply_prob` x f(interest) x f(player
   burst); else left on read;
3. a `reply` at `read + reply_latency` (lognormal by archetype, x status, /
   interest);
4. a ghost decision in the same step: `ghost_per_exchange` x (1 −
   interest/100) x stage factor. A ghost sets `ghosted` (hidden) and cancels
   their queue.

A burst of player messages gets one reply decision; the reply may be up to
three bubbles.

### 9.3 Initiations and double texts

Daily, each live person's `initiations` are drawn (`SOCIAL_BEHAVIOUR`) and
queued inside their free hours. A `double_text` is queued after `after_hours`
of player silence, capped by `max_in_row`. Each carries an instruction id
(§11.3).

### 9.4 Read receipts and typing

- On a `receipts: true` app, the person's `read` writes `read_at` on the
  player's messages ("Read 21:16").
- `open_thread` writes the player's read time; the person sees it if the
  player's receipts are on; a long left-on-read costs interest at
  `left_on_read_sensitivity` per hour, applied at that person's next event.
  Turning receipts off hides the player's reads, and theirs from the player.
- **Typing**: on a `typing` app, a thread shows typing dots only while a
  model call for that person is in flight (`session.typing`, in memory,
  §16.2) -- "being written now". A due message waiting for a pass shows "…"
  (§11.1).

### 9.5 Moving to numbers

`ask {what: number}` or a person's `offer_number` proposal (§10.5) is decided
by the engine: stage gates (§10.3), then a draw on trust x archetype; else
"not yet" or a cool-off. On accept: stage `numbers`, Texts and Natter threads
open, the dating-app thread stays readable.

## §10 — Relationships

### 10.1 What the engine keeps per person

`state.social.people[pid]` (dynamic only):

```
seen_on, swiped, liked_at, matched_at, stage, stage_since,
warmth 0-100, interest 0-100, trust 0-100, mood, mood_until,     (hidden)
regard_day, regard_today {warmth, trust},                          (the day cap, §10.2)
familiarity {exchanges, contact_days}, last_player_at, last_them_at,
ghosted, unmatched_by, blocked, ended_by, arc_point,               (hidden)
asks_today {kind: n}, media_budget {photo, voice, video},
requests[], canon[], knows[], history[], rev, rng_n
```

Written only by `social_*` effect kinds (rule 3). `rev` increments on every
write to this person (§16.3). No cap on live relationships; the queue walks
only people with something scheduled.

### 10.2 How the model grows them

Every voiced reply proposes `regard: {warmth, trust, mood}`. The engine
applies it AT COMMIT (§16.3), through `social_regard`:

- **Per reply**: `relationships.yaml` `regard.per_reply` (warmth −4..+4,
  trust −2..+2).
- **By archetype**: `warmth_mult`; `warmth_cap_band` (a dry texter never
  passes "warming").
- **Per day**: `regard.per_day_gain` (warmth +10, trust +6) per person per
  in-game day, counted against **the in-game day at commit**
  (`regard_day`/`regard_today`); losses are not capped.
- **Interest is the engine's**: `interest.per_exchange`,
  `interest.shared_hobby_mention` (a hobby id matched in the player's text by
  the engine), `interest.daily_decay`, the left-on-read cost, drift. The model
  moves how they FEEL; whether they keep turning up is behaviour.

**Ruling (v0.22.0 T9 review, controller): engine-authored replies grow too.**
A fallback reply (§11.4, the engine's own words when no model answers or both
attempts fail) proposes `relationships.yaml` `regard.fallback` (starting point
warmth +2, trust +1; data is authority, §20: T26 measured it against a working
neutral model, +1/+1, and set +1/+1 under its own smaller daily cap,
`regard.fallback.per_day` 3/2, so a dead model server progresses, more slowly
than a working one), through the same `social_regard` path and the same
per-day cap, instead of zero. Without it a run with no model, or a model that
always fails, could never pass `talking` (warmth starts at 20, `numbers` needs
35) while its only moves were losses. The model still grows a relationship
faster and in both directions; T26 measures both. Applied in T13.

Applying at commit (not at the message's in-game time) is the rule because the
words may be voiced after later events; the per-day cap and every gate read
the state at commit, re-validated (§16.3).

### 10.3 Stages

Transitions are engine-only; gates and branches in `relationships.yaml`:

| Stage | From | Gate (starting points) | Trigger |
|---|---|---|---|
| `matched` | -- | mutual like | event |
| `talking` | matched | 4 exchanges | automatic |
| `numbers` | talking | warmth ≥ 35, trust ≥ 25, ≥ 12 exchanges, ≥ 2 days | `ask number` / `offer_number`, accepted |
| `meeting` | numbers | warmth ≥ 45, trust ≥ 35 | a meet agreed (§14) |
| `dating` / `friends` / `fling` | meeting | 2 meets `good` or better; branch by both intents | automatic |
| `exclusive` | dating | warmth ≥ 80, trust ≥ 70, ≥ 4 weeks dating, both long-term | `ask exclusive` / `ask_exclusive`, accepted |
| `winding_down` | any live | a turn, or warmth < 15 for 5 days | event |
| (recovery) | winding_down | warmth ≥ `cold.recover_above` (starting point 30) for 2 days, and not after a turn's `then` | automatic, back to `stage_before` |
| `ended` | any | -- | `end` by the player; an arc's `then` |
| `faded` | any live | no contact either way for 21 days | event |
| `ghosted` | hidden: the visible stage stays | ghost decision | event |

**Ruling (v0.22.0 T9 review, controller):** `winding_down` is not a dead end.
A cold spell that warms again recovers to the stage it left; an arc's turn
(`then:`) does not recover this way, only by a `reconnect`.

Not an endgame: `exclusive` goes on accruing history and can wind down or
end. `faded` and `ended` can be re-opened by a `reconnect` initiation or by
the player writing; the engine decides whether they answer.

The player sees stage words ("Just matched", "Talking", "Swapped numbers",
"Seeing each other", "Dating", "Together", "Friends", "Cooling off", "Ended")
and a five-word closeness band from warmth and familiarity ("new", "warming",
"close", "very close", "inseparable"). Never a number; never "ghosted". The
card shows "Last heard from 9 days ago".

### 10.4 History

Engine events only (matched, first message, numbers, first voice note/photo/
video, a meet and its band, a fight (warmth −8 or worse in a day), a
reconciliation, a turn, ended/faded/reconnected), each with day and an engine
line; bounded at `social.history_max` (60), the oldest folded into one
"earlier" line. The card's "Your story"; the voice gets the last eight.

### 10.5 Proposals: what the model may ask the engine for

The schema's `proposals` enum is built per call from this table's rows whose
gate holds NOW, and each is re-checked at commit. No proposal moves a stage,
spends a budget or bypasses a draw by itself:

| Proposal | Gate | Engine decision | Draw | Effect |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `offer_number` | stage `talking`, §10.3's numbers gate | A pending request to the player; on `yes`, the gate re-checked, then stage `numbers` | none (the person chose) | `social_request`, then `social_stage` |
| `ask_meet` | stage ≥ `numbers`, meeting gate | The engine picks a venue (a shared-hobby kind) and a free slot from their schedule; a pending request | `SOCIAL_MEET` for venue ties | `social_request` |
| `offer_photo` / `offer_voice` / `offer_video` | budget ≥ 1, stage unlocked for the kind | An unsolicited-media draw at `media.yaml` `unsolicited.offered_mult` x the normal odds; on success, at commit, a NEW due message of that kind from them is queued (its own decision, its own voicing with a `media` key) and the budget is spent | `SOCIAL_MEDIA` | `social_media_decision` |
| `invite_game` | stage ≥ `talking`, no game running | A pending game invite | none | `social_request` |
| `ask_exclusive` | stage `dating`, exclusive gate | A pending request | none | `social_request`, then `social_stage` |
| `cool_off` | any live stage | Interest − `cool_off_step` (archetype, ≤ 5) | none | `social_regard` (interest) |

Ending, unmatching and winding down are never model proposals: they come from
arcs, archetypes and thresholds.

### 10.6 Ask pacing

One rule for every `ask` kind, as relationship pacing: each person counts
`asks_today` per kind; a second ask of any kind the same day lowers that
ask's accept odds by `relationships.yaml` `asks.repeat_mult` and costs
`asks.repeat_warmth`; a third is refused by the catalogue ("Give it a day").

### 10.7 Mood

`mood` is an enum from `relationships.yaml` `mood.values` (`neutral`, `fond`,
`playful`, `tired`, `annoyed`, `hurt`), set by the latest committed reply,
reverting to `neutral` after `mood.decay_hours`. Its only mechanical reads are
the media accept multiplier (bounded to `mood.media_mult` [0.3, 1.3]) and the
voice prompt.

## §11 — Voicing: the model writes what they say

### 11.1 Which messages, in what order

Only the voicing pass (§16.2) calls the model. Its pick order: due messages
in the thread with the highest priority (the last `open_thread` raises a
thread's `focus` stamp), then by warmth, then by age. One call voices one
person's whole due burst in one thread; never two people in one call.
`social.voice.max_per_pass` (config, 3) people per pass; the pass re-runs
while due messages remain.

An unvoiced message shows as a notification ("Kai: New message") and as "…"
in its thread until committed; typing dots appear only while its call is in
flight. §16.2's table gives every state the player sees.

### 11.2 The schema

`voicing.reply_schema(decision, proposals)` per call (`engine/llm/schemas.py`
patterns, `additionalProperties: false`):

```json
{
  "bubbles": [{"text": "string<=280"}],          // 1..N, N from the decision
  "media": {"caption": "string<=200"},           // only when the decision attached media
  "regard": {"warmth": -4..4, "trust": -2..2, "mood": enum},
  "learned": ["string<=140"],                    // <=2
  "shared": ["string<=140"],                     // <=2
  "proposals": [enum of §10.5's rows legal now]
}
```

The engine decides, BEFORE the call, what the reply is: text, a voice note
(the bubbles are its transcript), a photo or video with a caption, a decline,
an opener, a reveal. The `media` key exists only when media is attached, so
"I sent you a pic" over no photo is unsamplable; §11.5 catches it in prose.
Without a grammar, `render_format_block` and `conform` hold the shape
(v0.19.0's rungs).

### 11.3 The voicing prompt

`voicing.messages(snapshot)` composes exactly these blocks, in this order:

1. **The story's texter persona**: `prompts/texter.md`, verbatim.
2. **WHO YOU ARE**: the profile as second-person character notes, texting
   style as description, canon (§12.1).
3. **WHERE YOU ARE WITH THEM**: stage words, closeness band, mood, intent and
   what they believe the player wants, history's last eight rows.
4. **WHAT YOU KNOW ABOUT THEM**: the profile they saw, and `knows` (§12.2).
5. **THE CONVERSATION**: the thread summary, then the last
   `social.memory.verbatim` (24 in the prompt) bubbles with times.
6. **RIGHT NOW**: the time, their status, how long since each side wrote,
   read state, and the SITUATION line for this decision, taken from
   `texter.md`'s instruction table (`opener`, `reply`, `double_text`,
   `anxious_check_in`, `decline_photo`, `send_selfie`, `send_video`,
   `reveal_turn`, `post_meet`, `reconnect`, `game_banter`, ...), so the story
   owns every word.
7. **FORMAT**: the schema, or the format block.

### 11.4 The call and its profile

`get_backend().chat(messages, profile="texter", response_format=...,
reasoning="off", label="social_voice")`, not streamed (typing dots show
meanwhile). `config/default.yaml`:

```yaml
llm:
  profiles:
    texter:
      lane: narration
      require_tools: false
      min_context: 4096
      max_tokens: 360
      reasoning: "off"
      reasoning_budget: 0
      temperature: 0.9
      # model: "" -- inherits big's binding; set it to a smaller model for speed
```

`reasoning_budget: 0` is a starting point: a server with an untrusted
reasoning-off patch (CLAUDE.md's deferred row) may starve; T14 measures it on
whatever model the owner has loaded and records the number it sets (never
required to pass).

**Failure never blocks.** No server, a starved reply, or two failed agreement
checks: the due message is filled from `texter.md`'s per-archetype
`fallbacks:` (a seeded pick), regard zero, nothing learned, `voiced:
fallback`. A fallback is final: once committed it has been shown, so it is
never re-voiced.

### 11.5 Agreement checks

`agreement.check(reply, decision, state, pid)`, the phone's third audit
question, measured against fixtures of both kinds (A2's precision rule):

| Check | On | Fails when |
|---|---|---|
| Media claimed | bubbles | They say a photo/voice/video was sent ("sent you a pic", "listen to this") and none is attached |
| Media denied | bubbles | Media attached; the bubbles say they will not send it |
| Meeting claimed | bubbles | They agree to or state a meet `meets` has not agreed, or deny one it has |
| Stage claimed | bubbles | They name a stage not reached ("my girlfriend", "now we're exclusive"): a STATE check on `relationships.yaml` `claims:` word lists per stage |
| Profile contradicted | bubbles, `shared`, `learned` | Their age, job or neighbourhood stated otherwise; a `learned` fact contradicting the player's profile |
| Another person | bubbles, `shared`, `learned` | A name from the player's contacts this person cannot know (`cast.py`'s precision rules reused) |
| Number | bubbles | More bubbles than allowed |
| Decline wording | bubbles | A decline decision whose bubbles agree |

A failure retries once with a one-line correction; a second failure drops the
offending bubbles or facts if others remain, else falls back (§11.4). A
failing `shared`/`learned` fact is dropped, never stored. Failures are
recorded in the receipts.

### 11.6 Rule 12, pinned for every prompt

The story composes three prompts, each a bare `backend.chat` call (no
storyteller, no governance directives) with an exact block list:

| Prompt | Blocks | Test |
|---|---|---|
| Voicing | §11.3's seven | `test_the_voice_prompt_is_exactly_its_blocks` |
| Thread summary | system: `prompts/thread_summary.md` verbatim; user: CURRENT SUMMARY, NEW MESSAGES | `test_the_summary_prompt_is_exactly_its_blocks` |
| Date recap | system: `prompts/date_recap.md` verbatim; user: WHO, WHERE, HOW IT WENT (the band), TOPICS, FORMAT | `test_the_recap_prompt_is_exactly_its_blocks` |

Each test composes its prompt for a fixture and asserts the messages are
exactly those blocks, in order, with the story file verbatim and unprefixed.
Any added block fails it. No vocabulary list is kept: structure is pinned, not
words.

## §12 — Memory and context

### 12.1 Canon

Fixed canon is the generated profile. Growing canon is `shared` (after §11.5's
checks), up to two per reply, in `people[pid].canon` (`social.canon_max`, 40;
the oldest folded into the thread summary), fed back in block 2 every call. An
arc's `reveal` writes canon from data. Canon is prose for the voice only; no
mechanic reads it (§13.1 gates on `flags`).

### 12.2 What they know about the player

`learned` facts (after checks) in `people[pid].knows` (`social.knows_max`,
30). A person knows only what the player told THEM, plus the profile they saw:
no fact crosses threads.

### 12.3 The conversation

The prompt sees `verbatim` (24) bubbles; state keeps `social.state_messages`
(starting point 60; 24 since T26's size bound, §17.2) per thread (§17.2). Older ones fold into the thread's `summary` (≤ 180
words) through `engine/memory/summarizer.py`: its fallback compression and a
new `renderer` parameter (the thread renderer, "Tue 21:14 you: ...", not
`_render_turns`), on the `small` profile -- a UTILITY call, degradable: in
hosted mode it waits `hosting.utility_wait_seconds` and is then skipped, and
the deterministic fallback is the expected hosted path (tested). At most one
summary call per voicing pass, made after its replies commit, holding no lock;
the summary commits like a reply (§16.3).

### 12.4 Budget

Budgeted by `engine/memory/budget.py`: blocks 1, 6, 7 fixed; 2-5 share the
rest, trimmed in order: verbatim tail (to 12), history (to 4), canon (by
recency), summary. A test fits a 4096-token window with a 40-person save at
day 90.

## §13 — Media: photos, video and voice

### 13.1 Special events, by rule

`media.yaml`:

```yaml
budget:            # per person; refills on `daily`
  photo: {start: 0, per_day: 0.15, cap: 2, unlock_stage: talking}
  voice: {start: 0, per_day: 0.25, cap: 2, unlock_stage: talking}
  video: {start: 0, per_day: 0.05, cap: 1, unlock_stage: numbers}
request:
  accept: {base: 0.25, trust_per_10: 0.08, warmth_per_10: 0.04}
  later_share: 0.3
unsolicited:
  per_day_at_stage: {talking: 0.02, numbers: 0.06, dating: 0.1}
  offered_mult: 2.0           # §10.5
subjects:
  - {id: selfie_now, when: free}
  - {id: what_im_doing, when: [free, out]}
  - {id: view_from_work, when: work}
  - {id: pet, when: free, requires_flag: has_pet}
player_subjects: [...]
style: "..."                 # the story's image style suffix
```

A request (`ask {what: photo|voice|video}`) is decided by the engine: the
app capability, budget ≥ 1, stage unlocked, §10.6's pacing, then a draw on
accept odds x archetype `accept_mult` x mood's bounded multiplier; else a
decline or "later" (a pending request the engine keeps or breaks). Subjects
gate on fixed profile `flags`, never on canon text. Targets (§20): about one
photo per one to two weeks in a close relationship, voice notes about weekly
after numbers, video roughly monthly.

### 13.2 The job

A reply carrying media creates a row in `state.social.media[mid]`:

```
{mid, kind: photo|voice|video|portrait|own, pid, thread_id, message_id,
 subject, caption, seed, priority, account, status: requested|submitted|running|ready|failed|fallback,
 provider, prompt_id, attempts, url, poster_url, error, created_at, finished_at}
```

The message is delivered at commit with a placeholder tile (procedural, the
caption on it, "sending…") and swaps when the job lands. On resume every
unfinished job is resubmitted (idempotent: the same seed and prompt).

### 13.3 The image request

`media.photo_request(person, subject, caption)` builds the positive prompt
from the person's fixed `appearance`, the subject's scene, the model's caption
(≤ 200 chars, as written), time of day and status, and `media.yaml`'s
`style`. **No negative prompt** (v0.15.1), **no sanitiser, no classifier**
(rule 12): the only processing is the length cap.

**Seeds** come from `engine/game/rng.py::_mix` (blake2b): `_mix(seed,
f"{SOCIAL_MEDIA}:face:{pid}", 0)` for portraits, `_mix(seed,
f"{SOCIAL_MEDIA}:{pid}:{mid}", 0)` for sent media -- stable across processes.
When a workflow binds `reference_image`, the person's first generated portrait
is uploaded and bound, so later images keep the face (an IP-Adapter or similar
node in the operator's workflow; the shipped `photo` workflow needs none).

### 13.4 The ComfyUI pipeline, complete

A new package **`engine/media/comfy/`**; `providers/comfy.py` is rebuilt on
it:

| Module | Does |
|---|---|
| `client.py` | `ComfyClient`: base URL, auth header, `system_stats`, `upload_image`, `submit`, `history`, `fetch`, `interrupt`; every call bounded; `httpx` with `net.ssl_context()` |
| `workflows.py` | Loads an API-format JSON template (exported from ComfyUI unmodified) and its **bindings**, binds a request into it |
| `jobs.py` | `MediaJobWorker`: a priority queue, submit → poll → fetch → store → hand-off |
| `storage.py` | `storage.media_dir()/phone/<owner>/<save_id>/<mid>.<ext>`, `<owner>` = `local` in local mode |

**Who switches what (I2).** Phone media are ON exactly when
`phone_media.provider == comfyui`, and read only the `comfyui.*` CONNECTION
keys (`base_url`, `api_key`, `auth_header`, timeouts, `workflows`); they never
read `comfyui.enabled`. `comfyui.enabled` stays the art pipeline's
`ComfyProvider` switch. In the same task the old `ComfyUIClient`'s
`/prompt` submit of its one-node stub graph is **retired** (the graph produces
nothing; the class keeps its queue and prompt building), with a test that a
flagship turn under `comfyui.enabled: true` makes no HTTP request, and a
GOVERNANCE row for the in-turn queue that remains without a submit.

All five sampler keys are bound, so `photo_sdxl.json` reproduces
`build_workflow`'s graph exactly (a test compares the bound graph to the old
literal, node for node).

**The protocol** (`jobs.py`), each step a status and a log line with the job id:

1. **Build**: `workflows.bind`; a binding naming a missing node or input is a
   load-time ERROR.
2. **Upload** (only when bound): `POST /upload/image`.
3. **Submit**: `POST /prompt {"prompt": graph, "client_id": job_key}` →
   `prompt_id`; a 400 with `node_errors` is FAILED, errors kept, not retried.
4. **Poll**: `GET /history/{prompt_id}` every `poll_seconds`; an execution
   error is retried once; past `job_timeout_seconds`, `POST /interrupt`
   best-effort and FAILED.
5. **Collect**: the declared output node's files under any of `images`,
   `gifs`, `videos`, `audio`.
6. **Fetch**: `GET /view?...` → bytes, capped at `max_bytes`, type by magic
   bytes (PNG, JPEG, WEBP, MP4, WEBM); a video's poster is its first image
   output, else the procedural tile.
7. **Store**: atomic write (`engine/persistence/atomic.py`) to the
   owner-scoped path.
8. **Hand-off**: the result goes to the session through §16.4 (try the turn
   lock, else the inbox), after the liveness check of §16.2; applied by
   `social_media` (engine-only); emits **`media_ready`** (§17.3) through the
   room-checked `FlaskScene.emit_to_session` (§17.1).
9. **Failure**: after `retries`, `failed` → the procedural stand-in for good
   (`fallback`). The person still sent "a photo"; it did not load. Never a
   raise into anything.

**Scheduling (I8).** The worker's queue is ordered by priority -- 0 thread
media (photo, voice, video), 1 the player's own photos, 2 portraits -- then
round-robin by account, then age; one job at a time (`max_concurrent: 1`).
Portraits follow `phone_media.portraits`: `off` (procedural tiles always),
`on_match` (default: a person's portrait is generated when they match, so the
deck costs nothing), `on_view` (when their card is shown). `daily_job_cap` per
account turns further jobs into procedural tiles at once, logged once per day.
A test runs a 60-card session under `on_match` and asserts zero portrait jobs
before a match, and under `on_view` that submissions stop at the cap.

**Owner-scoped serving**: `GET /api/phone/media/<save_id>/<name>` resolves the
save through the session store and, hosted, the account gate (another
account's save id answers as missing); traversal refused as `send_generated`
does.

- **`procedural`** (default): `ProceduralProvider`'s tile with the caption; no

- **`stub`**: `tests/comfy_stub.py::StubComfy`, an `httpx.MockTransport`

- **`comfyui`**: the real thing. **Not verified live in this release unless
  the owner has a server up** during T17-T19; the task records which.
  docs/MEDIA_SERVERS.md (new) says what was and was not run.

### 13.6 Voice notes (TTS)

The existing Voxtral route through `TTSClient.synthesize(text, voice=...)` on
the `SpeechWorker`, off by default (`tts.enabled: false`). New:

- `tts.voices`: the four ids `engine/api/settings.py` already offers, tagged
  `{id, sex}` (`neutral_male` m, `neutral_female` f, `warm_female` f,

- **The cache-key fix (F7)**: `write_audio`, `cached_url` and `synthesize`

- A voice note is a `MediaJob` of kind `voice`, capped at

## §14 — Meet-ups

### 14.1 Proposing

`propose_meet {pid, venue, day, slot}`, or a person's `ask_meet` proposal (a
pending request the player answers). Venues from `venues.yaml` (`{id, label,
neighbourhood, kind, hobbies, duration_hours}`). The engine decides: accept if
gates hold and they are free in that slot; else a counter (the nearest free
slot); else "not yet". An agreed meet is a Calendar row and a `meet_start`
event.

### 14.2 The day

At `meet_start`, `wait` stops with a notification and the catalogue offers
`meet {go | cancel}`. `cancel` costs warmth and interest
(`relationships.yaml` `meets.cancel_cost`, larger the later). No answer by the
slot's end is a player no-show. A person's flake (`flake_prob`) is drawn when
the meet is agreed and shown at the start.

### 14.3 The outcome

`go` advances the clock by the venue's duration and resolves from
`SOCIAL_MEET` with `relationships.yaml` `meets:`:

```yaml
meets:
  quality: {compat: 1.0, chemistry: 1.0, warmth: 0.005, venue_hobby_bonus: 0.08, spread: 0.12}
  bands: {great: 0.75, good: 0.6, fine: 0.45, awkward: 0.3}   # else bad
  effects: {great: {warmth: 8, trust: 6, interest: 10}, ...}
  cancel_cost: {...}
```

Two `good`-or-better meets move `meeting` to its branch (§10.3); a history row
records it.

### 14.4 The recap and the texts after

The recap (≤ 120 words, second person) is written in a voicing pass (§16.2) by
one bare `chat` call on the **`texter`** profile, with the §11.6 recap blocks
and the present set {the person}. Its agreement check fails a recap whose tone
contradicts the band (A2's contradiction rule), retried once, else an engine
line ("Coffee at The Copper Kettle with Kai. It was a little awkward."). The
person's `post_meet` message is queued from their pace -- or, for `bad` or a
ghoster, not at all. The recap is a system card in the thread.

## §15 — Time, sessions and login

### 15.1 Time inside a session

The wall-clock tick is off. Time moves only by actions: each `cost_minutes`,
and **`wait`** -- `until: next` (the next notifying event, capped at
`social.wait_next_cap_hours`, 8), `15m`, `1h`, or `morning` (08:00, sleeping).

**"Let time pass while I watch"** (local mode only; off by default; a client
preference): after 30 real seconds idle on a thread whose person is typing or
due, it sends `wait {until: 15m}`, a player action like any other. In hosted
mode the payload says `auto_wait: false` and the client hides the preference:
each wait can create due messages and so a voicing admission.

### 15.2 The away advance

On resume (`SessionStore.resume`) in a story declaring `paths.social`,
`away.away_hours(state, now)`:

```
elapsed = now_wall - state.social.last_active_wall
hours   = min(elapsed / 3600 x social.away.rate, social.away.max_hours)
```

applied as ONE `advance_time(state, hours)` (fractional hours: the clock takes
floats, so no remainder is lost between resumes), recorded as `{kind: away,
hours, real_seconds}` in the transcript, then saved. `last_active_wall` is
stamped on every mechanics action and by the away advance itself, so a second
resume of the same save adds only the minutes between, never the day twice.

```yaml
social:
  away: {mode: real, rate: 1.0, max_hours: 72}     # mode: real | none
  voice:
    max_per_pass: 3
    workers: 2
    queue_max: 64
    admit_wait_seconds: 5
    max_attempts: 6
    pass_deadline_seconds: 300
    commit_retry_ms: [50, 100, 200, 400, 800]
  max_text_chars: 600
  memory: {verbatim: 24}
  state_messages: 60
  history_max: 60
  canon_max: 40
  knows_max: 30
  wait_next_cap_hours: 8
  max_events_per_advance: 2000
  autosave_seconds: 10
```

**Why this does not break R-03.** The away advance is one recorded input at a
defined moment; a replay from the inputs reproduces it. Within a session
nothing moves on the wall clock.

### 15.3 What the player sees on login

The resume frame (`default_state.resume_opening`, a phone branch) is the lock
screen: "While you were away: 5 messages from 3 people, 1 new match, 1 match
expired", and the notifications. The resume requests a voicing pass for the
due messages (§16.2), which re-enter the queue under the normal rules.

### 15.4 Real-clock alignment (ruled out)

The in-game clock runs free (a new run starts at 19:00, day 1). Aligning it to
the player's local time would make a seed's run depend on when it began;
ruled out (NOT WIRED, §22).

## §16 — The phone turn: mechanics, then voicing

The phone turn is two things that never hold the same lock at once: a fast
**mechanics action** the player waits for, and a **voicing pass** the player
never waits for.

### 16.1 The mechanics action

`default_scene`'s `phone_action` handler calls `run_guarded(session, ...,
runner=turn.run_mechanics, admit=False, metric="phone")`. The three keywords
are new on `run_guarded`, defaulting to today's behaviour (`runner=run_turn`,
`admit=True`, `metric="turn"`), so every other caller is unchanged: the same
non-blocking take of `GameSession.lock`, the same busy answer, the same
deadline and error handling, the same `end_after_turn` call after release.
With `admit=False` it **never calls `admit_turn`**: no admission and no model
call, so a swipe never enters the hosted narration queue. With
`metric="phone"` it reports a `phone` metric (`event: action`, §17.1), not a
`turn`, so swipes do not swamp the admission statistics.

`turn.run_mechanics(session, intent)`, under the lock:

1. **Drain the inbox** (§16.4), each item re-validated. *(Added in T14.)*
2. **Execute** the intent (`execute_phone_intent`): a receipt or a refusal;
   `cost_minutes` and any `wait` through `advance_time`, which walks the event
   queue and creates due messages.
3. **Create media jobs** for due messages decided to carry media, and portrait
   jobs per §13.4, and enqueue them; the worker holds no session lock. *(Added
   in T19.)*
4. **Mark dirty** and stamp `last_active_wall`; save at once (§17.2) after a
   match, a stage change, an ending or an away advance; otherwise the dirty
   timer saves within `social.autosave_seconds`.
5. **Emit** `turn_update` (`narration: ""`, the `phone` block).

After `run_guarded` releases the lock, the handler calls
`voicer.request(session)` when any due message is unvoiced.

Each later task adds its step to this one function; no task leaves a stub for
a later one to replace.

### 16.2 The voicing pass

`engine/social/voicer.py::VoicingScheduler`, a process singleton
(double-checked getter, `renew_after_fork`, classified in
`tests/fixtures/module_state.yaml`):

- **A bounded request queue** (`social.voice.queue_max`, 64 entries), one entry
  per live session object at most; a full queue drops the oldest entry with a
  log line (requests are idempotent: the next player action requests again).
- **`social.voice.workers` threads** (2), and a leaf lock
  (`VoicingScheduler._lock`, in `engine/locks.py`'s leaf list and
  `tests/lock_order.py`) over the queue and the in-flight table; its holder
  takes nothing else.
- **Bound to the session OBJECT, never its id.** An entry holds the
  `GameSession` and its `epoch`, a new field on `GameSession` set from a
  process-wide counter at every `_build` (create or resume). The in-flight
  table is keyed by `(session_id, epoch)`, so a resumed run (same id, new
  object) never shares, reruns or inherits an old pass.

**Liveness**, checked before admission, before the snapshot and before every
commit: `store.get(session.session_id, session.owner) is session` and
`session.epoch == pass.epoch` and `not session.ending`. When it fails, the
pass ENDS: nothing committed, nothing saved, no re-queue, and its reference to
the session dropped so a released engine can be collected.

A pass:

1. **Admit** (hosted only), holding no lock: `admit_turn(label="voice",
   account=session.owner, deadline_seconds=hosting.turn_deadline_seconds,
   cancel=pass.cancel)`, with a SHORT wait, `social.voice.admit_wait_seconds`
   (5). Busy → the pass gives its thread back and is re-queued with backoff
   (2, 4, 8, 16, 32 s); after `social.voice.max_attempts` (6) attempts it is
   DROPPED. Its messages stay due, unmarked (nothing was marked yet), and the
   next player action or resume requests a new pass. A thread is held at most
   `admit_wait_seconds` per attempt, so blocked passes cannot starve the pool.
   Local mode has no admission; the `texter` call takes its lane as every
   local call does.
2. **Snapshot**: try the turn lock (non-blocking, on the
   `social.voice.commit_retry_ms` schedule; all busy → re-queue as in step 1,
   counted against the same attempts). Under it, READ the due messages to
   voice (§11.1's priority) and, per person, the regenerated profile, a deep
   copy of their dynamic slice (`rev` included), the thread tail and summary,
   each due message's decision and situation, and the proposals legal now.
   Then MARK those messages in `session.voicing` -- an in-memory map on the
   `GameSession` (`message_id -> pass id`), **never in `GameState`, never in a
   save**. Release; `end_after_turn` if set.
3. **Call** the model per person, sequentially, holding no lock; the payload's
   `typing` for a thread is true while a call for it is in flight
   (`session.typing`, in memory). Prompt, call, conform, agreement checks
   (pure over the snapshot), retry, fallback.
4. **Commit** each reply as it lands (§16.3) and emit `phone_message` through
   the room-checked emitter (§17.1).
5. **Summarise** at most one thread past its limit (§12.3), committed the same
   way.
6. **Finish**: release the admission; clear this pass's marks (under the lock
   at its last commit, or by the next lock holder if none: marks of a pass no
   longer in flight are ignored and pruned); if `rerun` was set, re-queue.

**Bounds.** Each pass has `pass.cancel` (an `engine/llm/gate.py::CancelToken`)
and a deadline, `social.voice.pass_deadline_seconds` (300; hosted, also
`hosting.turn_deadline_seconds`, whichever is sooner). Past the deadline the
remaining people in the pass get fallback lines at commit. `cancel` is fired
by: the store's release of that session (§16.5), a hosted drain or shutdown
(§17.1), and, hosted only, the session's room emptying (the disconnect
handler, when no socket remains in the room). A cancelled pass makes no
further call and commits nothing more. A model call already sent cannot be
interrupted; its answer is discarded when it returns.

**Unvoiced messages on load (the rule).** Since marks are never saved, a save
reloads with every unvoiced due message simply due (`voiced: false`). On
resume they **re-enter the voicing queue under the normal rules**: the resume
requests a pass, as any action does. Nothing can be stuck "typing".

**What the player sees**, local and hosted alike:

| State | Thread shows | Notification |
|---|---|---|
| Due, not in a pass (incl. queued for admission) | "…" (an unvoiced message) | "Kai: New message" |
| A call for that person in flight | typing dots | unchanged |
| Committed | the bubbles, one by one | the preview |
| Deadline passed or two failures | the fallback line | the preview |

The player's other actions are never blocked by a pass: it holds the turn
lock only for its snapshot and each commit. A mechanics action that finds the
lock held answers busy; the client re-sends once after 150 ms and, on a second
busy, keeps the composed text and attachment and shows "Not sent — tap to
retry" (§18.2).

**`voicer.run_until_quiet(session, llm_fn)`**: the same pass code run
synchronously on the caller's thread, no scheduler and no admission, until no
due message is unvoiced. The replay test, the simulator and
`screenshot_runs.py` drive voicing through it, so their results never depend
on thread timing.

### 16.3 The commit: re-validation, not a stale write

Under the turn lock (tried on the retry schedule; still busy → the reply goes
to the inbox, §16.4), for one voiced reply:

1. **Liveness** (§16.2). Fails → discard, end the pass.
2. **Is it still wanted?** The due message exists, is marked by this pass in
   `session.voicing`, and is `voiced: false`; the person is not unmatched,
   blocked or ended since; the thread exists. Else discard the reply (logged
   with both `rev`s).
3. **Re-check what depends on state.** Proposals intersected with §10.5's rows
   legal NOW; the state-dependent agreement checks (stage claimed, meeting
   claimed, another person) re-run against the live state; a newly failing
   bubble dropped, and with none left a fallback. Regard clamped against the
   per-day cap of the in-game day NOW (§10.2).
4. **Apply** through `apply_effect` in one `StateTransaction`:
   `social_voiced` (the text), `social_regard`, `social_memory`, each passing
   proposal's effect (an `offer_*` that passes queues a NEW due message of
   that media kind from them, §10.5). Bumps `rev`.
5. Mark dirty; release the lock; call `session.end_after_turn` if set, as
   `run_guarded` does.

A `rev` that moved since the snapshot is not by itself a discard: texts cross,
and a reply to the snapshot's conversation is still a reply. What must be true
is re-checked; what may have moved is allowed.

### 16.4 The session inbox (no leaf use of the turn lock)

`engine/social/inbox.py::SessionInbox`, one per `GameSession` object, a deque
under its own leaf lock (`SessionInbox._lock`, in the leaf list and
`tests/lock_order.py`; its holder takes nothing). A thread that must write a
session but cannot get its turn lock -- the media worker's hand-off (§13.4
step 8), a commit past its retries -- parks `{kind, payload, epoch, account}`
there, after the liveness check. It is drained under the turn lock by the
next mechanics action (§16.1 step 1) and the next pass's snapshot; each item
is re-validated as §16.3 does (its epoch equal to the session's; a media
result's account equal to the session's owner) before `apply_effect`. A
released session's inbox dies with it: its media jobs are rows in the last
save and resubmit on resume (§13.2); its voiced replies were never shown and
are re-voiced from the save's due messages.

### 16.5 Release, end, drain and shutdown

- **The store's release** (`SessionStore._release`, called by `delete`,
  `end`, `sweep_idle` and the one-run-per-account `_take_owned` path) gains a
  call `voicer.forget(session_id)`: it fires `cancel` on every in-flight or
  queued pass whose `(session_id, epoch)` no longer matches a live store entry
  and drops their references. (The liveness check makes this an optimisation;
  correctness does not depend on it.)
- **Flush on take** (local idle sweep and hosted one-run-per-account): the
  thread that takes a session's turn lock for good -- `sweep_idle` after its
  pop, and `_take_owned`'s callers (`_build` in hosted mode) after `_guard` is
  released -- calls `social.saves.flush_taken(session)` while still holding
  that lock: if dirty, it saves through `default_state._autosave` (which
  refuses when `session.ending`) after checking `store.owner_open(owner)`
  (hosted), exactly the two checks a turn's save and `SessionStore.owner_write`
  make. An admin `end`/`end_owner` never flushes (it marks `ending`), so a
  purge is never undone. In a story without `paths.social` the call is
  absent.
- **Hosted drain** (`engine/hosting/__init__.py::drain`) first fires `cancel`
  on every pass (`voicer.cancel_all()`), then waits, inside its `seconds`, for
  `store.turns_running() == 0` AND `voicer.in_flight() == 0`. A cancelled pass
  commits nothing further, so drain waits at most for one model call already
  sent. **Drain cancels passes, and counts the ones still finishing.**
- **Shutdown** (local and hosted) runs the same cancel, then the dirty timer's
  `flush_all()` (§17.2) with the same checks.

## §17 — Hosted mode, saves and the payload

### 17.1 Hosted

- **No admission for mechanics** (§16.1); a pass takes one, short-waited,
  re-queued, bounded (§16.2). One player's phone holds at most one narration
  ticket at a time; the summary is a degradable utility call (§12.3).
- **A rate bucket of its own.** `phone_action` and `POST /api/phone/action`
  do NOT join `engine/hosting/sockets.py::ACTION_EVENTS` (whose bucket,
  `actions_per_minute: 12`, would cap a player at twelve swipes a minute).
  They draw from a new per-account bucket,
  **`hosting.rate_limits.phone_actions_per_minute: 90`**, on socket and HTTP
  alike, through the same `engine/hosting/limits.py` machinery. Why 90: a
  swipe deck read at a glance runs at about one card a second (60 a minute,
  which is also Matchstick's whole daily allowance -- a player cannot sustain
  it past one minute), sends run far slower, and 90 leaves half again for
  bursts of opens and backs. T30 times a hand session in the pane and records
  the p95 cadence; if it exceeds 75% of 90, the number is raised in config.
- **The text cap.** `send.text` ≤ `social.max_text_chars` (600) and
  `like.comment` ≤ 140, checked by the catalogue's payload schema in every
  mode; hosted, every text field of `phone_action` is also put through
  `sockets.over_cap` against `hosting.max_input_chars` (1000) like any action
  event. Over the cap is refused, never cut.
- **Background pushes are room-checked.** `phone_message`, `media_ready` and
  any other emit from the voicing or media threads go through one helper,
  `FlaskScene.emit_to_session(session_id, event, payload)`, which runs the
  same `_room_check` a turn's `_emit` runs (default_scene's
  `on_player_choice`) before `emit(..., room=session_id)`. So a login revoked
  elsewhere stops receiving the run at once, as it does for turns.
- `MediaJobWorker` is a process singleton (double-checked getter, its lock in
  the leaf list, `renew_after_fork`, `module_state.yaml`), scheduling by
  priority and account round-robin with a per-account daily cap (§13.4).
- The admin panel shows no phone text (`tests/test_admin_no_play_text.py`
  extended to a phone save).
- **Metrics**: one new kind in `engine/hosting/metrics_schema.py::KINDS`,
  `phone`, fields `{ts: TIMESTAMP, account: ACCOUNT, story: STORY, event: ENUM
  {action, voice_pass, media_job}, status: ENUM {ok, busy, refused, fallback,
  failed, discarded, dropped}, ms: MILLISECONDS}`; `tests/test_metrics_schema.py`'s
  pin updated. A mechanics action is `event: action`; it is never a `turn`.

### 17.2 Saves

`state.social` is a plain dict on `GameState` (the `law` pattern: absent loads
as empty; no save-version bump). Nothing transient is in it: voicing marks and
typing live on the `GameSession` (§16.2). State keeps `social.state_messages`
(starting point 60; 24 since T26) per thread, and a dead thread none (T26: its
messages all go, a stamp of its last one stays, `archive.dead`); older messages move to an append-only **`phone_archive.jsonl`**
in the save directory, written by `SaveStore.save` before the envelope's
atomic replace (a crash leaves at worst a duplicate line, deduplicated by
message id on read). `GET /api/phone/thread/<thread_id>?before=<id>&limit=50`
pages through state then archive, read-only, owner-checked. Media files are
deleted with the save.

**When the phone saves.** Every save goes through `default_state._autosave`
(its `session.ending` refusal) and, hosted, the `owner_open` check
`SessionStore.owner_write` makes, always holding the turn lock:

- at once, inside the mechanics action, after a match, a stage change, an
  ending or an away advance (§16.1 step 4);
- at the end of each voicing pass that committed anything, under its last
  commit's lock;
- otherwise by **the dirty timer**: `engine/social/saves.py::DirtySaver`, a
  process singleton with one thread and a heap of `(due, session, epoch)`;
  marking dirty schedules the session at `now + social.autosave_seconds` (10).
  When due, it checks liveness (§16.2), tries the turn lock non-blocking
  (busy → again in 1 s), saves if still dirty, releases and calls
  `end_after_turn`. So a swipe with no follow-up is on disk within
  `autosave_seconds` (plus any busy retry);
- on take and on shutdown (§16.5: `flush_taken`, `flush_all`).

A hard crash (a killed process) loses at most the dirty mechanics of the last
`autosave_seconds`; anything else is saved.

**The size bound is measured, then pinned, after the last task that adds
state** (T26, after meet-ups): the simulator writes a day-180 `attentive` save
holding media rows, games and meets, records `save.json`'s size, and the test
pins it plus 25%. The design expectation is well under 1 MB.

### 17.3 The payload

`to_client_dict` gains `phone` ONLY for a story declaring `paths.social`
(byte-identical elsewhere, asserted for every shipped story):

```
phone: {
  now: {day, hour, minute, weekday},
  auto_wait: bool,
  apps: [{id, label, kind, badge}],
  notifications: [{id, app, thread_id, pid, kind, preview, at}],     # newest 30
  threads: [{thread_id, app, pid, name, avatar_url, stage_words, band,
             last: {from, kind, preview, at}, unread, typing, read_state}],
  open_thread: {thread_id, messages: [last 60], more: bool} | null,
  deck: {app, card: {...}, likes_left} | null,
  likes_you: [...], requests: [...], calendar: [...], games: [...],
  player: {...},
  refusal: {...} | null
}
```

**CURRENT (v0.22.0 T28):** each `apps` row also carries `capabilities`
(apps.yaml's list: what a thread on it can carry, which the compose bar
offers); `player` also carries `camera_roll: [{id, label}]` and `subjects:
[{id, kinds, label}]` (what the player may attach, §13.7), each fenced so a
fault empties that field only; a meet-up row of `requests` also carries
`venue_label` and `when` ("Fri 19:00"), worded as `calendar` words them.

**CURRENT (v0.22.0 T29):** the block also carries `contacts` (per person with
a listed thread: `{pid, name, avatar_url, photos, profile, app, app_label,
thread_ids, open, matched, matched_on, stage_words, band, history: [{day, at, text}],
about: <their canon>, last_heard_days}`, time-varying), `gallery` (received
photos and videos once their message is voiced: the media view plus `{pid,
name, thread_id, message_id, at}`, time-varying), `meet_options` (`{venues:
[{id, label, place, neighbourhood}], slots: [{id, start}], days: [1, 7]}`)
and `settings` (`{receipts, bio_max}`); `auto_wait` is `not hosting.enabled` -- whether
the client may offer the preference, which the client keeps (§15.1).

`phone_message` (a background push) carries `{message, thread: <that thread's
row>, badges: {app: n}, notification: {...} | null}`, so the thread list, the
home badges and the lock screen stay current between actions. `media_ready`
carries `{message_id, mid, url, poster_url, kind, thread}`.

Hidden values (warmth, interest, trust, mood, ghosted, arc, rev) never leave
the server; `tests/test_ui_contract.py`'s key scan gains the `phone` block and
both push payloads. `typing` is read from the session's in-memory map.

  memory per worker and records it.
- **Moves** are `game_move` intents; the engine validates placement, every
  word formed, scores (premiums, the 7-tile bonus), and refuses with reasons.
- **The person's move**: anchor-based generation over the prefix set, every
  legal move scored, sorted canonically, one picked by `games.skill` from the
  top `k` via `SOCIAL_GAME`, bounded by a NODE budget (`games.yaml`
  `wordgame.node_budget`). Tests assert the budget and the move's legality;
  the wall time is recorded, never asserted (CI's runner). They play on their
  own clock (a `game_move` event); a move may bring a `game_banter` message.
- **Effect**: a finished game adds familiarity and interest for both; a
  crushing win over a `needy` person costs a little warmth (`games.yaml`).

### 21.2 This or That

`engine/social/games/this_or_that.py`: five either/or pairs drawn from a
`games.yaml` bank; the player answers with one `game_move`; the person's
answers are derived from their traits, hobbies and personality plus seeded
noise (`SOCIAL_GAME`), on their own clock. The reveal records the agreements
as canon both ways (engine text, "You both pick mountains over beaches") and
queues their reaction as a due message (`game_banter`). Agreement count adds
familiarity and interest (`games.yaml`).
