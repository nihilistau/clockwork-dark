# v0.25.0 — The Clockwork Dark overhaul — design

Status: **written 2026-10-27, reviewed (opus, approved with fixes), revised the same day
(Revision 1, at the end of this file, maps each of the review's 29 findings to its fix), then
build.** The owner does not approve an
overhaul's design (CLAUDE.md, the 2026-09-26 re-cut): this file is written, reviewed and built.
Where it and the code later disagree, the code wins (AGENTS.md authority order) and this file is
corrected.

This is the first of the four overhauls that give an older story "the full HUE & CRY treatment"
(docs/superpowers/specs/2026-09-23-hue-and-cry-design.md is the model): its story and characters,
the engine systems apt to it, every ending reachable through its own door and tested, measured
balance, reviews, UI screens and art. The flagship is the oldest story in the repository and the
one every other graph story was measured against, so most of its parts already exist. The work is
therefore less "build a story" than "make the one that exists hold together": doors a player can
walk through without the narrator's help, a calendar that does not run on the wall clock, overlays
that do what they say, and a cast and a map that match the canon the lore already wrote.

**Rule 12.** Nothing here rates content. No intensity, no ceiling, no fade, no `safety:` block, no
register line in any prompt. The register of `games/clockwork-dark/prompts/storyteller.md` is the
owner's and the loaded model's, and this design edits that prompt only to name the cast and the
places (§3).

**Canon.** Nothing in AGENTS.md's canon list is renamed or removed: the agents
`clockwork_storyteller` and `clockwork_assistant`; the locations `forest_clearing`,
`edgewood_square`, `edgewood_bakery`, `tinker_caravan`, `millhaven_gate`; the phases `dormant`,
`stirring`, `spreading`, `consuming`; the arcs `quiet_life`, `whisper`, `march`, `convergence`;
the seven skills and the six difficulty bands. The eight ending ids, the 25 quest ids, the five
canon NPC ids and every item id stay too (saves and the canon dictionary name them). Everything
below is additive or an edit inside an existing id.

## Goal

When v0.25.0 ships:

1. **Every one of the eight endings is earned through its own door, and every door can be walked
   without the narrator.** Each ending is locked by name by one authored choice (a card beat) or
   by the terminal death, in a place where its person stands at that hour (`npc_here`, §6.6). No
   ending depends on a model choosing to offer a `flag` intent, and no id-less `ending_lock`
   remains in the story. Every stage on every ending's path completes by a card, a place, an hour,
   an item, a day, a stat or an engine-written standing (§4.4's table), never by a narrative flag
   alone and never by the model-written disposition.
2. **Every ending is reachable, and measured.** A doors harness plays one policy per ending over
   40 seeds and reports which ending each run reached, when, and at what cost; the bands are
   written down before any constant moves (rule 10).
3. **The calendar no longer runs on the wall clock's tick count.** The caravan, the tinker and
   the press are rolled once per in-game day crossed, on NEW streams no background tick touches
   (§6.1), inside `clock.advance_time`, so the Whisper arc's door (which waits on the caravan)
   replays from the seed, the choices and the hours crossed. (Hours are still partly crossed by the
   background tick, `default_state._background_tick`, which is wall-clock by design: the rolls no
   longer depend on how those hours were cut, not on where they came from.)
4. **Every overlay action is a legal intent the server checks** (rule 1). Buying, selling, eating,
   using, wearing, crafting and taking posted work from the flagship's overlays run their skills;
   an action not on offer is not pressable, and one that went stale is refused before or by the
   turn, in words.
5. **The flagship's UI debts are paid:** the encounter's look and digit keys, faction names on the
   sheet, a screen that shows the ways the story can end once they are open, and the ending screen
   drawing its plate instead of printing an art key.
6. **The world matches its own lore:** Hallam, Nessa Fallow, old Wenna and Edrin Fallow are people
   with routines; the tunnels the doom clock opens and the join on the inward road are places;
   every road the map marks `# needs band` can produce an encounter.
7. **The six bugs found while reading are fixed** (§1.4): leaving the ninth pin no longer gives
   you the ninth pin; the sacrifice takes the thing you chose to pay; Nessa has one surname; the
   tunnels' authored door leads somewhere; the ending screen draws a plate, not a key; and the
   sealed seam's riddle counts its own answer's letters.
8. **Art is approved and live play is gated on the owner** (§9, §16): the owner approved the art
   pack on 2026-10-27 (47 plates through the `xai` provider), so T15 is an ordinary task; play
   with a model still runs only on the owner's go-ahead.

## Non-goals

- **The doom clock is not redesigned.** `evil_progress`, the four phases, the base rate (0.028),
  `doom_resistance`, the engagement factor and the five doom beats stay as they are. The HUE & CRY
  spec's non-goal "converting the flagship's doom clock into an agenda" stands.
- **No Law, premises, jobs or agendas** (§5.2 says why for each), and no `paths.social`.
- **No balance constant changes before the doors harness has measured it** (rule 10). Every number
  this file introduces is a first value, marked unmeasured, until T10.
- **No change to any other story's turns.** Byte-identity is asserted (§11.3). The Wicked Garden's
  and Dev Story's use of the three legacy wall-clock event slots is left exactly as it is (§6.1),
  and so is `WorldSim.on_tick`'s eager draw on the three legacy streams; the Garden's own overhaul
  (v0.27.0) converts its slots. Dev Story is not converted or retired in this release.
- **No change to the narrator's grammar for any story.** The new overlay-only verbs (§7) never
  enter `legal_intents`, and the overlay's uncapped lists are built beside the capped catalogue,
  not into it, so no story's turn schema or chip catalogue moves, the flagship's included.
- **The companion is unchanged.** `clockwork_assistant` stays `pipeline: false` with its five
  forms and its hint tiers; the hints file's own rule ("NEVER a quest marker") keeps it from
  pointing at doors.
- **Hidden stats stay hidden on screen.** No awareness meter, no doom number on screen; the new
  endings screen shows only doors that are already open (§8.4). That hiding is the client's: the
  payload's `endings` block keeps shipping every tier's title, tease and gate as it does for every
  story, because withholding them in `endings.to_client` would move HUE & CRY's payload. The
  flagship's teases are written to be safe in the payload, and the gap is a NOT WIRED row (§14).
- **NEON CITY's paper overlay and The Wicked Garden's contracts overlay** keep sending typed text;
  theirs are the v0.28.0 and v0.27.0 overhauls'. Core's `onAct` accepts both shapes (§7.4).
- **Not fixed here:** survival's hunger and death are not cut-invariant (engine-wide, §13); a
  choice whose text promises an action but carries no intent (engine-wide, §13).
- **No new archetypes, skills, factions or recipes**, no New Game+ (its seeds stay unread), and no
  sound cue beyond what v0.24.0 shipped.

### 1.2 Endings depend on the narrator

Every ending's gate reads quest completion, and every quest on an ending's path completes on a
narrative flag that only the `flag` intent sets. The narrator offers that verb when it chooses to
(`intents._flag`). `scripts/simulate.py`'s policies raise every pending flag themselves
(`_pending_flag`), so the balance table has never measured the thing a player depends on: a model
that offers the beat. HUE & CRY solved the same problem with decks whose cards ARE the choices;
the flagship has none.

### 1.3 The calendar runs on the wall clock

`caravan_arrival`, `tinker_camp` and `militia_press` are rolled by
`engine/world/schedules.py::ScheduleRoll`, called only from `WorldSim.on_tick`, the background
tick that `default_state._background_tick` runs from real elapsed time. Each tick rolls the
**per-day** probability once whatever hours it covers, so the caravan's real rate depends on how
many ticks a day is cut into, which depends on how long the player sat on the menu (issue R-03's
class). The draw itself is eager: `on_tick` calls `world_rng(state, SCHEDULE_CARAVAN)` (and the
tinker's and militia's) on every tick before `check_*` looks for a config block
(`engine/world/world_sim.py:250-272`), and `world_rng` advances the stream's counter on every
call (`engine/game/rng.py:120-132`), so those three counters count real-time ticks in every
story, whether or not it declares the slots. The Whisper arc's gate is `event_seen:
caravan_arrival` plus three days (`days_since_event: 3`), so the second arc of the flagship opens
on wall-clock time. The same three slots are the only events The Wicked
Garden and Dev Story declare (their `schedules.yaml` headers call them "the engine's three event
slots").

### 1.4 Content bugs found while reading

1. **Leaving the ninth pin gives you the ninth pin.** `the_ninth_pin`'s stage `take_it_or_dont`
   completes on `nf_ninth_taken` OR `nf_ninth_left`, and its `on_complete` grants `ninth_pin`
   unconditionally.
2. **The sacrifice always takes the written name.** `the_sacrifice`'s `choose_what` accepts the
   written name, the ninth pin or a brass shard; `pay_it` then requires `not_has_item:
   the_written_name` and its `on_complete` removes the written name. A player who chose the shard
   and still carries the name can never pay; one who chose the pin pays the name instead.
3. **Nessa has two surnames.** The lore (`the_nine_count.md`) says Nessa Fallow;
   `childs_drawing.yaml` calls her "the Weller girl" twice (its `summary`, line 7, and `see_it`'s
   objective, line 21) and its ledger fact says "Nessa Weller" (line 57). No epilogue names her.
   The Wellers are a real family elsewhere (`lost_goat.yaml`'s goat and boy, `militia_press.yaml`'s
   Weller boy) and keep their name; only the three lines that mean Nessa change.
4. **The tunnels' door leads nowhere.** The doom beat `tunnels_open` (0.70) IS read: it gates the
   `sealed_seam` set-piece at `forest_clearing` (`data/challenges/edgewood.yaml:62-90`). What
   leads nowhere is what lies past it: the flag that puzzle grants, `seam_opened`, and the beat's
   `discovery_hidden_path` are read by no place, path or quest.
5. **The ending screen prints a key.** Core's `Ending.jsx` renders "Added to the gallery:
   `banked_oven`" (the epilogue index's `gallery_key`), a raw art key, for every flagship ending.
6. **The seam's riddle miscounts.** `sealed_seam`'s prompt asks for "six letters", and its
   `answer` is `time`. The prompt becomes "four letters".

## §3 — The story

### 3.1 Premise

Unchanged in substance, sharpened in shape. You wake beneath birch trees at the forest's edge
beside Edgewood, the last comfortable village before the Marches. Something patient and brass is
working outward from the Wound: not a demon but a pattern that converts living order into ticking
mechanism, crossing one ring every nine days (the Nine Count, `data/lore/the_nine_count.md`). You
are never required to stop it. You may take the bakery's work and never learn its name; you may
listen, and keep a list; you may stand where the militia counts people; you may look at the shape
long enough to name it, and then pay for the naming. The quiet life is a complete game.

The register stays the storyteller prompt's: pastoral, grounded, wry in the small things, and
honest that the evil ticks either way.

### 3.2 Three acts, following the phases

No act is a gate of its own: the arcs and phases already gate, and an act is how the spec, the
prompt and the harness talk about where a run is.

|---|---|---|---|

| **II — The Count** | `stirring` (0.2-0.5) | Whisper and March: Nessa's drawings, the wrong map, ward charms; Sera's levy and its clock (§5.1), the press in the square, Sera's bargain; the barrows; the first doom beats | The Long Witness; The Counted March |

## §4 — The endings and their doors

### 4.1 The rule

- **Each ending is locked by name** (`{type: ending_lock, ending: <id>}`) in exactly the place
  listed below, and its module plays on the same beat (`{type: ending_module}`). No id-less lock
  remains in the story (asserted, §12.2). The finale quests that carried one —
  `banking_the_fire` and `the_last_quiet_thing` — lose it; `the_sacrifice`'s quest-level lock
  moves onto its card (the card can lock because the gate below reads the flags the same beat sets
  first, before the lock effect runs).
- **Each door is a deck card** in a repeatable deck whose `when:` puts it where its person IS at
  that hour: every door deck with a person reads `npc_here: <id>` (§6.6), which asks `npc_sim`
  itself, so a quest pin, a world event or a doom beat that moves the person moves the door with
  them. Each door card is gated on its own ending (`{ending: {eligible: <id>}}`, the HUE & CRY
  pattern, AUTHORING §3.5), so a door and its gate cannot drift apart; the one exception is `S2`,
  whose ending's `paid` clause is the beat itself, so it is gated on its stage (§4.3). "Not yet" is
  never the last chance: the deck re-arms when the player walks out (`director.rearm`).
- **One door per arming.** Every deck in this story is `draw: 1`, all its cards in the pool. Two
  endings that can be open in one place at one time are either beats of one card (`H1`'s `stay`
  and `stay_anyway`, made exclusive by beat-level `when:`, §6.5) or cards made exclusive by their
  `when:` (`H1` is not dealt while The Named Wound is eligible, so `Q1` and `H1` never compete in
  `the_morning_firing`).
- **The fail-forward has two doors**: the terminal death (§6.3), and "stay anyway" at the morning
  firing for a player whose Hearth gate is shut.
- **A stage carried by a beat's effect loses its narrative flag.** Where a card now carries what a
  stage's `on_complete` used to give (the pin, the removed item, the lock), the narrator must not
  be able to complete that stage without it, so the flag leaves the stage's `narrative_flags`:
  `nf_ninth_taken` and `nf_ninth_left` (`take_it_or_dont`), `nf_sacrifice_chosen` (`choose_what`)
  and `nf_sacrifice_paid` (`pay_it`). The cards write them; `complete_when` still reads them, so
  `tests/test_quests.py::test_every_narrative_flag_is_reachable_from_some_stage` (declared within
  read) holds. The T14 review found three more of the class: `nf_hearth_book_closed`
  (`banking_the_fire`'s `the_question`; H1's beats lock the ending the stage no longer locks) and
  `nf_press_spoke_up` and `nf_press_gave_name` (`militia_press`'s `the_weller_boy`; L2's beats carry
  reputation shifts the stage lacks); they left their stages too. Every other stage keeps its
  narrative flags: the narrator may still raise them, and whichever comes first wins, because no
  other stage's card carries an effect the stage lacks (`the_last_quiet_thing`'s Q1 locks The Named
  Wound, but Q1 is gated on the ending alone, so a narrated completion leaves its door open).
- **Hours are half-open** everywhere in this file: `[a, b]` is `hour_between: [a, b]`, a through
  b−1 (`quests.py:566`).

### 4.2 The eight endings and the ten doors

Gates rewritten where the old one read a flag only the narrator could raise or a door that no

### 6.1 E1 — Probabilistic declared world events, on the day roll

`engine/world/schedules.py::declared_events_due` gains a probabilistic form. A declared event with
`probability_per_day` is rolled **once per in-game day crossed** in `(from_day, to_day]` on which
it is eligible, inside `advance_time`'s day roll, on its own stream:

```yaml
events:
  caravan_arrival:
    probability_per_day: 0.83      # one draw per eligible day crossed (first value: §6.1 "Rates")
    min_day: 5                     # eligible from this day
    when: { min_awareness: 0 }     # optional; asked on each day, NOT once (no fired flag)
    stream: event.caravan_arrival  # optional; default "event.<event_id>"
    duration_days: 2
    location_id: edgewood_square
    npc_ids: [npc_odran]
    rumor_from: npc_odran          # optional: a tiered rumour attributed to this NPC
    text: "Odran's cart comes in off the Millhaven road with the dust still on it."
```

- **New streams, never the legacy three.** The default stream is `event.<event_id>`. The three
  legacy names (`schedule.caravan`, `schedule.tinker`, `schedule.militia`, `engine/game/rng.py:34-36`)
  are refused as a `stream:` by the loader and the validator, because `WorldSim.on_tick` draws them
  eagerly on every background tick in every story (§1.3), so their counters count real-time ticks.
  `on_tick` is not touched: its eager draws are pinned by the other stories' save digests (§11.3).
  A v0.24.0 flagship save gains three fresh streams at counter 0, which is harmless (a stream's
  counter is read by nothing but its own draws), and its three legacy counters go on counting
  ticks, read by nothing.
- **Eligible** = `day >= min_day`, `when` (if any) holds, and the event is not active ON THAT DAY.
  Activity is computed per day, inside the loop: an event is active on day d when some instance of
  it started on or before d and its `expires_day` is after d. The instances are the ones
  `state.world_events` held BEFORE this advance's `WorldSim.expire_events` (`advance_time` takes a
  `(event_id, expires_day)` snapshot of probabilistic events just before it expires them, and passes
  it in), plus the ones drawn earlier in this same call (drawn on d with `duration_days` n: active
  d through d+n−1). So a 48-hour sleep cannot draw the caravan on day 6 and again on day 7, and an
  event that expires inside the span is still counted as active on its last days. (The review read
  the expiry the other way round: `expire_events` runs at the span's END day, before the loop, so
  an event expiring mid-span was REMOVED and allowed extra draws on days it was still active. The
  fix is the same snapshot.)
- One `world_rng(state, stream)` draw per eligible day; an ineligible day draws nothing. So the
  draws depend only on the seed, the days crossed and the choices, never on how the hours were cut
  into steps or ticks.
- A probabilistic event **repeats**: once expired it is eligible again. A `when:` on it is a gate,
  not a once-only trigger, so it sets no `event_fired_` flag. Inside one multi-day step `when` is
  read against the state as the step ends; the flagship's three gates read awareness, which no
  clock or time-driven system writes (only effects do, and none runs inside one step), so they are
  cut-invariant. A `when:` event with no probability keeps today's fire-once meaning and today's
  code path, byte for byte (the per-day activity above applies to the probabilistic form only).
- `rumor_from` draws `pick_rumor(state, rng, source_npc=...)` on the event's own stream, after the
  probability draw; the rumour lands on `state.rumors` as today. **The rumour's tier is read at the
  step's end** (the phase as the step ends), not per day: the phase moves with `evil_progress`
  inside the step, and one step crossing a phase boundary may carry a rumour from the later tier.
  So the event days are cut-invariant and the rumour text is cut-invariant except across a phase
  change inside one step; the acceptance says exactly that.
- **Force hook.** `WorldSim.on_tick(force=[...])` and `advance_world_day(force_events=...)` (and
  the `advance_world_tick(force_event=)` system skill over it, `engine/skills/builtin/mechanics.py:254-265`)
  keep their meaning: a forced id that names a legacy slot the story configures runs the legacy
  roll as today; otherwise, a forced id that names a declared event starts that event today with no
  draw (its rumour, if any, is drawn on its stream). So `tests/test_world_sim.py`,
  `tests/test_npc_sim.py`, `tests/test_rumors.py` and `tests/test_law_propagation.py`, which force
  `caravan_arrival`, `tinker_camp` and `militia_press` against the flagship, keep passing with the
  same ids (T1 lists and re-runs them).
- `goods:` (read by nothing today; `engine/world/schedules.py` writes it into a payload no code
  reads) is not carried over. Dropped, not wired: the caravan's stock is Odran's vendor table.

**Rates (rule 10).** The conversion must keep the rates the background tick actually yielded, not
the numbers in `schedules.yaml`. Today each legacy roll uses its per-day probability once per
background tick (`schedules.py:360-361, 442-443`), and the tinker's per-week probability scaled by
the tick's own hours (`days_elapsed = hours / 24`, `world_sim.py:243`). In the harness one 2-hour
tick runs per turn (`scripts/simulate.py`, `world.tick_hours: 2.0`), so with DESIGN.md
§ EvilTicker's measured hours per turn (seed 42, 200 turns) the effective per-day rates are:

| Policy | h/turn (measured) | Ticks per day | Caravan (0.25/tick) | Press (0.03/tick, awareness ≥ 20) | Tinker (0.05/week × tick hours) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| hero | 3.00 | 8.00 | 0.900 | 0.216 | 0.0048 |
| cautious | 3.21 | 7.48 | 0.884 | 0.204 | 0.0045 |
| baker | 4.16 | 5.77 | 0.810 | 0.161 | 0.0034 |
| pauper | 3.56 | 6.74 | 0.856 | 0.186 | 0.0040 |
| reckless | 5.62 | 4.27 | 0.707 | 0.122 | 0.0025 |
| **mean** | | | **0.83** | **0.18** | **0.0038** |

These are DERIVED (1 − (1 − p)^k for k ticks a day; the tinker 0.05/7 × 2/h), from measured hours
per turn, not measured event counts: no design task may run engine code. **T1 measures the
pre-change rates first**, before converting anything: `simulate.py --policy all --turns 200`, 40
seeds, counting each event's starts per eligible in-game day per policy, and writes that table
into the CHANGELOG and `schedules.yaml`'s header. The per-day probabilities are then set to the
measured mean across the five policies (the derived means above are the placeholders T1
replaces). One per-day number cannot match five policies whose rates differed only because their
turns cut the day differently: that spread (0.71-0.90 for the caravan) collapses, which is the
point of the conversion, and is recorded as the measured change it is, with
`probability_per_day` as its lever. The first draft's 0.25, 0.03 and 0.0073 were the per-tick
numbers read as per-day and would have made the press about five times rarer; they are withdrawn.

The flagship declares `caravan_arrival` (from day 5), `tinker_camp` and `militia_press`
(`when: {min_awareness: 20}`), each with `text:` and the rates T1 measures, and removes its three
legacy top-level blocks. It also declares the stopped wheel's door:

```yaml
  the_last_hour:
    when: { value: { name: evil_progress, min: 1.0 } }   # fire-once, deterministic
    duration_days: 60
    forces_scene: the_stopped_wheel
    text: "Nothing in Edgewood keeps time any more except the thing that took it."
```

`the_last_hour` is the existing fire-once form, untouched. Inside one multi-day step it is read
against the step's end, so its recorded day is a day of that step, not necessarily the day the
reading crossed 1.0; nothing reads that day but its 60-day expiry and the forced scene, which is
dealt on the next turn either way. (A pre-existing quirk of the fire-once path, found here and not

### 6.3 E3 — The legacy terminal death may lock an ending

`death.yaml`'s flagship-shaped terminal (`phases` + `flag`: the first death in a listed phase sets
the flag, the next ends the run) gains an optional `ending:`. **The two shapes are told apart by
`"when" in terminal`**: with `when`, it is the `{when, ending}` terminal (HUE & CRY's) and is read
exactly as today; without `when`, it is the legacy shape, and `ending` (if present) is the ending
it locks when it ends the run. Today `check_death` takes the `{when, ending}` branch whenever
`terminal.ending` is set, and `evaluate_condition(None)` is True (`encounter.py:1141-1149`,
`quests.py:1007-1008`), so an `ending:` added beside `phases`/`flag` without this change would make
every first death terminal; and `death_terminal_problem` refuses `ending` without `when`
(`encounter.py:314-321`), so it would not load. Both change to branch on `"when" in terminal`.

When the legacy terminal ends the run and `ending` names a declared ending, the run locks it
through the same guarded path the `{when, ending}` terminal uses
(`encounter._terminal_ending_death`, the `terminal: true` lock under `_terminal_lock_guard`) and
plays its module, so `epilogue.for_state` shows its card on that turn. `death_terminal_problem`
accepts `ending` beside `phases`/`flag` (it still refuses mixing `when` with `phases`/`flag`) and
checks the id. The flagship declares `ending: the_dark_keeps_its_hours`, closing DESIGN.md's "that
terminal locks no ending, so it shows no epilogue". A story whose legacy terminal declares no
`ending` behaves exactly as today, and HUE & CRY's `{when, ending}` terminal reads exactly as
today (both asserted, §12.3).

## §7 — The overlay-to-intent fix (rule 1)

Closes the GOVERNANCE row "An overlay's action as an intent" for the flagship's four overlays.

### 7.1 The protocol

- The client sends `overlay_action {session_id, intent: {action, target}, label}` over the socket
  (or `POST` the turn route with `overlay_intent` in the body; one resolver serves both).
- `default_state.resolve_overlay_intent(session, raw)` returns `(intent, sentence, refusal)`:
  refuses an `action` not in `OVERLAY_ACTIONS`; refuses a pair not in `overlay_targets(state)`
  (uncapped, read under no lock: a cheap pre-check); otherwise `sentence =
  describe_overlay_intent(state, intent)` (the label `overlay_targets` gives the pair, never a raw
  id; `describe_intent` cannot serve, because it reads `legal_intents` and returns `""` for
  `use`/`equip`/`unequip` and for any target past the grammar's eighth) and the turn runs through
  `run_guarded(session, sentence, intent, overlay=True)` exactly like a chosen chip — the hosted
  queue, the turn lock, the autosave.
- **Under the turn lock the dispatcher checks it again, against the overlay's list.**
  `run_turn(..., overlay=True)` calls `execute_intent(intent, engine, overlay=True)`. Today
  `execute_intent` re-checks every intent against `legal_intents(engine.state)`
  (`engine/agents/tool_dispatcher.py:150-171`), which never holds `use`, `equip` or `unequip` and
  caps `buy`, `sell`, `eat`, `work` and `craft` at eight targets (`intents.py:80`), so without the
  mark every overlay-only press and every ninth target would be refused. With the mark it checks,
  in order:
  1. **A scene owns the turn** (`intents.scene_owns_turn(state)`: a dealt card, an open
     encounter, a running set-piece, an open job): refused with the engine's words, "That will
     have to wait: what is in front of you comes first." The refusal is the turn's receipt, so it
     reaches the prose (rule 1), and the scene is left exactly as it was.
  2. **The pair is not in `overlay_targets(state)`** (the clock moved, a coin spent elsewhere):
     refused with the skill's own reason where one exists (`trade.refusal_to_buy`,
     `_craft_refusal`), else "That is not on offer here and now."; the refusal reaches the prose.
  3. Otherwise `to_tool_call` and the skill, exactly as for a chip.
  Without the mark nothing changes: every chip, typed line and other caller passes `overlay=False`
  by default and is checked against `legal_intents` as today.
- **A refusal before the turn** (the pre-check: an unknown action, or a pair not in the overlay's
  list) answers `turn_error {message, busy: false, stale_choice: true}`: nothing ran, the save is
  unchanged, the overlay stays open and refetches its legal list. The pre-check does NOT refuse on
  a scene owning the turn: that refusal belongs in the prose, so it is left to the lock. (The
  client never sends one in the normal case: `/api/intents/overlay` reports `scene_owns_turn` and
  the overlay draws nothing pressable while it is true, §8.1. A press that races a card being dealt
  reaches the lock and is refused there, in words.)
- Typed text still carries no intent. `resolve_player_intent` is unchanged.

### 7.2 The verbs
